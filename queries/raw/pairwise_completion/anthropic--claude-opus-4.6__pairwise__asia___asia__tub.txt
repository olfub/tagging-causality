

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

**Step 1: Understand the entities**
- **Asia** is a geographic continent/region.
- **Tuberculosis (TB)** is an infectious disease caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*.

**Step 2: Evaluate Option A – "Asia causes Tuberculosis"**
While a continent doesn't literally *cause* a disease, this can be interpreted epidemiologically: conditions prevalent in parts of Asia — such as high population density, overcrowding, limited healthcare access, poverty, and certain environmental factors — contribute to higher rates of TB transmission and incidence. Asia indeed bears a disproportionately high burden of TB globally. In this sense, being in/from Asia is a **risk factor** for TB.

**Step 3: Evaluate Option B – "Tuberculosis causes Asia"**
A disease cannot cause a continent to come into existence. This direction is nonsensical — there is no logical mechanism by which TB would create or give rise to a geographic region.

**Step 4: Conclusion**
The only plausible causal direction is that conditions associated with Asia (geographic, socioeconomic, demographic) contribute to the prevalence of Tuberculosis.

<Answer>A</Answer>
