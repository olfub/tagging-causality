1. **Interpret the variables**
   - **Hepatotoxic**: indicates liver injury/toxicity (e.g., drug-induced liver injury, toxin exposure, hepatitis causing hepatic damage).
   - **Upper_pain**: typically refers to **upper abdominal pain**, often right upper quadrant/epigastric discomfort.

2. **Check biological plausibility**
   - Liver injury/inflammation can cause **capsular stretching**, biliary involvement, or hepatic congestion, which commonly manifests as **right upper quadrant or epigastric pain**.
   - So **hepatotoxicity → upper abdominal pain** is a plausible and clinically observed pathway.

3. **Assess the reverse direction**
   - **Upper abdominal pain** is a **symptom**, not a mechanistic driver of hepatocellular injury.
   - Pain itself does not generally cause hepatotoxicity; at most, it could lead to **analgesic use** (e.g., acetaminophen), which could then cause hepatotoxicity—but that would be an *indirect* pathway with additional variables (medication exposure), not a direct “upper_pain → hepatotoxic” relationship.

4. **Most likely direct causal relationship**
   - The direct and medically standard direction is **hepatotoxic causes upper_pain**.
