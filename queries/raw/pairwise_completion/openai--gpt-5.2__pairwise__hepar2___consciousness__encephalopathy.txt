1. **Define the terms**
   - **Consciousness**: the state/level of awareness and responsiveness (often assessed as alertness, arousal, orientation).
   - **Encephalopathy**: a diffuse dysfunction of the brain (metabolic, toxic, infectious, hypoxic, etc.) that commonly manifests as **altered mental status**.

2. **Check typical clinical directionality**
   - In practice, encephalopathy is a **pathophysiologic process** that **impairs** brain function.
   - A key clinical manifestation of encephalopathy is **altered level of consciousness** (confusion, somnolence, stupor, coma).

3. **Assess plausibility of the reverse direction**
   - “Consciousness causes encephalopathy” is not biologically coherent as a general causal claim: consciousness is an emergent functional state, not a primary etiologic driver of diffuse brain dysfunction.
   - While certain mental states/behaviors could indirectly contribute to risk (e.g., substance use), that is not “consciousness” causing encephalopathy; it’s specific exposures/illnesses causing encephalopathy.

4. **Conclusion**
   - The medically plausible causal relationship is that **encephalopathy leads to changes in consciousness**.
