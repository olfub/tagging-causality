# Analyzing Cause-and-Effect Relationship Between Diseases and X-rays

Let me analyze both options step by step to determine which causal relationship is more plausible.

## Understanding the components:
- **Tuberculosis**: An infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis bacteria, primarily affecting the lungs
- **Lung Cancer**: A malignant tumor in lung tissue, often caused by smoking, environmental factors, etc.
- **X-ray**: A diagnostic imaging technique using electromagnetic radiation

## Evaluating Option A: "Either Tuberculosis or Lung Cancer causes X-ray"

This option suggests that having tuberculosis or lung cancer leads to getting an X-ray examination. This aligns with medical practice because:
- Both diseases create visible changes in lung tissue
- X-rays are a standard diagnostic tool to detect these conditions
- When patients show symptoms of either condition, doctors typically order chest X-rays as part of diagnosis

## Evaluating Option B: "X-ray causes Either Tuberculosis or Lung Cancer"

This option suggests that X-ray exposure leads to developing tuberculosis or lung cancer. Let's examine this:
- Tuberculosis is an infectious disease caused by bacteria, not by X-ray exposure
- While ionizing radiation (including X-rays) can increase cancer risk, diagnostic X-rays use minimal radiation
- The radiation dose from diagnostic X-rays is too low to be a significant cause of lung cancer

## Conclusion:
Option A describes the standard clinical pathway - diseases cause physical changes that are then diagnosed using X-rays. Option B contradicts medical knowledge about how these diseases develop.

<Answer>A</Answer>
