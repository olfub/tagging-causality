

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship: MvmtFeatures vs. Scenario

## Step-by-step reasoning:

1. **Understanding the variables in hail forecasting context:**
   - **Scenario** refers to the overall meteorological/weather scenario — the broader atmospheric setup (e.g., synoptic patterns, frontal positions, instability regimes, wind environments).
   - **MvmtFeatures** refers to movement features — observable characteristics of storm motion, propagation, and kinematic behavior.

2. **Causal direction reasoning:**
   - The overarching weather **Scenario** (atmospheric setup, pressure patterns, jet stream position, wind profiles) dictates how storms form, organize, and **move**.
   - Movement features (storm speed, direction, propagation mode) are *consequences* of the meteorological environment defined by the scenario.
   - For example, a strongly sheared environment (part of the scenario) causes storms to move and propagate in specific ways (movement features).

3. **Ruling out the reverse:**
   - While movement features can be used *diagnostically* to classify or identify a scenario, this is an inferential/observational relationship, **not a causal one**.
   - Movement features don't *cause* the scenario to exist — they are manifestations of it.

## Conclusion:
The broader meteorological scenario is the upstream cause that drives the specific movement characteristics observed in storms.
