1. **Interpret the variables**
   - **albumin**: Serum albumin level, largely determined by **hepatic synthetic function**, nutrition, inflammation, and protein loss states.
   - **hcv_anti**: Anti–hepatitis C virus antibody (a marker of **current or past HCV infection/exposure**).

2. **Consider plausible biological mechanisms**
   - It is biologically plausible that **HCV infection** (which leads to **hcv_anti positivity**) can, over time, cause **chronic hepatitis/cirrhosis**, reducing hepatic protein synthesis and thereby **lowering albumin**.
   - The reverse—**albumin level causing anti-HCV antibodies**—doesn’t fit physiology. Albumin does not induce specific antiviral antibody production.

3. **Directionality and temporality**
   - Exposure/infection with HCV occurs first → immune response develops anti-HCV antibodies (**hcv_anti positive**) → potential progression to chronic liver disease → possible **decrease in albumin**.
   - Even though **hcv_anti** is a *marker* rather than the pathogenic agent itself, in causal modeling it often stands in for “HCV infection/exposure,” which can plausibly influence albumin.

4. **Confounding note**
   - A common cause (true HCV infection) can generate both **hcv_anti positivity** and **low albumin**, but between the two proposed directions, the marker-of-infection → liver function effect is far more plausible than albumin → antibody.
