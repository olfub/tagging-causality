

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

**Step 1: Understand the terms**
- **Cancer** (in this context, lung cancer) is a disease involving uncontrolled cell growth in the lungs.
- **Dyspnoea** is the medical term for difficulty breathing/shortness of breath — it is a **symptom**.

**Step 2: Evaluate Option A (Cancer → Dyspnoea)**
Lung cancer is a well-established cause of dyspnoea through multiple mechanisms:
- Tumor obstructing airways
- Pleural effusions compressing lung tissue
- Lymphangitic spread reducing gas exchange
- Loss of functional lung parenchyma

This is a classic and universally recognized clinical relationship.

**Step 3: Evaluate Option B (Dyspnoea → Cancer)**
Dyspnoea is a symptom, not a pathological process. It does not trigger malignant transformation. While dyspnoea may co-occur with conditions (e.g., COPD) that share risk factors with cancer (e.g., smoking), the breathlessness itself does not cause cancer. Correlation through shared risk factors ≠ causation.

**Step 4: Conclusion**
The direction of causation is clearly from cancer to dyspnoea. Cancer is the disease; dyspnoea is one of its downstream effects (symptoms).
