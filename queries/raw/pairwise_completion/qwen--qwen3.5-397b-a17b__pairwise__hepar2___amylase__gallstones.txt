## Step 1: Understanding Amylase
Amylase is a digestive enzyme produced primarily by:
- The pancreas
- Salivary glands

It helps break down carbohydrates. Elevated amylase levels in blood are typically a **marker** of pancreatic issues, not a causative agent itself.

## Step 2: Understanding Gallstones
Gallstones are hardened deposits that form in the gallbladder from:
- Cholesterol
- Bilirubin
- Other bile components

They can block bile ducts and pancreatic ducts.

## Step 3: Analyzing Direction A (Amylase → Gallstones)
- Amylase is an enzyme that aids digestion
- There is **no known biological mechanism** by which amylase would cause stone formation
- Amylase doesn't accumulate or crystallize to form stones
- This relationship is **not supported** by medical evidence

## Step 4: Analyzing Direction B (Gallstones → Amylase)
- Gallstones can block the common bile duct or pancreatic duct
- This obstruction can lead to **acute pancreatitis**
- Pancreatitis causes pancreatic cells to release amylase into the bloodstream
- This results in **elevated serum amylase levels**
- Gallstone pancreatitis is one of the **most common causes** of acute pancreatitis
- This relationship is **well-documented** in medical literature

## Step 5: Conclusion
The medically established causal pathway is:
**Gallstones → Duct Obstruction → Pancreatitis → Elevated Amylase Levels**
