1) **Clarify the variables**
- **Age**: time since birth; progresses deterministically and cannot be changed by social or economic conditions.
- **SocioEcon (socioeconomic status)**: income/education/occupation/wealth; can change over a person’s life and differs across people.

2) **Check temporal/physical feasibility**
- A cause must be able to vary the effect.
- **SocioEcon cannot cause Age**: regardless of wealth or education, a person’s chronological age does not change. (At most, SocioEcon can affect *health/aging outcomes* or *life expectancy*, but not actual Age as typically measured in insurance datasets.)
- **Age can cause SocioEcon**: as people age, they tend to move through schooling, career stages, asset accumulation, retirement, etc., which systematically affects income and wealth distributions.

3) **Interpretation in car insurance risk modeling**
- Age is often treated as an upstream demographic driver that influences many downstream factors (employment status, income level, vehicle choice, mileage, etc.).
- SocioEcon is plausibly downstream of age (with additional drivers like family background), but it is not a driver of age.

4) **Conclusion**
The more plausible causal direction is **Age → SocioEcon**.
