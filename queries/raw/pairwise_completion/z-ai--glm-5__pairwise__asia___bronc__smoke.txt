# Determining the Causal Relationship Between Smoking and Bronchitis

To determine which cause-and-effect relationship is more likely, I need to analyze both possibilities using medical knowledge and causal reasoning principles.

## Step 1: Understanding the conditions
- **Bronchitis**: An inflammation of the bronchial tubes that carry air to and from the lungs
- **Smoking**: The act of inhaling tobacco smoke, which contains numerous harmful chemicals

## Step 2: Analyzing Option A: "Bronchitis causes Smoking"
This would imply that having bronchitis leads someone to start smoking. There is no known biological mechanism or medical evidence suggesting that bronchitis would cause someone to begin smoking. In fact, people with bronchitis typically experience breathing difficulties and are advised to avoid smoke.

## Step 3: Analyzing Option B: "Smoking causes Bronchitis"
Medical research has established that:
- Smoking damages the airways and lungs
- Cigarette smoke irritates bronchial tubes and damages cilia (hair-like structures that clean the airways)
- This damage increases mucus production and makes the airways more susceptible to infection
- Long-term smoking is a primary risk factor for chronic bronchitis
- Many studies show higher rates of bronchitis among smokers compared to non-smokers

## Step 4: Temporal relationship
In causal relationships, the cause must precede the effect. Typically, people begin smoking before developing bronchitis, not after. This temporal sequence supports Option B.

## Conclusion
Based on medical evidence, biological plausibility, and the temporal relationship between these variables, the correct causal relationship is that smoking causes bronchitis, not the reverse.

<Answer>B</Answer>
