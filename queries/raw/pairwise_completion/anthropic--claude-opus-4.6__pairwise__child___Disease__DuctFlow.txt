

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

### Step 1: Understanding DuctFlow
**DuctFlow** refers to the pattern of blood flow through the **ductus arteriosus** — a fetal vascular connection between the aorta and pulmonary artery. After birth, the direction and presence of this flow (left-to-right, right-to-left, or absent) is clinically significant in congenital heart disease.

### Step 2: Considering Direction A (Disease → DuctFlow)
The underlying congenital heart **disease** determines the hemodynamic environment, which in turn dictates the ductal flow pattern:
- **Right-sided obstructive lesions** (e.g., pulmonary atresia, critical pulmonary stenosis) → right-to-left ductal flow (duct supplies pulmonary blood flow)
- **Left-sided obstructive lesions** (e.g., hypoplastic left heart syndrome, critical coarctation) → right-to-left ductal flow (duct supplies systemic blood flow)
- **Normal/mild disease** → left-to-right or no ductal flow (duct closes normally)

The disease creates the pressure gradients that determine ductal flow direction.

### Step 3: Considering Direction B (DuctFlow → Disease)
Ductal flow is a **hemodynamic observation/consequence** — it does not create the structural heart defect. You cannot reason that a particular flow pattern through the ductus *causes* a congenital malformation.

### Step 4: Bayesian Network Context
In well-known diagnostic Bayesian networks for congenital heart disease (e.g., the ALARM network), **Disease** is modeled as a parent node that causally influences observable findings like DuctFlow, consistent with the medical reasoning above.

## Conclusion
The congenital heart disease determines the ductal flow pattern, not vice versa. DuctFlow is an observable **effect** of the underlying disease.
