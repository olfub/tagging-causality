# Analyzing the Cause-and-Effect Relationship Between Cancer and X-ray

To determine which cause-and-effect relationship is more likely, I'll analyze both possibilities:

## Option A: Cancer causes X-ray
This means that having cancer leads to getting an X-ray examination.

Evidence supporting this relationship:
- X-rays are a common diagnostic tool used to detect and monitor cancer
- When cancer is suspected based on symptoms, doctors routinely order X-rays
- Patients with known cancer often receive regular X-rays to monitor disease progression or treatment effectiveness
- This represents standard medical practice and occurs millions of times daily worldwide

## Option B: X-ray causes Cancer
This implies that exposure to X-ray radiation leads to the development of cancer.

Evidence supporting this relationship:
- X-rays are a form of ionizing radiation, which is a known carcinogen
- High doses of ionizing radiation can damage DNA and potentially cause cancer
- However, modern diagnostic X-rays use minimal radiation doses
- The probability of developing cancer from a diagnostic X-ray is extremely low
- This effect, while theoretically possible, is relatively rare compared to the diagnostic application

## Comparison
The relationship in Option A (Cancer causes X-ray) represents a direct, intentional, and frequent medical practice. When cancer is suspected or confirmed, X-rays are routinely performed for diagnosis and monitoring.

While Option B has scientific validity (ionizing radiation can contribute to cancer development), the probability is much lower, and this represents a rare potential side effect rather than a common outcome.

Therefore, the predominant cause-and-effect relationship between these two variables is more likely to be that cancer leads to X-ray examinations.

<Answer>A</Answer>
