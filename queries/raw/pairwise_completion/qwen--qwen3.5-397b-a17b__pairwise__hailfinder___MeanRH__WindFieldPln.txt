## Step-by-Step Analysis

### Step 1: Understanding the Variables

**MeanRH (Mean Relative Humidity):**
- A thermodynamic property measuring water vapor content in the air
- Indicates moisture availability in the atmosphere
- Important for storm development and hail formation

**WindFieldPln (Planar Wind Field):**
- A dynamic/kinematic property describing wind speed and direction patterns
- Driven primarily by pressure gradients, temperature differences, and Coriolis force
- Critical for storm organization and severity

### Step 2: Examining Physical Mechanisms

**Can Humidity Cause Wind? (A)**
- Humidity has a minor effect on air density (moist air is slightly less dense)
- This can influence buoyancy and convection indirectly
- However, this is a **secondary effect** - not a primary driver of wind patterns
- Wind fields are mainly determined by pressure gradients and large-scale dynamics

**Can Wind Cause Humidity Changes? (B)**
- Wind **transports air masses** with different moisture content (moisture advection)
- Wind patterns determine where moist or dry air moves
- Convergence/divergence in wind fields affects vertical motion, influencing condensation
- This is a **well-established, primary meteorological process**

### Step 3: Context of Hail Forecasting

In severe weather forecasting:
- Wind shear and wind patterns organize storms and affect their intensity
- These wind patterns redistribute moisture throughout the atmosphere
- Humidity is more of a **state variable** that responds to transport by winds
- Both are influenced by larger-scale atmospheric conditions, but wind→humidity advection is the stronger directional relationship

### Step 4: Conclusion

From atmospheric physics, **wind fields transporting moisture (advection)** is a much stronger and more direct causal mechanism than humidity affecting wind patterns. While both variables are important predictors for hail, the physical causality more strongly supports wind affecting humidity distribution.

<Answer>B</Answer>
