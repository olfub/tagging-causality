1. **Interpret the terms**
   - **hepatotoxic**: refers to a toxic effect on the liver (often drug-/chemical-induced liver injury).
   - **RHepatitis**: most plausibly refers to a form of **hepatitis** (liver inflammation), e.g., “recurrent hepatitis” or a coded/abbreviated hepatitis diagnosis.

2. **Directionality in clinical causation**
   - **Hepatotoxicity → hepatitis** is a common and well-recognized pathway: toxic injury to hepatocytes can trigger inflammation and present clinically/histologically as **toxic hepatitis** (a subtype of drug-induced liver injury).
   - The reverse (**hepatitis → hepatotoxicity**) is less coherent: hepatitis is an inflammatory condition; it doesn’t typically “cause” something to be hepatotoxic. Rather, a *substance* is hepatotoxic, or hepatitis can *increase susceptibility* to toxicity from certain drugs, but that’s not the direct causal framing given.

3. **Most likely cause-effect relationship**
   - **hepatotoxic causes RHepatitis** is the more medically consistent causal direction.
