

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship Between Fat and Gallstones

## Step-by-step reasoning:

**Step 1: Understanding gallstone formation**
Gallstones form when substances in bile (especially cholesterol) become supersaturated and crystallize in the gallbladder.

**Step 2: How fat could cause gallstones (A)**
- Excess body fat (obesity) increases hepatic cholesterol synthesis and secretion into bile.
- This leads to cholesterol supersaturation of bile, a key step in cholesterol gallstone formation.
- Obesity is one of the most well-established risk factors for gallstones — this is a widely supported epidemiological and mechanistic finding.
- High dietary fat intake can also alter bile composition.

**Step 3: How gallstones could cause fat (B)**
- Gallstones can impair bile flow, which actually *reduces* fat digestion and absorption (since bile emulsifies dietary fats).
- There is no known mechanism by which gallstones would increase body fat. If anything, impaired bile flow would reduce fat absorption.

**Step 4: Conclusion**
The well-established medical evidence points to excess body fat (obesity) as a cause of gallstone formation, not the other way around. The mechanism is clear: more fat → more cholesterol in bile → supersaturation → gallstone formation.

<Answer>A</Answer>
