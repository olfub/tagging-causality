

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

### Understanding the Variables
- **SocioEcon**: Socioeconomic status of the household (income, education level, occupation, wealth).
- **GoodStudent**: Whether the insured (typically a young driver) is a good student academically.

### Evaluating Option A: GoodStudent → SocioEcon
- This would mean a student's grades **cause** the household's socioeconomic status.
- While long-term educational achievement can *eventually* influence one's own future socioeconomic standing, in the insurance context, GoodStudent refers to a young driver's *current* academic performance, while SocioEcon reflects the *household's current* economic standing.
- A teenager's grades don't determine their family's income or social class.

### Evaluating Option B: SocioEcon → GoodStudent
- Higher socioeconomic status provides: better schools, tutoring resources, more stable home environments, parental education support, and less financial stress — all of which **promote** better academic performance.
- Lower socioeconomic status can lead to fewer educational resources, potentially requiring part-time work, reducing study time, etc.
- This direction is well-supported by sociological and educational research and is consistent with standard Bayesian network models used in car insurance (e.g., the classic "Insurance" BN), where SocioEcon is a **parent node** of GoodStudent.

### Conclusion
SocioEcon is the upstream, more fundamental variable that influences whether a young driver is a good student — not the reverse.

<Answer>B</Answer>
