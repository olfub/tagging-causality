## Step-by-Step Analysis

### Step 1: Understanding the Medical Terms
- **Cirrhosis**: A chronic liver disease characterized by scarring of liver tissue and impaired liver function
- **Palms** (in this context): Refers to "palmar erythema" - redness of the palms, which is a clinical sign

### Step 2: Examining the Pathophysiology
Cirrhosis leads to several physiological changes:
- Impaired liver metabolism of hormones (particularly estrogen)
- Altered blood circulation
- Vasodilation of blood vessels

These changes can manifest as **palmar erythema** (red palms), which is a well-documented clinical sign of liver disease.

### Step 3: Evaluating Causal Direction
- **A. Cirrhosis → Palms**: The liver disease causes the physical sign (red palms) through hormonal and vascular changes
- **B. Palms → Cirrhosis**: Having red palms cannot cause liver scarring and dysfunction - this has no physiological basis

### Step 4: Medical Consensus
In medical literature, palmar erythema is classified as a **sign/symptom** of cirrhosis, not a cause. The disease process flows from liver damage to clinical manifestations.

### Conclusion
The causal relationship flows from the disease (cirrhosis) to the clinical sign (palmar erythema), not vice versa.
