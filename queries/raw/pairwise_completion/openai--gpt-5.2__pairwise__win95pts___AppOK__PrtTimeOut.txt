1. **Interpret the variables in a Windows 95 printing context**
   - **PrtTimeOut**: a printer/port/spooler condition where Windows waits for the printer (or connection) and eventually times out.
   - **AppOK**: the application’s status (responsive/working vs. hung/erroring) while attempting to print.

2. **Ask which one can realistically produce the other**
   - A printer timeout is typically caused by **printer-side or communication/spooler issues** (offline printer, bad cable/port, wrong driver, LPT handshake problems, network printer unreachable, spooler problems).
   - An application being “OK” (running normally) **does not create** a printer timeout; at most it *triggers a print attempt*, but the timeout arises from the printer path not responding.

3. **Consider the more plausible causal direction**
   - If **PrtTimeOut** occurs, it can lead to **observable effects in the app**: the app may hang while printing, show an error, or remain responsive depending on how it handles the print call. In any case, the app’s observed “OK/not OK” status is downstream of the timeout event.
   - The reverse (“AppOK causes PrtTimeOut”) is not a typical causal mechanism.
