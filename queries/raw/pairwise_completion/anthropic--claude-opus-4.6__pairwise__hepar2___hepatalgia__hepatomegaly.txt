

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship Between Hepatalgia and Hepatomegaly

## Step-by-Step Reasoning:

**Definitions:**
- **Hepatalgia** = liver pain (pain in the hepatic region)
- **Hepatomegaly** = enlargement of the liver

**Key Anatomical Fact:**
The liver parenchyma itself lacks pain receptors. Pain sensation in the liver comes primarily from **Glisson's capsule** (the fibrous covering of the liver), which is richly innervated with sensory nerve fibers.

**Evaluating Option A (hepatalgia → hepatomegaly):**
Pain is a subjective symptom/sensation. Pain alone does not cause physical organ enlargement. There is no known physiological mechanism by which liver pain would lead to liver enlargement.

**Evaluating Option B (hepatomegaly → hepatalgia):**
When the liver enlarges, it **stretches Glisson's capsule**. This mechanical stretching activates pain receptors (nociceptors) in the capsule, producing hepatalgia. This is a well-established clinical mechanism — patients with hepatomegaly from conditions like congestive heart failure, hepatitis, or hepatic tumors commonly experience right upper quadrant pain due to capsular distension.

## Conclusion:
The enlargement of the liver (hepatomegaly) causes stretching of the liver capsule, which produces liver pain (hepatalgia). This is a well-recognized pathophysiological mechanism.

<Answer>B</Answer>
