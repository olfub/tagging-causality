This is a task involving medical causal reasoning. Let's break down the relationship between Steatosis and alcoholism step by step.

**Step 1: Define the terms.**
*   **Alcoholism (Alcohol Use Disorder):** This is a chronic disease characterized by uncontrolled drinking and preoccupation with alcohol. It involves the ingestion of substantial amounts of ethanol over time.
*   **Steatosis:** This refers to the abnormal retention of fat (lipids) within a cell or organ. In this context, it almost certainly refers to *hepatic steatosis*, commonly known as "fatty liver."

**Step 2: Analyze Relationship A (Steatosis causes alcoholism).**
*   *Hypothesis:* Having a fatty liver drives a person to develop an addiction to alcohol.
*   *Medical Evidence:* There is no established physiological or psychological mechanism suggesting that the accumulation of fat in liver cells compels a person to consume excessive alcohol. While a patient diagnosed with fatty liver might feel stress, which could theoretically lead to coping mechanisms involving substance use, this is an indirect psychological pathway, not a direct biological cause. Therefore, Steatosis is not considered a cause of alcoholism.

**Step 3: Analyze Relationship B (Alcoholism causes Steatosis).**
*   *Hypothesis:* Chronic, heavy consumption of alcohol leads to a fatty liver.
*   *Medical Evidence:* This is a well-established pathophysiological pathway. The metabolism of alcohol (ethanol) in the liver produces toxic byproducts like acetaldehyde and disrupts normal metabolic processes. Specifically:
    1.  It increases the ratio of NADH/NAD+, which inhibits fatty acid oxidation (breakdown of fats).
    2.  It promotes lipogenesis (creation of fats).
    3.  It impairs the export of lipoproteins.
*   *Conclusion:* These factors lead to the accumulation of triglycerides within hepatocytes (liver cells). This condition is specifically known as Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (AFLD).

**Step 4: Compare the Probability.**
*   Relationship B describes a fundamental, textbook pathophysiology of liver disease.
*   Relationship A describes a relationship that is not biologically supported.

**Conclusion:**
Alcoholism is a primary cause of hepatic steatosis. Steatosis is a downstream effect (symptom/sequela) of the metabolic changes induced by heavy alcohol consumption.

<Answer>B</Answer>
