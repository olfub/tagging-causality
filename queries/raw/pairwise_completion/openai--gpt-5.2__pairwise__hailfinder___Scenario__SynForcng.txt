1. **Interpret the variables**
   - **SynForcng** (synoptic forcing) refers to large-scale atmospheric dynamics (e.g., troughs, jet streaks, frontal zones, ascent) that create an environment favorable for convection and severe weather, including hail.
   - **Scenario** typically refers to the resulting weather setup/regime or categorized forecast situation (e.g., “supercell scenario,” “QLCS scenario,” “high-shear/low-CAPE scenario”), or a modeled/forecast scenario describing what unfolds.

2. **Check physical direction of influence**
   - Synoptic-scale forcing helps determine *which* convective mode and severe-weather “scenario” is most likely by controlling ascent, shear profiles, storm initiation mechanisms, and storm organization.
   - A “scenario” (as a description/category of expected storm evolution) does not physically drive the synoptic-scale forcing; it’s an outcome/characterization of it.

3. **Causal plausibility**
   - It is meteorologically plausible that **SynForcng → Scenario**.
   - The reverse (**Scenario → SynForcng**) would imply the categorized event/setup causes the large-scale dynamics, which is not consistent with atmospheric causality.
