To determine the most likely cause-and-effect relationship between `MeanRH` (Mean Relative Humidity) and `WindFieldPln` (Wind Field Plane/Planar Wind Field) in the context of hail forecasting, let's analyze the physical atmospheric processes step by step.

**Step 1: Define the Variables**
*   **MeanRH (Mean Relative Humidity):** This represents the amount of moisture in the air relative to the maximum amount of moisture the air can hold at that temperature. It is a thermodynamic variable concerning moisture content.
*   **WindFieldPln (Wind Field Plane):** This refers to the speed and direction of air movement across a horizontal plane (advection, shear, convergence/divergence). It is a kinematic variable concerning motion.

**Step 2: Analyzer Relationship A (MeanRH causes WindFieldPln)**
*   Does humidity directly drive the wind field?
*   While humidity affects air density (moist air is less dense than dry air) and can influence buoyancy (virtual temperature), which eventually drives vertical motion (convection), it is rarely the *primary* driver of the large-scale or mesoscale horizontal wind field.
*   The large-scale wind field is driven primarily by pressure gradient forces (caused by temperature differences) and the Coriolis effect. Humidity is a secondary factor in terms of generating momentum.

**Step 3: Analyze Relationship B (WindFieldPln causes MeanRH)**
*   Does the wind field affect humidity?
*   **Moisture Advection:** Yes. The wind field transports air masses. If the wind blows from a moisture source (like an ocean or a lake) into a region, it directly increases the moisture content and thus the Relative Humidity of that region.
*   **Convergence/Divergence:** Wind fields determine where air converges (piles up) or diverges (spreads out). Convergence at the surface often forces air upward, leading to cooling and condensation, which alters the local RH profile.
*   **Mixing:** Turbulence and wind shear mix different layers of the atmosphere, redistributing moisture and changing the Mean RH of a specific column.

**Step 4: Conclusion**
In meteorological dynamics, the kinematic state of the atmosphere (the wind field) is the primary mechanism for transporting properties like heat and moisture. Therefore, the wind brings the moisture to a location (advection), rather than the moisture spontaneously generating the wind field.

It is physically more plausible that the movement of air (WindFieldPln) dictates the distribution and average values of humidity (MeanRH) in a specific location through transport and mixing.
