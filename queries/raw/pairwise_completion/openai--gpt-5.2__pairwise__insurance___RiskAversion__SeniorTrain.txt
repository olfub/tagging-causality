1) **Clarify what the variables represent (typical in auto insurance data):**  
- **RiskAversion**: a relatively stable latent trait (preference for safety, cautious driving, avoidance of risk).  
- **SeniorTrain**: whether a senior driver completed a senior driver training course (a discrete action/choice, often voluntary or sometimes incentivized).

2) **Consider the “decision to take training” mechanism:**  
Taking a training course usually requires **opting in** (time, effort, sometimes money). People who are more risk-averse are generally **more likely to seek safety-improving actions** (defensive driving courses, extra practice, refresher courses).

3) **Consider the reverse direction (training → risk aversion):**  
Training can increase knowledge, awareness, and may encourage safer behaviors, but shifting a deeper trait like “risk aversion” is typically **weaker and less direct** than trait → behavior selection. Training is more plausibly an intervention that affects **driving skill/behavior** (e.g., fewer risky maneuvers) rather than fundamentally changing a person’s underlying risk preference.

4) **Selection effects in insurance datasets:**  
In observational insurance data, a common pattern is **self-selection**: cautious (risk-averse) drivers disproportionately take optional courses. That makes **RiskAversion → SeniorTrain** the more likely causal direction.

5) **Conclusion:**  
The more likely cause-and-effect relationship is that **RiskAversion causes SeniorTrain** (risk-averse seniors choose training).
