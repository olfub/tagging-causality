## Step-by-Step Analysis

**Step 1: Define the terms**
- **Hepatotoxic**: Refers to liver toxicity or liver damage
- **Nausea**: A feeling of sickness with an inclination to vomit (a symptom)

**Step 2: Understand the medical relationship**
- Hepatotoxicity is a pathological condition affecting the liver
- Nausea is a clinical symptom that can result from various underlying conditions

**Step 3: Analyze the causal direction**
- When the liver is damaged or toxic, it impairs the body's ability to process toxins and metabolites
- This buildup of substances, along with liver inflammation, commonly triggers nausea as a symptom
- Many hepatotoxic drugs and conditions list nausea as a common presenting symptom

**Step 4: Consider reverse causation**
- Nausea is a symptom, not a disease process itself
- Feeling nauseous does not cause damage to liver tissue
- There is no known physiological mechanism where nausea would lead to hepatotoxicity

**Step 5: Clinical evidence**
- In medical practice, hepatotoxicity is well-documented as a cause of nausea
- The reverse relationship (nausea causing liver damage) is not supported by medical literature

## Conclusion

Hepatotoxicity (liver damage/toxicity) can cause nausea as one of its symptoms, but nausea does not cause hepatotoxicity.
