## Step-by-Step Analysis

### Step 1: Understanding the Markers

**HBsAg (Hepatitis B Surface Antigen)**: This is a protein found on the surface of the hepatitis B virus. Its presence indicates an active hepatitis B infection. It's produced by the virus itself.

**HBc_anti (Hepatitis B Core Antibody)**: This is an antibody produced by the patient's immune system in response to exposure to the hepatitis B core antigen.

### Step 2: Temporal Sequence in Hepatitis B Infection

1. **Initial infection** with hepatitis B virus occurs
2. **HBsAg appears first** (typically within 1-9 weeks after exposure) - this indicates active viral presence
3. **Immune system responds** to the viral infection
4. **HBc_anti develops** as part of the immune response (appears after HBsAg)

### Step 3: Evaluating the Causal Relationships

**Option A (hbc_anti causes hbsag)**: 
- Antibodies are produced by the host's immune system
- Antibodies cannot cause viral antigens to appear
- This contradicts the biological mechanism of infection

**Option B (hbsag causes hbc_anti)**:
- HBsAg indicates active viral presence/replication
- Viral presence exposes the immune system to viral antigens
- This triggers antibody production including HBc_anti
- This aligns with the natural course of infection and immune response

### Step 4: Conclusion

The presence of HBsAg (indicating active infection) precedes and triggers the immune response that produces HBc_anti. While technically both markers result from the underlying HBV infection, the directional relationship from viral antigen presence to antibody production is biologically accurate.

<Answer>B</Answer>
