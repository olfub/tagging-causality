1. **Define the entities**
   - **Cirrhosis**: end-stage chronic liver disease with fibrosis and nodular regeneration, causing impaired hepatic synthetic function and portal hypertension.
   - **Edema**: excess fluid in the interstitial space (often peripheral edema) and/or fluid accumulation in body cavities (e.g., ascites).

2. **Plausible mechanism: Cirrhosis → edema**
   Cirrhosis commonly leads to edema through several well-established pathways:
   - **Reduced albumin synthesis** → **hypoalbuminemia** → decreased plasma oncotic pressure → fluid shifts into interstitial tissues → **peripheral edema**.
   - **Portal hypertension** → increased hydrostatic pressure in the splanchnic circulation → fluid transudation → **ascites**.
   - **Splanchnic vasodilation and “effective arterial underfilling”** → activation of RAAS/SNS/ADH → **sodium and water retention** → worsened edema/ascites.

3. **Reverse direction: edema → cirrhosis**
   - Edema is typically a **symptom/sign** of underlying conditions (heart failure, nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis, venous insufficiency, etc.).
   - Edema itself does **not** cause the chronic hepatocellular injury and fibrogenesis required to produce cirrhosis. There are rare scenarios where systemic conditions (e.g., chronic right-sided heart failure) can contribute to hepatic congestion and “cardiac cirrhosis,” but the causal agent there is the cardiac disease, not edema per se.

4. **Conclusion**
   The more likely cause-and-effect relationship is **cirrhosis causing edema**.
