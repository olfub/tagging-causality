1. **Define the two variables**
   - **MidLLapse (mid-level lapse rates):** A thermodynamic property describing how quickly temperature decreases with height in the mid-troposphere (e.g., 700–500 mb). Steeper lapse rates imply greater potential instability and are often tied to air-mass characteristics (EML, cold air aloft, etc.).
   - **SynForcng (synoptic forcing):** Large-scale dynamical forcing for ascent/descent (e.g., troughs, jet streak divergence, vorticity advection, frontogenesis). This is fundamentally driven by the synoptic-scale pressure/wind/temperature fields and their evolution.

2. **Check physical plausibility of A: “MidLLapse causes SynForcng”**
   - Mid-level lapse rates can influence **convection intensity** and storm mode, but they do not *generate* synoptic-scale dynamics like troughs, jet streaks, or large-scale ascent patterns.
   - Synoptic forcing is not typically a downstream consequence of local/static lapse-rate steepness.  
   **So A is unlikely.**

3. **Check physical plausibility of B: “SynForcng causes MidLLapse”**
   - Synoptic forcing can be associated with processes that **modify lapse rates**, such as:
     - **Cold advection/cooling aloft** with an approaching trough (steepens mid-level lapse rates).
     - **Large-scale ascent** leading to cooling (can steepen lapse rates in certain layers, though moisture/latent heating can complicate this).
     - **Air-mass advection** (synoptic patterns transport an EML or colder mid-level air).
   - While mid-level lapse rates often originate from upstream source regions, the *synoptic pattern/forcing* is a plausible causal driver of their presence and evolution in a forecast area.  
   **So B is more plausible than A.**

<Answer>B</Answer>
