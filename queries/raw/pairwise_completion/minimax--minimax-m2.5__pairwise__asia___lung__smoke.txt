1. **Identify the variables** – “Smoking” is a behavior (exposure) and “Lung Cancer” is a disease (outcome).  
2. **Look at the temporal order** – People start smoking *before* they develop lung cancer; the disease appears later in life.  
3. **Evidence from epidemiology** – Numerous large‑scale studies consistently show that smokers have a much higher risk of developing lung cancer than non‑smokers.  
4. **Biological plausibility** – Tobacco smoke contains carcinogens that cause DNA damage in lung cells, leading to malignant transformation.  
5. **Consider reverse causality** – It is implausible that having lung cancer would cause a person to start smoking; lung cancer does not produce a behavior that leads to smoking initiation.
