1. **Define the concepts**
   - **INTUBATION**: placing an endotracheal tube to secure the airway.
   - **VENTLUNG**: the patient’s lungs are being mechanically ventilated (i.e., on a ventilator).

2. **Typical ICU sequence**
   - In most ICU workflows, **intubation is performed first** to establish a secure airway interface.
   - **Mechanical ventilation is then delivered through the endotracheal tube**.

3. **Mechanistic dependence**
   - **VENTLUNG (invasive mechanical ventilation)** generally *requires* an endotracheal tube (or tracheostomy) as the conduit.
   - Thus, **intubation is a direct enabling step** that makes VENTLUNG possible.

4. **Edge cases (don’t dominate the usual relationship)**
   - Ventilation can occur without intubation (e.g., NIV/BiPAP), but that’s typically not what “VENTLUNG” denotes in many ICU data contexts.
   - Intubation can occur briefly without sustained ventilation, but in ICU practice it commonly leads to ventilation.

**More likely cause → effect:** **INTUBATION → VENTLUNG**.
