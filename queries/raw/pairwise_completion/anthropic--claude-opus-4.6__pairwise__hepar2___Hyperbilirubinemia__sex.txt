

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship

## Step-by-step reasoning:

**Option A: Hyperbilirubinemia causes sex**
- Hyperbilirubinemia is a medical condition involving elevated bilirubin levels in the blood.
- Biological sex is determined at conception by chromosomal composition (XX or XY).
- A metabolic/liver condition cannot determine a person's biological sex.
- This direction of causation is **not plausible**.

**Option B: Sex causes Hyperbilirubinemia**
- Biological sex is a well-known risk factor for hyperbilirubinemia.
- **Male sex** is an established risk factor for neonatal jaundice/hyperbilirubinemia — male newborns have higher rates than females.
- Conditions like **Gilbert syndrome** (a common cause of unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia) are more prevalent in males.
- Hormonal differences between sexes can influence bilirubin metabolism (e.g., testosterone may inhibit bilirubin conjugation).
- Sex is a temporally prior, fixed biological variable that can influence disease risk.

## Conclusion:
Sex is a biological determinant that can influence the likelihood of developing hyperbilirubinemia, not the other way around.

<Answer>B</Answer>
