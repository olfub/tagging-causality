## Step-by-Step Reasoning

Let me analyze this causal relationship carefully:

**Step 1: Understand what each event represents**
- **Burglary**: An unauthorized entry into a property with intent to commit theft or other crime
- **Alarm**: A security device that sounds/alerts when triggered by sensors

**Step 2: Analyze Option A (Alarm causes Burglary)**
- This would mean the alarm sounding somehow causes someone to commit a burglary
- This doesn't make logical sense - alarms are deterrents, not instigators
- An alarm going off doesn't make a burglar decide to break in

**Step 3: Analyze Option B (Burglary causes Alarm)**
- This means when a burglary occurs, it triggers the alarm system
- This aligns with how security systems are designed to work
- Burglars breaking in → sensors detect intrusion → alarm sounds
- This is the intended function of burglar alarms

**Step 4: Consider real-world mechanics**
- Motion sensors, door/window contacts, and glass break detectors are designed to detect unauthorized entry
- When these sensors detect a break-in (burglary), they activate the alarm
- The causal chain flows from the criminal act to the detection system

**Step 5: Reference to causal reasoning literature**
- This is actually a classic example from Bayesian network textbooks (Pearl's causal diagrams)
- The standard model shows: Burglary → Alarm (not the reverse)

**Conclusion**: The burglary is the cause that triggers the alarm effect, not vice versa.

<Answer>B</Answer>
