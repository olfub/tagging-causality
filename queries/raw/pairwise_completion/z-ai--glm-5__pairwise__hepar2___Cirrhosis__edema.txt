Here is the step-by-step reasoning to determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship:

1.  **Define Cirrhosis**: Cirrhosis is a late-stage liver disease characterized by scarring (fibrosis) of the liver tissue. This scarring prevents the liver from functioning properly.

2.  **Define Edema**: Edema is swelling caused by excess fluid trapped in the body's tissues. In the context of liver disease, this often presents as peripheral edema (swelling in the legs and ankles) or ascites (fluid accumulation in the abdomen).

3.  **Analyze the Physiological Mechanism (Option A)**: When the liver is damaged by cirrhosis, it cannot produce enough albumin (a protein that keeps fluid in the bloodstream) and cannot effectively regulate fluid balance. This leads to low oncotic pressure and portal hypertension. Consequently, fluid leaks out of the blood vessels and into the surrounding tissues, causing edema. This is a well-documented medical complication of cirrhosis.

4.  **Analyze the Physiological Mechanism (Option B)**: Edema is a symptom or a clinical sign, not a primary disease agent. The presence of excess fluid in tissues does not cause the specific cellular damage and scarring that defines cirrhosis. While edema can be a symptom of many conditions (like heart failure or kidney disease), it does not cause liver cirrhosis.

5.  **Conclusion**: Cirrhosis disrupts the body's fluid regulation mechanisms, leading to the symptom of edema. Therefore, the causal direction flows from the disease (cirrhosis) to the symptom (edema).
