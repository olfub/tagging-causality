

# Analyzing the Cause-and-Effect Relationship

## Step-by-step reasoning:

1. **Understanding "palms" in medical context**: This likely refers to **palmar erythema** (reddened palms), which is a well-known clinical sign in hepatology.

2. **Cirrhosis → Palmar erythema**: Cirrhosis leads to impaired liver metabolism, which causes elevated estrogen levels. This hyperestrogenemia results in arteriolar vasodilation in the palms, producing the characteristic redness known as palmar erythema. This is a **well-established pathophysiological mechanism**.

3. **Palmar erythema → Cirrhosis**: Red palms are a **sign/symptom**, not a disease process. A symptom like palmar erythema cannot cause the structural liver damage (fibrosis and nodular regeneration) that defines cirrhosis. This direction is not medically plausible.

4. **Conclusion**: Cirrhosis is the underlying disease that produces palmar erythema as a clinical manifestation, not the other way around.
