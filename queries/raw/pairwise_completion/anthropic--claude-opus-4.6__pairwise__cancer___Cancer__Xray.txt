

# Analyzing the Cause-and-Effect Relationship

## Step-by-step reasoning:

1. **Interpret "Xray" in context**: In the lung cancer domain (especially in classic Bayesian networks like the ASIA model), "Xray" refers to a **positive/abnormal X-ray result**, not the act of taking an X-ray itself.

2. **Option A: Cancer → Xray**: Lung cancer causes abnormalities (e.g., masses, nodules) in the lung tissue, which then **show up as positive findings on an X-ray**. This is a well-established clinical causal pathway — the disease produces the observable diagnostic result.

3. **Option B: Xray → Cancer**: While ionizing radiation can theoretically increase cancer risk, the radiation dose from a standard chest X-ray is extremely low (~0.1 mSv) and is **not considered a meaningful cause of lung cancer**.

4. **Clinical context**: In medical reasoning, diagnostic results (like X-ray findings) are typically **effects** of underlying diseases, not causes. Cancer is a latent condition that **generates** observable evidence on imaging.

## Conclusion:
Cancer (the disease) causes a positive X-ray finding, not the other way around.
