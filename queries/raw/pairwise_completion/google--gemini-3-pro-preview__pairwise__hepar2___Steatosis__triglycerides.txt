To determine the most likely cause-and-effect relationship between **Steatosis** and **Triglycerides**, let's define the terms and examine the pathophysiology.

**Step 1: Define the terms**
*   **Triglycerides (TG):** A type of fat (lipid) found in the blood and stored in adipose (fat) tissue. They are synthesized by the liver or derived from dietary intake.
*   **Steatosis (Hepatic):** The abnormal retention of lipids (fats) within a cell. In the context of the liver (hepatic steatosis), it refers to the accumulation of triglycerides and other fats inside liver cells (hepatocytes).

**Step 2: Analyze the physiological process (Pathophysiology)**
*   **How Steatosis occurs:** Hepatic steatosis develops when the rate of importation or synthesis of fatty acids by the liver exceeds the rate of export or catabolism (breakdown).
*   **The role of Triglycerides:** 
    1.  The liver takes up free fatty acids from the blood.
    2.  The liver converts these free fatty acids into **triglycerides**.
    3.  Under normal conditions, these triglycerides are packaged into VLDL (Very Low-Density Lipoprotein) and exported out of the liver.
    4.  If there is an oversupply of substrates (like glucose or fatty acids), or if the export mechanism is impaired, **triglycerides accumulate within the hepatocytes**.
    5.  This accumulation *is* the condition known as **Steatosis**.

**Step 3: Evaluate the Directionality**
*   **Does Steatosis cause Triglycerides?** No. Steatosis is the *state* of having accumulated fat. While one could argue that a fatty liver might dysregulate lipid metabolism further, Steatosis itself is defined by the presence of the fat. It is the result, not the creator of the basic molecule.
*   **Do Triglycerides cause Steatosis?** Yes. Technical precision is important here: The *accumulation* of triglycerides is what physically constitutes steatosis. High levels of triglyceride synthesis and retention within the liver cell are the direct cause of the condition known as hepatic steatosis. Without the buildup of triglycerides (and other lipids), steatosis cannot exist.

**Step 4: Conclusion**
While elevated *serum* (blood) triglycerides are often a biomarker associated with fatty liver, biologically, the deposition of triglycerides inside the liver cells is the causal mechanism that results in the state of Steatosis. Therefore, the presence and accumulation of triglycerides drive the condition.
