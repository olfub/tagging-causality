   - It is possible that a person’s travel experiences (e.g., frequent business travel) could influence the decision to relocate (e.g., moving closer to an airport). However, this reverse causality is less common and generally weaker than the forward effect.

   - Numerous studies consistently show a strong correlation between residence and travel patterns, supporting a causal link from residence to travel.  
   - The reverse causality (travel → residence) is supported by far fewer empirical findings and usually occurs in specific sub‑populations (e.g., frequent business travelers).
