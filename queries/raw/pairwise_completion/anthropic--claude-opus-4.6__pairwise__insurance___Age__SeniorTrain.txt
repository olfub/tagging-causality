

# Analyzing the Cause-and-Effect Relationship

## Step-by-step reasoning:

1. **Understanding the variables:**
   - **Age**: The age of the driver/policyholder.
   - **SeniorTrain**: Whether the driver has completed a senior driver training course (e.g., a mature/senior driver improvement program).

2. **Direction of causality:**
   - A person's **age** determines whether they qualify for, are encouraged to take, or are required to complete a **senior driver training** course. These programs are specifically targeted at older drivers (typically 55+).
   - A senior training course does **not** cause a person to be a certain age — age is a fundamental demographic attribute that exists independently.

3. **In insurance risk modeling:**
   - Age is an exogenous variable (determined by nature/time).
   - SeniorTrain is a downstream decision/action that depends on the person being old enough to qualify.

## Conclusion:
Age is a precondition for participating in senior training, making **Age → SeniorTrain** the correct causal direction.
