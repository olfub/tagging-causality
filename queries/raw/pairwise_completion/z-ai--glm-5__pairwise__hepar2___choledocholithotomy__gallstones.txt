# Analyzing the Cause-Effect Relationship Between Choledocholithotomy and Gallstones

## Step 1: Understanding the Medical Terms
- **Gallstones**: Hard deposits that form in the gallbladder, composed primarily of cholesterol or bilirubin
- **Choledocholithotomy**: A surgical procedure to remove stones from the common bile duct

## Step 2: Analyzing Option A
Does choledocholithotomy cause gallstones?
- Choledocholithotomy is a treatment procedure that removes existing stones
- The purpose of this surgery is to eliminate stones, not create them
- There's no known mechanism by which this surgical procedure would cause formation of new gallstones

## Step 3: Analyzing Option B
Do gallstones cause choledocholithotomy?
- Gallstones can form in the gallbladder and sometimes migrate to the common bile duct
- When stones block the common bile duct (choledocholithiasis), they can cause pain, infection, and jaundice
- This condition requires treatment, often via choledocholithotomy
- The presence of stones in the common bile duct creates the medical necessity for this surgical intervention

## Step 4: Establishing the Temporal Relationship
- Gallstones form first (cause)
- Choledocholithotomy is performed as a treatment (effect)
- This follows the logical sequence of disease → treatment

## Step 5: Conclusion
Based on medical knowledge and logical causality, gallstones cause the need for choledocholithotomy, not the reverse.

<Answer>B</Answer>
