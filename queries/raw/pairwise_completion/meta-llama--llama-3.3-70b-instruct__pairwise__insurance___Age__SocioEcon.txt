To determine the more likely cause-and-effect relationship between "Age" and "SocioEcon" (socioeconomic status), let's break down the potential relationships step by step.

1. **Understanding the Variables**:
   - **Age**: This refers to the age of an individual. It's a biological factor that increases over time.
   - **SocioEcon (Socioeconomic Status)**: This encompasses not just income but also educational attainment, occupation, and sometimes the area of residence. It's a complex measure that reflects an individual's or group's social and economic position in relation to others, based on their access to resources, goods, and services.

2. **Analyzing Cause-and-Effect**:
   - **A. Age causes SocioEcon**: This suggests that an individual's age directly influences their socioeconomic status. While age can impact socioeconomic status indirectly (e.g., younger individuals may be in the process of acquiring education and thus have lower incomes), saying age directly causes socioeconomic status overlooks the complexity of socioeconomic factors.
   - **B. SocioEcon causes Age**: This implies that socioeconomic status influences age, which logically doesn't make sense as age is determined by the passage of time and is not a factor that socioeconomic status can directly influence.

3. **Considering Indirect and Direct Relationships**:
   - Socioeconomic status can be influenced by various factors including education, occupation, and access to resources, which can indirectly relate to age (as older individuals may have had more time to acquire education, establish a career, etc.). However, the direct causal relationship as stated is not accurate for either option in a straightforward manner.
   - It's more reasonable to consider that socioeconomic status (SocioEcon) can influence life opportunities, health outcomes, and access to resources, which might indirectly affect an individual's life expectancy or quality of life as they age, but not their age itself.

4. **Conclusion**:
   Given the above analysis, the statement "SocioEcon causes Age" (B) is logically incorrect as socioeconomic status cannot cause or change an individual's age. The age of an individual is independent of their socioeconomic status in terms of a direct cause-and-effect relationship.

   However, if we're discussing which has a more plausible indirect influence, socioeconomic status can influence factors associated with aging and life outcomes, but saying it causes age is not accurate.

   Therefore, neither statement accurately represents a direct cause-and-effect relationship as typically understood. But if we must choose based on the logical flow of potential influence, acknowledging that age can influence socioeconomic status through life stages and opportunities (e.g., education, career progression), the more consideration might lean towards age influencing socioeconomic outcomes indirectly rather than the other way around.
