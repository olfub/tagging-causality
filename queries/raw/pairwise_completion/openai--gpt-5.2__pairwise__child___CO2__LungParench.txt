1. **Interpret the variables clinically**
   - **LungParench**: primary lung parenchymal pathology (e.g., pneumonia, pulmonary edema, RDS/bronchiolitis affecting alveoli and gas exchange).
   - **CO2**: measured arterial/venous CO₂ level (reflecting adequacy of alveolar ventilation and, secondarily, V/Q matching).

2. **Physiology: what directly determines CO₂?**
   - CO₂ is mainly governed by **alveolar ventilation** (and to some extent effective ventilation given V/Q mismatch).
   - When lung parenchyma is diseased, it commonly causes **hypoventilation, increased dead space, or severe V/Q mismatch**, which can lead to **CO₂ retention (hypercapnia)**.

3. **Does elevated CO₂ typically cause lung parenchymal disease?**
   - Elevated CO₂ can cause **respiratory acidosis** and alter pulmonary vascular tone, but it **does not typically create parenchymal pathology** (it’s generally a consequence/marker of ventilatory failure rather than a cause of pneumonia/edema/etc.).

4. **Clinical directionality (especially in pediatric CHD workups)**
   - In differentiating pulmonary vs cardiac causes, **parenchymal lung disease is a common upstream driver** of abnormal blood gases, including elevated CO₂.

Therefore, the more likely causal direction is **LungParench → CO2**.
