To determine the more likely cause-and-effect relationship between cirrhosis and alcohol, let's analyze the two options:

A. Cirrhosis causes alcohol.
- This option suggests that cirrhosis, a condition characterized by scarring of the liver due to chronic liver disease, leads to the consumption of alcohol. However, the development of cirrhosis is a result of liver damage, which can be caused by various factors including chronic alcohol abuse, hepatitis, and other liver diseases. It does not directly cause the consumption of alcohol.

B. Alcohol causes cirrhosis.
- This option proposes that the consumption of alcohol leads to cirrhosis. It is well-documented in medical literature that chronic and excessive alcohol consumption can lead to alcoholic liver disease, which progresses through stages from steatosis (fatty liver) to alcoholic hepatitis, fibrosis, and eventually cirrhosis. The liver's attempt to metabolize alcohol leads to the formation of toxic substances that can cause liver inflammation and scarring.

Given the established medical understanding of liver disease and the effects of alcohol on the liver, it is clear that chronic alcohol consumption is a significant risk factor for developing cirrhosis, not the other way around.

<Answer>B</Answer>
