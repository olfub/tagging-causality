

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship: MidLLapse vs. Scenario

## Definitions
- **MidLLapse**: Mid-Level Lapse Rate — a measurable atmospheric parameter describing how quickly temperature decreases with height in the mid-levels of the atmosphere.
- **Scenario**: The overarching weather regime or synoptic pattern (e.g., type of storm environment setup).

## Step-by-step Reasoning

1. **Physical causation direction**: The large-scale weather scenario/regime (e.g., synoptic setup, air mass type, frontal configuration) dictates the thermodynamic profile of the atmosphere, including mid-level lapse rates.

2. **Modeling convention in hail forecasting**: In Bayesian network models used in hail forecasting, "Scenario" typically acts as a latent or parent node representing the weather regime, which then *generates* observable atmospheric parameters like lapse rates, shear values, and moisture profiles.

3. **A weather regime determines the lapse rate, not vice versa**: A particular synoptic pattern (e.g., an elevated mixed layer advecting over a region) causes steep mid-level lapse rates — not the other way around. The lapse rate is an *observable consequence* of the scenario.

4. **MidLLapse as an effect**: Mid-level lapse rate is a diagnostic/measurable quantity that results from the broader atmospheric setup, making it a downstream variable from the scenario.

## Conclusion

The weather **Scenario** (synoptic regime) determines the atmospheric conditions, including the **MidLLapse** rate — not the reverse.
