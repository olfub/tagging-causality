## Understanding the Variables

- **Dewpoints**: A thermodynamic measure of atmospheric moisture content — the temperature at which air becomes saturated.
- **SfcWndShfDis** (Surface Wind Shift Discontinuity): Represents mesoscale surface boundaries (fronts, drylines, outflow boundaries) where wind direction or speed changes abruptly.

## Evaluating Direction A: Dewpoints → SfcWndShfDis
- Dewpoints reflect the moisture content of an air mass.
- While strong moisture *gradients* can be associated with boundaries (e.g., drylines), the dewpoint value itself does not mechanistically cause wind to shift. Wind shifts are driven by pressure gradients and density differences between air masses, not by moisture alone.

## Evaluating Direction B: SfcWndShfDis → Dewpoints
- Surface wind shift discontinuities represent boundaries between different air masses.
- When a boundary (front, dryline, outflow boundary) passes through or is positioned nearby, it directly changes the observed dewpoints by advecting air of different moisture character into a region.
- For example: a cold front passage drops dewpoints; a dryline sharply separates moist from dry air. The boundary *determines* which air mass (and thus which dewpoint values) is observed.

## Conclusion
The physical mechanism strongly supports that surface boundaries (wind shift discontinuities) influence the local dewpoint values, not the reverse. Boundaries are the dynamical drivers that redistribute moisture.
