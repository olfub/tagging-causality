Here is the step-by-step reasoning to determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship:

**1. Define the medical terms:**
*   **Steatosis:** This is the medical term for fatty liver, specifically the accumulation of fat in the liver cells (hepatocytes). It is a pathological condition or a disease state.
*   **AST (Aspartate Aminotransferase):** This is an enzyme found primarily in the liver, heart, and muscles. Inside the liver, it is contained within the liver cells.

**2. Analyze the biological mechanism:**
*   In a healthy liver, AST levels in the blood are low because the enzyme is contained within the cells.
*   When liver cells are damaged or inflamed, the cell membranes become permeable or rupture. This causes the intracellular enzymes, including AST, to leak out into the bloodstream.
*   Therefore, elevated AST levels in a blood test are a biomarker indicating liver cell injury.

**3. Evaluate Relationship A (Steatosis causes AST):**
*   Steatosis (fatty liver) involves the accumulation of fat droplets in liver cells. This accumulation causes cellular stress, inflammation, and sometimes direct injury to the cell structure (steatohepatitis).
*   This injury causes the liver cells to leak AST into the bloodstream.
*   Therefore, the pathological process of Steatosis leads to the clinical finding of elevated AST. This relationship is biologically plausible and commonly observed in clinical practice.

**4. Evaluate Relationship B (AST causes Steatosis):**
*   AST is an enzyme involved in amino acid metabolism. It acts as a catalyst, not a structural agent.
*   There is no biological mechanism by which the presence or leakage of the AST enzyme itself causes fat to accumulate in the liver. AST is a marker of the damage, not the cause of the fat accumulation.

**5. Conclusion:**
Steatosis is the disease process that damages liver cells. This damage causes the release of AST. Therefore, Steatosis is the cause, and elevated AST is the effect.
