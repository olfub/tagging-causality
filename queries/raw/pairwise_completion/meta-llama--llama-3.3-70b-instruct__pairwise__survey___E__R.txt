To determine which cause-and-effect relationship is more likely between "Education" and "Residence," let's analyze the possible interactions step by step.

1. **Understanding the Variables**:
   - **Education**: This typically refers to the level of schooling or academic achievement an individual has attained.
   - **Residence**: This refers to the place or location where an individual lives.

2. **Analyzing Education Causes Residence (A)**:
   - If we consider "Education causes Residence," we are looking at a scenario where the level of education an individual has directly influences where they choose to or can reside.
   - There are plausible scenarios where education level could influence residence. For example, higher education might provide better job opportunities that are location-specific, or an individual might move to attend a university. However, the primary driver here seems more related to opportunities and choices that arise from education rather than education directly causing a change in residence.

3. **Analyzing Residence Causes Education (B)**:
   - Considering "Residence causes Education," we examine how where an individual lives might influence their educational attainment.
   - Residence can significantly impact access to quality education. For instance, residing in an area with better-funded schools, more educational resources, or proximity to esteemed universities can directly affect the level and quality of education one can attain. Conversely, living in areas with underfunded schools or limited access to educational resources can hinder educational opportunities.

4. **Determining the More Likely Relationship**:
   - Both directions of causality are possible, but the question asks for the more likely relationship.
   - **Residence** often determines the **quality and accessibility of educational institutions** one can attend. This can limit or expand educational opportunities based on geographical location, making residence a significant determinant in the potential level of education one can achieve.

Based on the steps above, it seems that the location of one's residence can have a significant impact on the access to and quality of education, potentially making **Residence causes Education** a more direct and likely cause-and-effect relationship in many contexts.

<Answer>B</Answer>
