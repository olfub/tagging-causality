    *   **Anorexia:** In a general medical context, this refers to a loss of appetite (not necessarily the psychiatric condition *Anorexia Nervosa*).

2.  **Analyze Relationship A (Hepatitis causes anorexia):**
    *   Inflammation of the liver (hepatitis) invokes a systemic inflammatory response.
    *   One of the classic prodromal (early warning) symptoms of viral hepatitis is significant nausea, malaise, and a profound loss of appetite (anorexia).
    *   The buildup of toxins that the liver fails to filter, along with the release of cytokines during inflammation, directly suppresses the appetite centers in the brain.
    *   Therefore, anorexia is a well-documented and common symptom of hepatitis.

3.  **Analyze Relationship B (Anorexia causes Hepatitis):**
    *   Loss of appetite (anorexia) or even the condition Anorexia Nervosa can lead to malnutrition and liver dysfunction (often seen as elevated liver enzymes).
    *   However, "Hepatitis" specifically denotes inflammation. While severe malnutrition can damage the liver (e.g., steatosis or fatty liver), simply having a loss of appetite does not typically *cause* the inflammatory condition known as hepatitis in the same direct mechanism that viruses or toxins do.
    *   Self-starvation results in liver stress, but hepatitis is not a primary downstream effect of anorexia itself.

4.  **Compare Likelihoods:**
    *   It is standard medical knowledge that hepatitis presents with anorexia as a symptom. The arrow of causality flows strongly from the liver disease to the symptom.
    *   The reverse (anorexia causing hepatitis) is not a standard etiology.

5.  **Conclusion:** The relationship where hepatitis leads to the symptom of loss of appetite is the medically accurate causal path.
