**Step‑by‑step reasoning**

1. **What is bronchitis?**  
   - Bronchitis is inflammation of the bronchial tubes (the airways that carry air into the lungs).  
   - The inflammation causes swelling, mucus production, and narrowing of the airways, which can obstruct airflow.

2. **What is dyspnoea?**  
   - Dyspnoea (shortness of breath) is a symptom—a feeling of difficulty breathing or breathlessness.  
   - It can arise from many conditions that affect the lungs, heart, or metabolic balance.

3. **Pathophysiological link**  
   - When the bronchial tubes are inflamed (bronchitis), the airway lumen is partially blocked by mucus and swelling.  
   - This obstruction makes it harder for air to move in and out of the lungs, leading to the sensation of dyspnoea.  
   - Hence, the typical clinical picture of acute or chronic bronchitis often includes dyspnoea as a symptom.

4. **Reverse relationship?**  
   - Could dyspnoea cause bronchitis? Dyspnoea is a symptom, not a disease that directly creates inflammation of the bronchi.  
   - While severe dyspnoea might lead to aggressive breathing maneuvers that irritate the airways, it is not a recognized primary cause of bronchitis.

5. **Likelihood**  
   - The scientific and clinical evidence strongly supports that bronchitis → dyspnoea (bronchitis causes the symptom).  
   - The opposite direction (dyspnoea → bronchitis) is far less plausible.

**Conclusion:** The more likely causal relationship is that bronchitis leads to dyspnoea.
