1. **Interpret the variables**
   - **hcv_anti**: anti–hepatitis C virus antibodies, a marker of **current or past HCV infection/exposure** (immune response to HCV antigens).
   - **Amylase**: an enzyme (mainly pancreatic/salivary); elevated levels typically reflect **pancreatic injury/inflammation**, salivary gland disease, reduced clearance, etc.

2. **Check biological plausibility of A (amylase → hcv_anti)**
   - Amylase is a host enzyme and does **not** induce specific anti-HCV antibodies.
   - There is no mechanism by which increased amylase would cause seroconversion to **HCV antibodies**.
   - So **A is very unlikely**.

3. **Check biological plausibility of B (hcv_anti → amylase)**
   - HCV infection (reflected by positive hcv_anti) can be associated with extrahepatic manifestations; pancreatitis is uncommon but reported, and systemic illness/inflammation can be linked with pancreatic enzyme abnormalities in some contexts.
   - Even if the association is weak, the causal direction **HCV-related illness → possible amylase elevation** is far more plausible than the reverse.

4. **Conclusion**
   - The only direction that makes biological sense is **hcv_anti causing (or being upstream of) changes in amylase**, not amylase causing anti-HCV positivity.

<Answer>B</Answer>
