Here is the step-by-step reasoning to determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship:

**Step 1: Define the terms.**
*   **Steatosis:** This is the medical term for the accumulation of fat in the cells of an organ, most commonly the liver (hepatic steatosis or "fatty liver"). It is a pathological condition characterized by the presence of lipid droplets within the cells.
*   **Triglycerides:** These are a type of fat (lipid) found in the blood and stored in fat cells. They are the main constituents of body fat and are the specific chemical compound that accumulates in the liver during steatosis.

**Step 2: Analyze the biological mechanism.**
Steatosis develops when the rate of triglyceride accumulation in the liver exceeds the rate of triglyceride removal (export or metabolism). This can happen due to increased delivery of fatty acids to the liver, increased synthesis of triglycerides within the liver, or decreased secretion of very-low-density lipoproteins (VLDL) from the liver.

**Step 3: Evaluate the causal direction.**
*   **Option A (Steatosis causes triglycerides):** This implies that the condition of having a fatty liver leads to the creation or existence of triglycerides. This is incorrect. Triglycerides are the fundamental biological molecule; they exist naturally in the body. Steatosis is the *result* of their abnormal accumulation.
*   **Option B (Triglycerides causes Steatosis):** This implies that the accumulation of the specific fat molecules leads to the condition. This aligns with the pathophysiology. The physical buildup of triglycerides within the hepatocytes (liver cells) is the direct cause of the histological finding known as steatosis. Without the accumulation of triglycerides, the condition of steatosis does not exist.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**
Steatosis is defined by the presence of triglycerides. The accumulation of triglycerides is the mechanism that creates the state of steatosis. Therefore, the presence and accumulation of triglycerides cause steatosis.

<Answer>B</Answer>
