To determine the more likely cause-and-effect relationship between steatosis and alcoholism, let's break down the concepts and their relationships step by step.

1. **Definition of Terms**:
   - **Steatosis**: This term refers to the accumulation of excess fat in cells. In the context of the liver, it's known as fatty liver disease. Steatosis can be caused by various factors including obesity, excessive alcohol consumption, and other metabolic issues.
   - **Alcoholism**: This is a condition characterized by the inability to stop or control alcohol use despite social, occupational, or health problems. Alcoholism can lead to various health issues, including liver disease.

2. **Exploring the Relationship**:
   - **Alcoholism causing Steatosis**: Excessive alcohol consumption is a well-established cause of liver damage, including the development of steatosis. Alcohol metabolism produces toxic products that can damage liver cells, leading to the accumulation of fat within the liver cells, which is known as alcoholic fatty liver disease.
   - **Steatosis causing Alcoholism**: While steatosis can be a consequence of alcoholism, it's not directly considered a cause of alcoholism. Alcoholism is more directly related to factors such as genetic predisposition, psychological factors, social environment, and stress, rather than being a direct consequence of having a fatty liver.

3. **Causal Direction**:
   - The relationship where **alcoholism causes steatosis** is more direct and well-supported by medical evidence. Alcohol consumption directly impacts the liver, leading to the accumulation of fat.
   - The reverse, **steatosis causing alcoholism**, does not have a direct causal link supported by medical evidence. Steatosis might be a result of alcoholism but does not cause the behavioral and physiological conditions that lead to alcoholism.

4. **Conclusion**:
   - Based on the understanding of both conditions and their relationship, it's more likely that alcoholism (excessive and chronic alcohol consumption) leads to steatosis (fatty liver disease) as part of the spectrum of alcoholic liver disease, rather than the other way around.
