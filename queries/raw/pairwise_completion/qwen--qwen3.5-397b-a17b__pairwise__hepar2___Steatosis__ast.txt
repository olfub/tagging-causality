1.  **Define Steatosis:** Steatosis, commonly known as fatty liver, is a pathological condition characterized by the abnormal retention of lipids (fat) within liver cells (hepatocytes). It is a structural and metabolic change in the liver tissue.

2.  **Define AST:** AST (Aspartate Aminotransferase) is an enzyme found primarily in the liver, heart, and muscles. In a healthy state, most AST is contained within liver cells. When liver cells are damaged, inflamed, or die, the cell membranes become permeable or rupture, releasing AST into the bloodstream. Therefore, serum AST levels serve as a biomarker for liver injury.

3.  **Analyze the Mechanism (Steatosis $\rightarrow$ AST):** When steatosis occurs, the accumulation of fat can cause stress, inflammation, and injury to the hepatocytes (a condition often progressing to steatohepatitis). This cellular injury causes the leakage of intracellular enzymes, including AST, into the blood. Consequently, the presence and severity of liver pathology (steatosis) lead to elevated levels of the biomarker (AST).

Elevated levels of AST in the blood are a *result* of tissue damage, not an active agent that triggers fat accumulation. There is no biological evidence suggesting that the enzyme AST itself causes lipids to accumulate in liver cells.
