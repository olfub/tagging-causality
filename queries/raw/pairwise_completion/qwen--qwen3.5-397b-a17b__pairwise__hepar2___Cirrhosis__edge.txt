## Step-by-Step Analysis

**Step 1: Understanding the terms**
- **Cirrhosis**: A chronic liver disease characterized by scarring (fibrosis) of liver tissue, leading to impaired liver function
- **Edge**: In medical context, this refers to the "liver edge" - a physical examination finding where physicians palpate the border of the liver

**Step 2: Understanding the relationship**
- The liver edge is a **physical finding** that can be observed/felt during examination
- Cirrhosis is a **disease process** that affects liver tissue structure

**Step 3: Analyzing causality direction**
- When cirrhosis develops, it causes structural changes in the liver (fibrosis, nodules, scarring)
- These structural changes manifest as observable changes in the liver edge (becoming hard, irregular, nodular, or enlarged)
- The liver edge itself is merely a **symptom/sign** of the underlying disease, not a cause

**Step 4: Evaluating the options**
- **Option A (Cirrhosis causes edge)**: Makes medical sense - the disease causes physical changes that can be detected as changes in the liver edge
- **Option B (edge causes Cirrhosis)**: Does not make medical sense - a physical examination finding cannot cause a disease process

**Step 5: Conclusion**
The disease process (cirrhosis) causes the physical manifestation (changes in liver edge), not vice versa.
