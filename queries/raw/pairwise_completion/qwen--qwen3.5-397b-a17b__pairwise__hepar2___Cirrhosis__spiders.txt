## Step-by-Step Analysis

### Step 1: Clarify the Medical Terminology
In medical contexts, "spiders" refers to **spider angiomas** (also called spider nevi or spider telangiectasias), not actual arachnid spiders. These are vascular skin lesions with a central red spot and radiating blood vessels resembling spider legs.

### Step 2: Understand Cirrhosis
Cirrhosis is a chronic liver disease where healthy liver tissue is replaced by scar tissue, impairing liver function including hormone metabolism.

### Step 3: Examine the Pathophysiology
- The damaged liver in cirrhosis cannot properly metabolize **estrogen**
- This leads to **elevated estrogen levels** in the bloodstream
- High estrogen causes **vasodilation** and the formation of spider angiomas on the skin
- Spider angiomas are considered a **clinical sign/symptom** of liver disease

### Step 4: Determine Causal Direction
- **Cirrhosis → Spider angiomas**: The liver disease causes the vascular lesions (✓ Correct)
- **Spider angiomas → Cirrhosis**: The skin lesions do not cause liver disease (✗ Incorrect)

### Step 5: Clinical Evidence
Spider angiomas are well-documented in medical literature as one of the cutaneous manifestations of chronic liver disease, particularly cirrhosis. They resolve when liver function improves or after liver transplantation.

### Conclusion
Cirrhosis (the liver disease) causes spider angiomas (the skin manifestation), not the reverse.
