**Step 1: Define the variables.**
*   **SynForcng (Synoptic Forcing):** This refers to large-scale atmospheric patterns and dynamics. It includes phenomena like fronts, troughs, jet streaks, and vorticity advection. Synoptic forcing is the "big picture" mechanism that lifts air masses, creating the environment necessary for storms to form.
*   **RHRatio:** This typically refers to a ratio involving Relative Humidity, often specifically looking at the moisture content at different levels of the atmosphere (e.g., the ratio of moisture near the freezing level vs. other levels) or entrainment rates. In general contexts, it relates to the state of moisture and humidity within a specific air column.

**Step 2: Analyze the physical processes.**
*   **Does humidity control large-scale weather patterns? (Option A):** While humidity is a component of the atmosphere, the specific ratio of relative humidity in a local column does not drive the movement of massive synoptic features like jet streams or cold fronts. The humidity is a *property* of the air mass being moved, not the engine moving it.
*   **Do large-scale weather patterns control humidity distribution? (Option B):** Yes. Synoptic forcing drives vertical motion (lifting) and advection (horizontal transport) of air.
    *   When synoptic forcing causes air to lift (as with a cold front or a shortwave trough), the air cools and relative humidity increases, potentially reaching saturation (cloud formation).
    *   Synoptic patterns transport moister or drier air into a region, directly altering the humidity profile and RHRatio.

**Step 3: Determine the causal direction.**
Synoptic forcing is the driver (the cause). It manipulates the atmospheric environment, causing changes in temperature, pressure, and moisture distribution. The RHRatio is a resulting state or condition (the effect) brought about by these large-scale movements and thermodynamic changes.

Therefore, the large-scale forcing determines the local humidity profile, not the other way around.
