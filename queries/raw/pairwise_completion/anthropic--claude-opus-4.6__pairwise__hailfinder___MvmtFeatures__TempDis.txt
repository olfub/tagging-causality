## Step-by-step reasoning:

**Step 1: Understanding the variables in hail forecasting context**
- **TempDis** (Temperature Distribution/Discontinuity): Represents thermodynamic properties of the atmosphere — temperature gradients, lapse rates, and thermal structure.
- **MvmtFeatures** (Movement Features): Represents kinematic/dynamic characteristics — storm motion, propagation patterns, wind shear-driven behavior.

**Step 2: Considering fundamental atmospheric causality**
In atmospheric science, the basic causal chain is:
> Temperature differences → Pressure gradients → Air motion/storm movement

Temperature distribution is a more **fundamental, upstream** environmental condition, while movement features are a **downstream response** to those thermodynamic conditions.

**Step 3: Application to hail forecasting**
- Temperature distributions (e.g., steep lapse rates, warm/cold advection patterns) shape the environment that determines how storms move and propagate.
- Movement features (storm motion, propagation direction) are largely *consequences* of the environmental thermal structure and resulting pressure/wind patterns.

**Step 4: Conclusion**
The thermodynamic environment (TempDis) is a driver of storm dynamics (MvmtFeatures), not the other way around. While feedback exists, the **primary causal direction** flows from temperature distribution to movement features.
