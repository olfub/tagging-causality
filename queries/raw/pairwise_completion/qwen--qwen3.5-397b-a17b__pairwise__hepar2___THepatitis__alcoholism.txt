## Step-by-Step Analysis

**Step 1: Understanding the conditions**
- **Hepatitis**: Inflammation of the liver, which can be caused by viruses (A, B, C, D, E), alcohol, toxins, medications, or autoimmune conditions
- **Alcoholism**: A chronic condition characterized by excessive, compulsive alcohol consumption (alcohol use disorder)

**Step 2: Evaluating Option A (Hepatitis causes alcoholism)**
- There is no established medical evidence that liver inflammation leads to alcohol dependence
- While someone with hepatitis might continue drinking, the disease itself doesn't cause addiction
- This direction contradicts our understanding of addiction mechanisms

**Step 3: Evaluating Option B (Alcoholism causes hepatitis)**
- Chronic excessive alcohol consumption is a **well-documented cause** of liver damage
- Alcohol is directly toxic to liver cells (hepatocytes)
- This can lead to: fatty liver → alcoholic hepatitis → cirrhosis
- "Alcoholic hepatitis" is literally named after this causal relationship
- This is established medical knowledge supported by extensive research

**Step 4: Medical consensus**
- The medical community universally recognizes alcohol abuse as a major cause of liver disease
- The reverse relationship is not recognized in medical literature

**Conclusion:**
The evidence strongly supports that alcoholism can cause hepatitis (specifically alcoholic hepatitis), while there is no credible evidence that hepatitis causes alcoholism.

<Answer>B</Answer>
