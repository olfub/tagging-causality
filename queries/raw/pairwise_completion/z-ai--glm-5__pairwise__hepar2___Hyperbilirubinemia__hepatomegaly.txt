*   **Hepatomegaly:** Enlargement of the liver. It is a physical sign detected by examination or imaging.

We must ask: Does an excess of bilirubin in the blood cause the liver to enlarge?
*   Bilirubin is a waste product of heme metabolism. While high levels can be toxic to the brain in infants (kernicterus), bilirubin itself is not generally hepatotoxic (toxic to liver cells) in adults.
*   In conditions like hemolytic anemia, high bilirubin levels occur due to the rapid breakdown of red blood cells. While the liver may enlarge in these cases, the cause of the enlargement is usually the increased workload, iron deposition, or extramedullary hematopoiesis, rather than the bilirubin concentration itself.
*   In genetic disorders like Gilbert's syndrome or Crigler-Najjar syndrome, patients have significant hyperbilirubinemia, but the liver size typically remains normal. This suggests that high bilirubin does not inherently cause liver swelling.

We must ask: Does an enlarged liver cause an excess of bilirubin in the blood?

*   **Mechanism 2 (Infiltration/Obstruction):** In diseases like amyloidosis or metastatic cancer, the liver enlarges due to the deposition of substances or tumor growth. This physical expansion can disrupt the liver's internal architecture and compress bile ducts, leading to elevated bilirubin.

This cellular damage impairs the liver's ability to excrete bilirubin, causing hyperbilirubinemia. *   Relationship A is medically implausible because bilirubin is a metabolic end-product that does not stimulate liver growth or cause inflammation.
