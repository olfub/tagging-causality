To determine the most likely cause-and-effect relationship, we need to analyze the temporal order and the mechanisms through which these variables interact in demographic studies.

**Step 1: Analyze the temporal order.**
In the life course of an individual, education is typically acquired early in life (childhood, adolescence, and early adulthood). Once an individual completes their education, they enter the labor market and establish an independent household. Therefore, education (the cause) usually precedes the establishment of one's independent, long-term residence (the effect).

**Step 2: Analyze the mechanism of Education causing Residence (Option A).**
There is a strong socioeconomic link between education and residence.
*   **Labor Market Sorting:** Higher levels of education qualify individuals for specific jobs that are often concentrated in certain geographic areas (e.g., tech hubs, financial centers, university towns, major cities). People move to these locations because their education enables them to work there.
*   **Income and Housing:** Education leads to higher income. Higher income allows individuals to afford housing in neighborhoods with specific characteristics (safer, better amenities, homeownership). This is a classic "socioeconomic status" pathway where education determines the financial resources that dictate where one can afford to live.
*   **Migration:** Educational enrollment is a primary driver of migration. People move to reside near universities. Upon graduation, they move to reside near employment.

**Step 3: Analyze the mechanism of Residence causing Education (Option B).**
While there is a correlation, the causal direction is generally weaker or refers to a different context:
*   **Reverse Causality Issues:** An individual's current residence does not cause the education they already possess. For example, living in a wealthy suburb as an adult does not cause one to have a college degree obtained years earlier.
*   **Contextual Effects (Childhood):** It is true that a *child's* residence (determined by parents) affects their educational outcomes due to school district quality. However, in demographic modeling of adult characteristics, the primary direction of flow is from achieved status (education) to living situation (residence).
*   **Access:** While living near a university might make attending classes easier (access), the primary driver is the desire for education leading to a change in residence, rather than the residence itself instilling education in the individual.

**Step 4: Compare the strength of the relationships.**
Option A describes a fundamental structural process in demography: educational attainment determines occupation and income, which in turn determine the type and location of residence (neighborhood sorting). Option B describes a spurious or secondary relationship (childhood context or access) that is less definitive for the general population. The "selection" process into neighborhoods is driven by education.

**Conclusion:**
Education is a primary determinant of socioeconomic status, which dictates the financial ability and professional necessity to live in a specific area. Therefore, education causes residence.
