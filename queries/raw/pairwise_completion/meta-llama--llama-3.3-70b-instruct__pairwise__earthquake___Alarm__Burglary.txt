To determine the more likely cause-and-effect relationship between "Alarm" and "Burglary," let's break down the scenario step by step:

1. **Understanding the Terms**: 
   - **Alarm**: A signal or warning, typically loud and designed to alert people to a potential or actual danger.
   - **Burglary**: The act of entering a building with the intention of committing a crime, typically theft.

2. **Analyzing the Relationship**:
   - **Alarm causes Burglary**: This would imply that the sounding of an alarm leads to or results in a burglary. Logically, an alarm is typically loud and designed to deter or alert others to potential wrongdoing, making it an unlikely cause for a burglary.
   - **Burglary causes Alarm**: This suggests that the act of burglary leads to or results in an alarm being triggered. This is logically consistent as many buildings are equipped with alarm systems designed to go off when someone enters improperly or when motion is detected in a supposedly vacant area.

3. **Evaluating the Logical Sequence**:
   - In a typical sequence, a burglary (or the attempt to commit one) would trigger an alarm system. The noise and commotion of a break-in, or the activation of a motion sensor, would be what causes the alarm to sound.
   - It's less plausible that the sounding of an alarm would precede and cause a burglary, as the purpose of an alarm is generally to deter such actions or to alert others to their occurrence, not to invite or provoke them.

4. **Conclusion**:
   - Based on the analysis, it is more logical and consistent with typical cause-and-effect relationships that a burglary (the act) would cause an alarm (the response to the act) rather than the other way around.
