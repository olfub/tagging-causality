In the context of intensive care, a shunt (such as a respiratory shunt where blood bypasses oxygenation in the lungs) often leads to severe hypoxemia and respiratory failure, necessitating intubation to provide mechanical ventilation and support. Conversely, intubation is a therapeutic intervention and does not typically cause a shunt. Thus, the more likely cause-and-effect relationship is that a shunt prompts the need for intubation.
