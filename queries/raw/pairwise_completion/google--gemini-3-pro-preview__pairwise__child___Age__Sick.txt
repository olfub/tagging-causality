This is a classic causal reasoning problem set within the medical domain. Let's break down the relationship between "Age" and "Sick" (sickness/disease status) step by step.

**Step 1: Analyze Option B (Sick causes Age).**
*   **Concept:** Does being sick cause a person to have a specific age or cause their age to change?
*   **Reasoning:** Age is a chronological measurement of time since birth. While severe sickness might make someone *appear* older or impact their life expectancy (how old they eventually get), the state of being sick does not mechanically cause the passage of time or determine a person's chronological age. Time passes regardless of health status.
*   **Conclusion:** This relationship is logically reversed and incorrect in a causal sense.

**Step 2: Analyze Option A (Age causes Sick).**
*   **Concept:** Does age influence the likelihood or presence of sickness?
*   **Reasoning:** In medicine, and specifically in pediatric cardiology, age is a critical determinant of health status.
    *   *Development:* As a child ages, their physiology changes. Certain congenital heart defects may present symptoms only after specific developmental milestones or ages are reached.
    *   *Accumulation:* In general medicine, the risk of many diseases increases or changes as we age due to the accumulation of cellular damage or developmental changes.
    *   *Probability:* The variable of "Age" is a predictor (cause) for the probability of being "Sick" with specific conditions. For example, a neonate is susceptible to different illnesses than a teenager.
*   **Conclusion:** Age is an independent variable that influences the dependent variable of health status (sickness).

**Step 3: Synthesis.**
Chronological age is a fundamental biological factor that influences susceptibility to disease and the manifestation of symptoms. A person's sickness does not retroactively or proactively determine their chronological age. Therefore, the causal arrow flows from Age to Sickness.
