Here is a step-by-step analysis of the relationship between diabetes and obesity:

**Step 1: Analyze the physiological mechanism of Option B (Obesity causes Diabetes).**
Medical research has established a strong causal link where excess body fat, particularly visceral fat, leads to insulin resistance. In individuals with obesity, adipose tissue releases pro-inflammatory cytokines and free fatty acids. These substances interfere with the insulin signaling pathway, making cells less responsive to insulin. To compensate, the pancreas produces more insulin. Over time, the pancreatic beta-cells cannot keep up with the increased demand, leading to their dysfunction and eventual failure. This results in hyperglycemia, the hallmark of Type 2 diabetes. Therefore, obesity is a primary driver of metabolic dysfunction leading to diabetes.

**Step 2: Analyze the physiological mechanism of Option A (Diabetes causes Obesity).**
This relationship is generally considered a reverse causation or a complication of management rather than the primary disease etiology. In fact, untreated or uncontrolled diabetes typically leads to *weight loss* because the body cannot utilize glucose and begins to break down fat and muscle for energy (catabolic state). While insulin therapy or certain medications for diabetes can lead to weight gain, the disease process itself (high blood sugar due to lack of insulin or insulin resistance) does not primarily cause obesity; rather, the underlying insulin resistance associated with the *development* of the disease promotes weight gain before diagnosis, but the obesity precedes the diabetic state.

**Step 3: Evaluate the temporal sequence.**
Longitudinal studies consistently show that weight gain and the development of obesity precede the diagnosis of Type 2 diabetes by several years. The progression is typically: Weight gain $\rightarrow$ Insulin Resistance $\rightarrow$ Prediabetes $\rightarrow$ Type 2 Diabetes. This temporal order supports obesity as the cause.

**Step 4: Consider medical interventions.**
Clinical evidence shows that lifestyle interventions resulting in weight loss can prevent or delay the onset of Type 2 diabetes in high-risk individuals. Furthermore, bariatric surgery often leads to the remission of Type 2 diabetes. The ability to reverse the diabetic state by removing the obesity strongly supports the causality running from obesity to diabetes.

**Conclusion:**
The overwhelming medical consensus is that obesity is a major risk factor and a primary cause for the development of Type 2 diabetes, driven by the mechanism of insulin resistance.
