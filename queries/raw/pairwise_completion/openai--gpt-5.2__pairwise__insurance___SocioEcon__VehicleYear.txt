1. **Define the variables**
   - **SocioEcon**: a proxy for a person/household’s socioeconomic status (income, education, neighborhood deprivation index, etc.).
   - **VehicleYear**: model year (or age) of the insured vehicle.

2. **Consider plausible causal mechanisms**
   - **SocioEcon → VehicleYear**: People with higher socioeconomic status generally have more purchasing/leasing power, better access to credit, and can more frequently replace vehicles—tending to own **newer** vehicles (higher VehicleYear). Lower socioeconomic status often correlates with keeping vehicles longer or buying used—tending to own **older** vehicles (lower VehicleYear).
   - **VehicleYear → SocioEcon**: Owning a newer vehicle might correlate with appearing wealthier, but it is unlikely to *cause* a change in underlying socioeconomic status (income/education/neighborhood deprivation). At most, VehicleYear is a **signal** of SocioEcon, not a driver of it.

3. **Check directionality / temporality**
   - Socioeconomic conditions typically precede and influence purchasing decisions (including vehicle choice and replacement timing).
   - VehicleYear is an outcome of a purchase decision, which is constrained by SocioEcon.

4. **Confounding vs causation**
   - There can be confounders (e.g., urban/rural, credit availability, household size) that affect both SocioEcon and VehicleYear, but this does not make VehicleYear a cause of SocioEcon.
   - In risk modeling terms, VehicleYear is commonly downstream of socioeconomic factors.

5. **Conclusion**
   - The more plausible causal direction is **SocioEcon causes VehicleYear**.
