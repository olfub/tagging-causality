    *   Defects such as Aortic Stenosis or Coarctation of the Aorta create pressure overload, forcing the left ventricle to pump against higher resistance.
    *   Defects such as large Ventricular Septal Defects can create volume overload.
    *   To compensate for this increased stress (particularly pressure overload), the myocardial cells enlarge, resulting in Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH).

    *   **A. Disease causes LVH**: The structural defect (the Disease) exists first (congenitally) and imposes a workload on the heart. The heart responds to this workload by developing LVH. It does not create structural congenital anomalies (like septal defects or valve malformations) that are present at birth. While LVH can lead to complications (like heart failure or arrhythmias), it is not the etiology of the congenital heart disease itself.
