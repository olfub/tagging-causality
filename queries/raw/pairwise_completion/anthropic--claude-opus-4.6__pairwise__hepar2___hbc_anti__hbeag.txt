

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship Between hbc_anti and hbeag

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

### Understanding the Variables
- **hbc_anti (Anti-HBc)**: Hepatitis B core antibody — an immune response marker indicating **current or past HBV infection**. It appears early and persists long-term.
- **hbeag (HBeAg)**: Hepatitis B e antigen — a viral marker indicating **active viral replication** and high infectivity.

### Temporal and Biological Considerations
1. **Anti-HBc** is a fundamental marker of HBV infection status. Its presence is essentially a prerequisite — you must have (or have had) HBV infection for it to appear.
2. **HBeAg** is a downstream marker that reflects a *specific phase* of infection (active replication). It can only appear in the context of an established HBV infection.
3. In causal models (e.g., HEPAR2 Bayesian network), **anti-HBc represents the underlying infection state**, which is causally upstream. HBeAg depends on that infection being present and actively replicating.

### Causal Direction
- Anti-HBc (infection indicator) → HBeAg (active replication marker) makes biological sense: the infection status (reflected by anti-HBc) is a necessary condition for HBeAg to manifest.
- The reverse (HBeAg causing anti-HBc) is less coherent — while viral replication contributes to immune response, the core antibody reflects a more fundamental state of infection.

## Conclusion
**Anti-HBc (hbc_anti)** represents the infection state that causally precedes and enables the presence of **HBeAg**.
