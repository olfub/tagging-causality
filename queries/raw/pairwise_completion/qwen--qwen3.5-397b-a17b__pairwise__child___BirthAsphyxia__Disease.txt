## Step-by-Step Reasoning

### Step 1: Define the Terms
- **Birth Asphyxia**: A condition where a baby doesn't receive adequate oxygen before, during, or immediately after birth (occurs at/perinatal period)
- **Congenital Heart Disease**: Heart defects that develop during fetal development and are present at birth (exists before birth)

### Step 2: Analyze the Temporal Relationship
- Congenital heart disease develops **during pregnancy** (typically in the first 8 weeks of gestation)
- Birth asphyxia occurs **during labor, delivery, or immediately after birth**
- The congenital condition necessarily **precedes** the birth event

### Step 3: Evaluate Causal Direction

**Option A (Birth Asphyxia → Disease):**
- Cannot be correct because the disease already exists before birth asphyxia occurs
- A congenital condition by definition cannot be caused by an event that happens at birth

**Option B (Disease → Birth Asphyxia):**
- Medically plausible: Congenital heart defects can compromise oxygen delivery
- Babies with significant heart defects have difficulty tolerating the transition from fetal to newborn circulation
- This predisposes them to oxygen deprivation during the birth process
- The pre-existing condition can contribute to asphyxia risk

### Step 4: Clinical Evidence
- Infants with congenital heart disease are at higher risk for perinatal complications
- The compromised cardiovascular system may not handle labor stress well
- This can lead to or worsen birth asphyxia

### Conclusion
The temporal sequence and medical understanding clearly indicate that the congenital heart disease (present before birth) can contribute to birth asphyxia (occurring at birth), not the reverse.

<Answer>B</Answer>
