Here is the step-by-step reasoning to determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship:

**1. Define the variables:**
*   **Age:** A chronological measure of how long a person has lived. It is a temporal variable that moves in one direction and cannot be altered by external behavioral factors.
*   **Risk Aversion:** A psychological trait or attitude reflecting an individual's tendency to avoid uncertainty and potential losses.

**2. Analyze the relationship "Age causes RiskAversion" (Option A):**
*   Biological and psychological maturation occurs as time passes.
*   As people age, they generally gain more life experience, often accumulate more assets (house, family, savings), and may experience biological changes (slower reaction times).
*   These factors contribute to a tendency to be more cautious and avoid risks to protect what one has. It is well-documented in behavioral economics and insurance data that risk-taking behavior tends to decrease as age increases. Thus, age is a causal driver of risk aversion.

**3. Analyze the relationship "RiskAversion causes Age" (Option B):**
*   This would imply that being risk-averse causes a person to accumulate years of life biologically.
*   While risk-averse individuals might have a slightly higher life expectancy (because they avoid fatal accidents or unhealthy behaviors), risk aversion does not cause the passage of time itself. A 20-year-old is 20 years old regardless of how risk-averse they are.
*   In a causal model, Age is an exogenous variable (determined by time), whereas Risk Aversion is an endogenous variable (influenced by age and other factors).

**4. Conclusion:**
Age is a fundamental demographic characteristic that precedes and shapes personality traits like risk aversion. Risk aversion is a resulting behavior or attitude, not a cause of chronological age.
