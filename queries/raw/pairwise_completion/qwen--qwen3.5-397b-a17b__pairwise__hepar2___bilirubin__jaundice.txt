## Step-by-Step Analysis

**Step 1: Understanding Bilirubin**
Bilirubin is a yellow-orange pigment produced when red blood cells break down in the body. It's normally processed by the liver and excreted through bile.

**Step 2: Understanding Jaundice**
Jaundice is a clinical sign characterized by yellow discoloration of the skin, whites of the eyes (sclera), and mucous membranes. It's a visible symptom, not a substance itself.

**Step 3: Establishing the Causal Direction**
- When bilirubin levels in the blood become elevated (hyperbilirubinemia), the excess bilirubin deposits in tissues
- This tissue deposition creates the yellow coloration that we observe and call "jaundice"
- Jaundice is the **effect/symptom** that results from high bilirubin levels
- Bilirubin is the **cause** that produces this visible sign

**Step 4: Medical Evidence**
- Doctors measure bilirubin levels to diagnose and monitor jaundice
- Treatments focus on reducing bilirubin levels to resolve jaundice
- This confirms the directional relationship flows from bilirubin to jaundice

**Step 5: Conclusion**
Elevated bilirubin is the underlying cause, and jaundice is the clinical manifestation. The reverse (jaundice causing bilirubin) doesn't make biological sense since jaundice is a symptom, not a substance that can produce bilirubin.
