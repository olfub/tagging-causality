## Step-by-Step Analysis

**Step 1: Understanding the conditions**
- **Cirrhosis** is a chronic liver disease where healthy liver tissue is replaced with scar tissue, impairing liver function
- **Alcohol** (specifically chronic, excessive consumption) is a substance that can affect the liver

**Step 2: Examining the biological mechanism**
- When alcohol is consumed, it is metabolized by the liver
- Chronic excessive alcohol consumption causes:
  - Liver cell damage and inflammation (alcoholic hepatitis)
  - Progressive scarring (fibrosis)
  - Eventually irreversible scarring (cirrhosis)

**Step 3: Evaluating causation direction**
- **Alcohol → Cirrhosis**: Well-documented in medical literature. Chronic alcohol abuse is one of the leading causes of cirrhosis worldwide
- **Cirrhosis → Alcohol**: There is no physiological mechanism by which having cirrhosis would cause someone to consume alcohol. In fact, patients with cirrhosis are medically advised to abstain from alcohol completely

**Step 4: Medical consensus**
- This causal relationship is extensively documented in medical research
- Alcohol-related liver disease progresses from fatty liver → alcoholic hepatitis → cirrhosis
- The temporal relationship shows alcohol consumption precedes the development of cirrhosis

**Conclusion:**
The established medical evidence clearly shows that chronic alcohol consumption is a cause of cirrhosis, not the reverse.
