## Step 1: Define the Terms
- **Disease**: A pathological condition or disorder affecting the body's structure or function (in this case, congenital heart disease)
- **Sick**: The state of being unwell, experiencing symptoms, or having impaired health

## Step 2: Analyze the Causal Direction
In medical causality:
- A **disease** is the underlying pathological condition that exists in the body
- Being **sick** is the manifestation or consequence of that disease affecting normal bodily function

## Step 3: Apply to Congenital Heart Disease Context
- Congenital heart disease is a structural defect present from birth
- This defect causes symptoms like fatigue, shortness of breath, cyanosis, etc.
- These symptoms make the child appear/feel "sick"
- The disease exists first and produces the sick state as an effect

## Step 4: Logical Reasoning
- You cannot become sick first and then develop a congenital disease (congenital means present at birth)
- The pathological condition (disease) precedes and produces the illness state (sick)
- This follows the standard medical model: Pathology → Symptoms → Clinical presentation

## Step 5: Conclusion
The disease is the **cause**, and being sick is the **effect**. The pathological condition produces the symptoms and illness state, not the other way around.
