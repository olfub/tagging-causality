## Step-by-Step Reasoning

**Step 1: Identify the medical terms**
- **PBC** = Primary Biliary Cholangitis (formerly Primary Biliary Cirrhosis), a chronic autoimmune liver disease
- **AMA** = Antimitochondrial Antibodies, autoantibodies that target mitochondria

**Step 2: Understand the clinical relationship**
- AMA is present in approximately 90-95% of patients diagnosed with PBC
- AMA is considered a diagnostic marker/biomarker for PBC
- AMA is highly specific for PBC but is not found in healthy individuals without the disease

**Step 3: Analyze the causal direction**
- PBC is an autoimmune disease where the immune system attacks bile ducts in the liver
- As part of this autoimmune process, the body produces various autoantibodies, including AMA
- The disease process (PBC) leads to antibody production (AMA), not vice versa
- AMA serves as a diagnostic indicator rather than a causative agent

**Step 4: Consider clinical evidence**
- Patients develop PBC pathology first, then AMA becomes detectable
- AMA levels don't correlate with disease severity or progression
- Removing AMA doesn't cure PBC, indicating AMA is not the cause
- The underlying autoimmune dysfunction causes both the liver damage and antibody production

**Step 5: Conclusion**
The disease process (PBC) causes the production of antibodies (AMA), making AMA a marker/consequence of the disease rather than its cause.
