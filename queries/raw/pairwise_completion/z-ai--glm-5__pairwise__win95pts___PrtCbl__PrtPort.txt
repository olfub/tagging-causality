The Printer Port is the host interface. The Printer Cable is the medium that relies on that interface to function. The existence and configuration of the port dictate the type of cable required (e.g., a parallel port requires a parallel cable, specific ECP modes require IEEE-1284 compliant cables).

*   **Scenario A (PrtCbl causes PrtPort):** If you change the printer cable (e.g., swap a generic cable for a bi-directional one), does the physical printer port on the computer or its BIOS configuration change? No. The port remains the same.

An ECP port configuration *causes* a requirement for a specific type of cable (an IEEE-1284 compliant cable) to function correctly. The state of the port determines the necessary state of the cable.

In the causal chain of a printer setup, the computer's hardware configuration (Port) is the root cause. The connection medium (Cable) is the dependent variable. The port settings create the conditions that the cable must meet. Therefore, the Port is the cause, and the Cable (or its selection/status) is the effect.
