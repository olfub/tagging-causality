To determine the more likely cause-and-effect relationship between "Residence" and "Travel," we must analyze the definitions and the typical structural relationships used in demographic and transportation studies.

**Step 1: Define the Variables**
*   **Residence:** This refers to a person's fixed dwelling place or home location. It is generally a stable state that serves as the baseline for an individual's daily life.
*   **Travel:** This refers to the movement of people between geographical locations. In demographic terms, travel is usually defined as movement *away from* the place of residence (e.g., commuting, leisure trips, errands).

**Step 2: Analyze "Residence causes Travel" (Option A)**
*   **Logical Flow:** A person establishes a residence first. Once established, the location of that residence dictates the necessity and mode of travel.
*   **Examples:**
    *   Living in a rural area often *causes* a higher reliance on personal vehicles for travel.
    *   Living in a dense urban center often *causes* higher usage of public transit or walking.
    *   The distance between residence and workplace *causes* the length and duration of a commute.
*   **Demographic Modeling:** In transportation planning and demographics, residential location is treated as an independent variable that predicts travel behavior (the dependent variable).

**Step 3: Analyze "Travel causes Residence" (Option B)**
*   **Logical Flow:** This suggests that the act of moving around leads to the establishment of a home.
*   **Examples:** A person might travel to a new city on vacation, like it, and decide to move there (changing residence).
*   **Limitations:** While travel can precede a *change* in residence (migration or relocation), this is a specific, less frequent event. For the vast majority of travel events (daily commutes, shopping, trips), the travel does not result in a new residence. Furthermore, without an existing residence, movement is often classified as migration or homelessness rather than standard "travel."

**Step 4: Compare Temporal Precedence and Stability**
*   **Stability:** Residence is the stable anchor; travel is the transient action.
*   **Precedence:** Typically, one must have a residence (an origin point) to undertake travel defined as leaving home. While people can move due to travel, the structural dependency in population studies is that where you live determines how you move.

**Conclusion**
While it is possible for travel to influence a decision to change residence, the fundamental causal relationship in demographics is that the characteristics of one's residence (location, type, neighborhood) determine travel patterns, frequency, and mode. Therefore, Residence is the cause, and Travel is the effect.

<Answer>A</Answer>
