

# Evaluating the Cause-and-Effect Relationship

## Step-by-step reasoning:

**Option A: Hepatitis causes alcoholism**
- There is no well-established medical mechanism by which liver inflammation (hepatitis) would lead to alcohol dependence.
- While chronic illness can sometimes contribute to substance use, hepatitis is not a recognized causal factor for alcoholism.

**Option B: Alcoholism causes hepatitis**
- This is a **well-established medical fact**. Chronic excessive alcohol consumption is a direct cause of **alcoholic hepatitis**, a condition involving inflammation of the liver.
- The progression is well-documented: heavy alcohol use → fatty liver → **alcoholic hepatitis** → potentially cirrhosis.
- Alcoholism is one of the leading causes of liver disease worldwide.

## Conclusion
The causal direction is clear: excessive alcohol consumption damages liver cells, triggering inflammation (hepatitis), not the other way around.

<Answer>B</Answer>
