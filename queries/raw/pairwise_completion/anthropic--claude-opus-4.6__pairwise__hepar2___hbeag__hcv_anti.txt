

# Evaluating the Causal Relationship Between HBeAg and Anti-HCV

## Step-by-Step Analysis

**Understanding the variables:**
- **HBeAg** (Hepatitis B e antigen): A marker indicating active Hepatitis B virus replication
- **Anti-HCV** (Hepatitis C antibody): An antibody indicating current or past Hepatitis C virus infection

**Considering shared risk factors:**
Both markers are associated with blood-borne viral infections sharing similar transmission routes (IV drug use, blood transfusions, etc.). However, shared risk factors represent confounding, not direct causation.

**Evaluating biological mechanisms:**

- **A (HBeAg → anti-HCV):** There is no known mechanism by which active HBV replication directly causes HCV infection or antibody production.

- **B (anti-HCV → HBeAg):** There is well-documented biological evidence that **HCV co-infection modulates HBV replication**. Specifically:
  - HCV core protein is known to interfere with HBV replication machinery
  - In co-infected patients, HCV often suppresses HBV viral load and can reduce HBeAg positivity
  - This is a recognized direct biological interaction between the two viruses

**Conclusion:** Option B has a supported biological causal pathway — HCV infection status (anti-HCV) can directly influence HBV replication markers (HBeAg) through viral interference mechanisms.

<Answer>B</Answer>
