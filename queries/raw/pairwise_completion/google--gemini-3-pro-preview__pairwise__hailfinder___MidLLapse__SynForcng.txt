*   **SynForcng (Synoptic Scale Forcing):** This refers to large-scale atmospheric patterns and dynamics, such as the position of jet streams, troughs, ridges, and frontal boundaries. *   **MidLLapse (Mid-Level Lapse Rates):** This refers to the rate at which temperature decreases with height in the middle levels of the atmosphere (typically roughly 700mb to 500mb). Steeper lapse rates mean the air gets colder faster as you go up, which contributes significantly to atmospheric instability (necessary for strong updrafts and hail formation).

**Step 2: Analyze the physical relationship (Does A cause B?).**
*   *Does a steep Mid-Level Lapse Rate cause Synoptic Forcing?*
    Steep lapse rates represent potential instability. While instability is crucial for thunderstorms, the local temperature profile itself does not create large-scale synoptic features like jet streaks or approaching cold fronts. Instability is a local thermodynamic condition, whereas synoptic forcing is a dynamic, large-scale driver. Therefore, MidLLapse does not cause SynForcng.

**Step 3: Analyze the physical relationship (Does B cause A?).**
*   *Does Synoptic Forcing cause steep Mid-Level Lapse Rates?*
    Synoptic scale forcing is often the mechanism that transports different air masses. For example, a synoptic trough can advect (transport) cold, dry air aloft over moist, warm air near the surface. This process ("differential advection") directly steepens the lapse rates. Specifically, strong synoptic patterns bring cold air in at the mid-levels, causing the temperature to drop rapidly with height. Thus, large-scale dynamics (SynForcng) effectively create the thermodynamic environment (MidLLapse).

**Step 4: Conclusion.**
In the hierarchy of atmospheric scales, synoptic forcing creates the environment in which specific thermodynamic profiles (like lapse rates) develop and evolve. Large-scale lifting and advection associated with synoptic forcing are physically responsible for changing the temperature profile aloft.
