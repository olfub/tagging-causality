

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship Between CO2 and CO2Report

## Step-by-Step Reasoning:

1. **Identify what each variable represents:**
   - **CO2**: The *actual* (true) carbon dioxide level in the patient's blood — a physiological state.
   - **CO2Report**: The *reported/measured* CO2 value — an observation or lab report of CO2.

2. **Determine the direction of causality:**
   - The true physiological CO2 level in the patient's blood is what determines what value appears on the lab report or measurement.
   - The report is an **observation** of the underlying true value, not the other way around.

3. **Apply general causal reasoning:**
   - In medical diagnostic models (such as Bayesian networks for congenital heart disease), the convention is that **true physiological states cause observed/reported measurements**, not the reverse.
   - A CO2 report does not change the actual CO2 level in the blood — it merely reflects it (possibly with measurement error).

## Conclusion:
The actual CO2 level (CO2) causally determines the reported CO2 value (CO2Report).
