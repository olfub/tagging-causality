1. **Check biological plausibility (mechanism).**  
   - **Flatulence → gallstones:** Flatulence is primarily due to intestinal gas production (fermentation of carbohydrates, swallowed air, altered gut motility/microbiome). There isn’t a credible mechanism by which intestinal gas would drive cholesterol supersaturation, bile stasis, or gallbladder nucleation—the key processes in gallstone formation.  
   - **Gallstones → flatulence:** Gallstones can impair bile flow intermittently or chronically. Bile acids aid fat digestion; reduced/erratic bile delivery can contribute to **dyspepsia, bloating, and increased gas symptoms** in some patients (though “gas” is nonspecific and not a classic hallmark).

2. **Match with typical clinical associations.**  
   - Gallstones are commonly associated with **postprandial discomfort**, **bloating**, **belching**, and “gassiness” complaints in real-world presentations (even if not highly specific).  
   - Flatulence is common and usually benign; it is not recognized as a risk factor for gallstones.

3. **Consider directionality and confounding.**  
   - Shared factors (diet high in fat, obesity, certain eating patterns) can contribute to both GI gas symptoms and gallstones, but this would be **confounding**, not flatulence causing stones.  
   - If forced to pick a causal direction, the more medically plausible direction is **gallstones contributing to gas/bloating symptoms**, not the reverse.
