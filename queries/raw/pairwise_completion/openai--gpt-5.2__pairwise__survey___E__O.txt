1. **Temporal order (what typically comes first):**  
   In most life courses, people complete some level of **education before** entering stable, long-term **occupational roles** (e.g., finishing high school/college/trade training before taking a career-track job).

2. **Mechanism (how one plausibly affects the other):**  
   Education builds **skills, credentials, and human capital** that directly shape:
   - eligibility for certain jobs (licensing/degree requirements),
   - access to higher-status occupations,
   - hiring and wage offers.  
   This is a well-established pathway in demographic and labor economics research.

3. **Strength and generality of the relationship:**  
   Across populations, education is a broad determinant of occupational outcomes. While the reverse can happen, it is more conditional and less universal.

4. **Reverse causality (Occupation → Education) exists but is typically secondary:**  
   Occupation can influence education through:
   - employer tuition benefits,
   - income enabling further schooling,
   - occupational requirements that prompt additional credentials.  
   However, this usually describes **adult/continuing education** and is not the dominant overall pattern compared to education shaping initial and subsequent occupational opportunities.

5. **Confounding considerations:**  
   Factors like family background, ability, and local labor markets affect both education and occupation, but even accounting for these, the **primary causal direction** in standard demographic models remains **Education → Occupation**.
