Here is the step-by-step reasoning to determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship:

1.  **Define the terms**:
    *   **Ascites** is the medical term for the abnormal buildup of fluid in the abdomen (peritoneal cavity).
    *   **Proteins** (specifically albumin in this context) are large molecules in the blood that perform many functions, one of the most important being the maintenance of oncotic (colloid osmotic) pressure.

2.  **Analyze the physiology (Starling forces)**:
    *   In the blood vessels, the heart creates pressure pushing fluid out (hydrostatic pressure), while proteins (mostly albumin) create pressure that holds fluid inside the vessels (oncotic pressure).
    *   A balance exists between these forces.

3.  **Analyze Option B (Proteins cause ascites)**:
    *   If the level of proteins in the blood drops significantly (a condition called **hypoalbuminemia**), the oncotic pressure decreases.
    *   When there is not enough protein to hold the fluid inside the blood vessels, the fluid leaks out into the surrounding tissues and body cavities.
    *   This accumulation of fluid in the abdomen is defined as ascites.
    *   This is a well-established pathophysiological mechanism seen in conditions like liver cirrhosis (where the liver fails to produce enough proteins) and nephrotic syndrome (where the kidneys leak proteins). Thus, a change in protein status is a direct cause of ascites.

4.  **Analyze Option A (Ascites causes proteins)**:
    *   Ascites is a condition resulting from fluid imbalance. It does not "cause" proteins to exist.
    *   While ascites fluid contains some protein, and the presence of massive ascites can lead to the loss of protein into the abdominal cavity (protein-losing enteropathy or third-spacing), the primary direction of causality for the *formation* of the condition is from the lack of protein to the fluid accumulation.
    *   Biologically, proteins are synthesized by the body based on genetic instructions and nutritional intake, not caused by fluid accumulation.

5.  **Conclusion**:
    *   The most direct and medically accurate causal link is that a decrease in serum protein levels leads to the formation of ascites.
