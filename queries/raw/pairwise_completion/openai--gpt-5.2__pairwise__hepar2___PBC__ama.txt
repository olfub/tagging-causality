1. **What the entities are**
   - **PBC (primary biliary cholangitis)** is an autoimmune cholestatic liver disease characterized by immune-mediated destruction of small intrahepatic bile ducts.
   - **AMA (anti‑mitochondrial antibodies)** are autoantibodies (classically against the E2 subunit of the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex) that are **strongly associated with PBC** and used diagnostically.

2. **Directionality considerations**
   - In most patients, **AMA is a marker of the autoimmune process** occurring in PBC. Clinically, we detect AMA *because* the disease process has triggered (or is accompanied by) an autoimmune response that generates these antibodies.
   - While AMAs may participate in pathogenesis and can sometimes be detectable **before** overt disease, **their presence alone is not sufficient to establish that AMA “causes” PBC** (many autoimmune markers correlate with disease without being the primary initiating cause). The more defensible causal framing is that the **underlying autoimmune dysregulation** drives both the bile duct injury (PBC) and the autoantibody production (AMA), making AMA closer to an effect/epiphenomenon or biomarker than a proven primary cause.

3. **Which is more likely given typical medical causality**
   - Given current medical understanding and how AMA is used (diagnostic association rather than established etiologic agent), **“PBC causes AMA”** is the more likely cause-and-effect relationship than “AMA causes PBC.”

<Answer>A</Answer>
