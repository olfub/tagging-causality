To determine the most likely cause-and-effect relationship, let's analyze the pathophysiology of both conditions.

**Step 1: Define the relationship between liver function and fluid balance.**
The liver plays a critical role in producing proteins, specifically albumin. Albumin helps maintain oncotic pressure (also known as colloid osmotic pressure) in the blood vessels. This pressure acts like a sponge, keeping fluid within the blood vessels and preventing it from leaking into surrounding tissues.

**Step 2: Analyze the effects of Cirrhosis (Option A).**
Cirrhosis is extensive scarring of the liver caused by long-term damage (e.g., from alcohol, hepatitis). As cirrhosis progresses:
1.  **Hypoalbuminemia:** The damaged liver produces less albumin. Low albumin levels reduce oncotic pressure, allowing fluid to leak from capillaries into the interstitial spaces (tissues).
2.  **Portal Hypertension:** Scarring obstructs blood flow through the liver, increasing pressure in the portal vein. This pushes fluid out of the blood vessels.
3.  **Hormonal Changes:** The body reacts to perceived low blood volume by retaining salt and water (via the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system), which worsens fluid accumulation.
*Conclusion:* These mechanisms lead to the accumulation of fluid in the legs (peripheral edema) and the abdomen (ascites). Therefore, cirrhosis is a well-established cause of edema.

**Step 3: Analyze the effects of Edema (Option B).**
Edema is a clinical sign—swelling caused by excess fluid trapped in body tissues. While edema indicates an underlying problem (such as heart failure, kidney disease, or liver disease), the presence of fluid in the tissues does not biologically degrade the liver or cause the scarring and fibrosis associated with cirrhosis. Edema is a *symptom* or a *consequence*, not a cause of liver parenchymal disease.

**Step 4: Compare the two relationships.**
*   A: Cirrhosis leads to physiological changes (low albumin, portal hypertension) that directly result in edema.
*   B: Edema is an accumulation of fluid that does not possess a mechanism to induce liver cirrhosis.

**Conclusion:**
The causal pathway flows from liver dysfunction (Cirrhosis) to fluid retention and swelling (Edema).

<Answer>A</Answer>
