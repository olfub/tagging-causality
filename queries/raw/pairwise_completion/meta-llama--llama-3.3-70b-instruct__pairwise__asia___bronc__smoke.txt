To determine the more likely cause-and-effect relationship between bronchitis and smoking, let's break down the information step by step:

1. **Understanding Bronchitis**: Bronchitis is an inflammation of the lining of your bronchial tubes, which carry air to and from your lungs. It can be acute or chronic. Acute bronchitis is usually caused by a viral infection, while chronic bronchitis is often associated with long-term exposure to irritating substances such as cigarette smoke.

2. **Understanding Smoking**: Smoking is the inhalation of smoke from burning tobacco products, such as cigarettes. It is a well-established risk factor for many diseases, primarily because of its harmful effects on the lungs and cardiovascular system.

3. **Association between Smoking and Bronchitis**: There is a well-documented link between smoking and the development of chronic bronchitis. Smoking damages the cilia in the lungs, which are necessary for removing dust, bacteria, and other small particles from the airways. This damage can lead to inflammation and the symptoms associated with bronchitis.

4. **Analyzing Cause and Effect**:
   - **Smoking causes Bronchitis (B)**: Given the direct harmful effects of smoking on the lungs, including the destruction of cilia and the promotion of inflammation, it's plausible that smoking can lead to the development of bronchitis. This relationship is supported by a significant body of medical evidence.
   - **Bronchitis causes Smoking (A)**: There is no established medical evidence to suggest that having bronchitis would cause someone to smoke. Instead, the likelihood is that smoking would contribute to the development of bronchitis over time due to the constant irritation and damage it causes to lung tissues.

5. **Conclusion**: Based on the established relationship between smoking and lung health, and considering the mechanisms by which smoking can lead to bronchitis, it is clear that smoking can be a cause of bronchitis, rather than the other way around.

<Answer>B</Answer>
