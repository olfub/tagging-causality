To determine the more likely cause-and-effect relationship between "Age" and "Education," let's analyze the two options step by step.

1. **Understanding the Variables**:
   - **Age**: This refers to the number of years a person has lived.
   - **Education**: This refers to the process of receiving or giving systematic instruction, especially at a school or university.

2. **Analyzing the Relationship**:
   - **Option A: Age causes Education**. This implies that as a person gets older, their level of education increases. In many educational systems, progression through the levels of education (primary, secondary, tertiary) is indeed age-related. Typically, a person's educational attainment increases as they get older, up to a certain point when they might complete their formal education.
   - **Option B: Education causes Age**. This suggests that the level of education one receives influences their age. However, education is something that happens over time and is acquired as a person grows older. It doesn't directly influence one's age; rather, one's age can influence the level and type of education they are eligible for or pursuing at any given time.

3. **Causal Direction**:
   - The concept of "cause" implies that the cause precedes the effect in time. For education to be an effect of age, it means that as age increases (a person gets older), they are likely to acquire more education, assuming they are engaged in the educational system. This temporal relationship supports the idea that age precedes and can influence the level of education one receives.
   - Conversely, saying education causes age would imply that the amount of education one receives directly influences their age, which contradicts our understanding of how age works (as a measure of the passage of time).

4. **Conclusion**:
   - Given the above analysis, it's more logical to conclude that **Age causes Education**. This is because age is a precursor to educational attainment in the sense that as individuals grow older, they typically progress through educational levels. While education can be pursued at various ages, the structure of most educational systems means that age is a determining factor in what level of education a person is likely to be pursuing at any given time.

<Answer>A</Answer>
