To determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship, we need to define the medical terms and analyze their physiological connection.

**Step 1: Define the terms.**
*   **Bronchitis:** This is a condition characterized by inflammation of the lining of the bronchial tubes, which carry air to and from the lungs. This inflammation causes mucus production, coughing, and swelling of the airways.
*   **Dyspnoea:** This is the medical term for shortness of breath or "air hunger." It is a symptom, not a disease itself. It is the subjective feeling of difficult or labored breathing.

**Step 2: Analyze Relationship A (Bronchitis causes Dyspnoea).**
*   In bronchitis, the bronchial tubes become inflamed and filled with mucus.
*   This inflammation narrows the airways and creates a physical obstruction to airflow.
*   Because the airways are narrowed and clogged, the lungs have to work harder to move air in and out.
*   This increased effort and reduced airflow leads to the sensation of shortness of breath (dyspnoea).
*   *Conclusion:* This is a biologically plausible and common progression. The disease (bronchitis) leads to the symptom (dyspnoea).

**Step 3: Analyze Relationship B (Dyspnoea causes Bronchitis).**
*   This suggests that the sensation of shortness of breath causes the bronchial tubes to become inflamed and infected.
*   While shortness of breath is a distressing symptom, it does not biologically trigger the viral or bacterial infection that causes acute bronchitis, nor does it cause the chronic irritation (usually from smoking) that causes chronic bronchitis.
*   Dyspnoea is an *effect* or a sign of an underlying problem, not a cause of structural lung disease.
*   *Conclusion:* This relationship is medically incorrect.

**Step 4: Compare the two options.**
*   Option A aligns with established medical pathology: Diseases cause symptoms.
*   Option B reverses the flow: Symptoms do not typically cause the diseases they are associated with.

**Final Conclusion:**
Bronchitis is the underlying condition (the cause) that leads to the symptom of shortness of breath (Dyspnoea).
