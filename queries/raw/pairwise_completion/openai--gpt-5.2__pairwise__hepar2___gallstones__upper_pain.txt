Let’s compare the two directions using clinical plausibility, temporality, and mechanism.

### 1) Mechanistic plausibility
- **Gallstones → upper_pain**: Gallstones can obstruct the cystic duct or common bile duct, causing increased intraluminal pressure and gallbladder contraction against an obstruction. This produces **biliary colic**, typically right upper quadrant or epigastric pain (often postprandial, especially after fatty meals), and can progress to cholecystitis/cholangitis/pancreatitis with pain.
- **upper_pain → gallstones**: Pain itself does not create gallstones. Gallstones form from bile supersaturation (cholesterol or pigment), gallbladder hypomotility, nucleation factors, and risk factors (e.g., female sex, obesity, rapid weight loss, pregnancy, hemolysis). Upper abdominal pain is not a known causal driver of stone formation.

### 2) Temporality
- Gallstones can exist asymptomatically for years and then **cause** episodes of upper abdominal pain when they intermittently obstruct.
- Upper_pain generally occurs **after** the biliary obstruction/irritation begins, not before as a causal antecedent to stone formation.

### 3) Clinical pattern / common causal framing
- In standard differential diagnosis, gallstones are a common **etiology** of upper abdominal pain.
- The reverse direction is not used clinically because it lacks a biological pathway and evidence.

### Conclusion
The more likely cause-and-effect relationship is **gallstones causes upper_pain**.
