Let’s interpret the two terms as they would commonly appear in printer diagnostics/status reporting:

- **TnrSpply** = *Toner Supply* (the actual amount/availability of toner in the cartridge).
- **PrtStatToner** = *Printer Status: Toner* (a status/indicator value Windows 95 or the printer driver reports, e.g., “OK”, “Low”, “Empty”, etc.).

### Step-by-step causal reasoning

1. **Physical reality vs. reported status**
   - *Toner supply* is a physical condition of the cartridge (how much toner is actually available).
   - *Printer status toner* is an interpretation/report of that condition (a sensor/estimation/driver-reported status).

2. **Direction of influence**
   - When **toner supply decreases**, the printer’s sensing/estimation logic changes the **toner status** to “Low” or “Empty”.
   - The reverse—changing a **status flag** in software causing more toner to exist—is not physically plausible. A status value might affect messaging or printing behavior (like warnings), but it does not create toner supply.

3. **Windows 95 troubleshooting perspective**
   - In Win95-era printer setups, the driver/status monitor typically *reads* consumable state (directly from the printer if supported, or from internal counters) and then sets a status value shown to the user.
   - Thus, **TnrSpply is upstream**, **PrtStatToner is downstream**.

### Conclusion
The more likely cause-and-effect relationship is that **toner supply determines the toner status**.
