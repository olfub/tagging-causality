1) **Define the variables clinically**
- **Disease**: the underlying congenital heart defect/pathophysiology (e.g., ductal-dependent systemic or pulmonary blood flow lesions, obstructive lesions, transposition physiology, etc.).
- **DuctFlow**: the observed presence/direction/magnitude of blood flow through the **ductus arteriosus** on echo (e.g., left-to-right, right-to-left, bidirectional; high vs low).

2) **Temporal/biologic plausibility**
- Congenital heart **disease exists first** (set by cardiac anatomy/physiology).
- The **ductal flow pattern** is a **hemodynamic consequence** of pressure/resistance relationships created by that disease (and by postnatal changes in PVR/SVR).

3) **Mechanistic link**
- Many CHDs **alter systemic vs pulmonary pressures/flows**, which directly determines:
  - whether the ductus stays open,
  - and the **direction** of shunting (e.g., right-to-left in severe left heart obstruction; left-to-right as PVR falls; bidirectional with mixed physiology).
- Thus, the disease provides a clear mechanism that *drives* DuctFlow.

4) **Reverse direction check (can DuctFlow cause Disease?)**
- Ductal flow itself generally does **not create the congenital defect**; it is typically a **marker** or **compensatory pathway**.
- While a **patent ductus arteriosus (PDA)** can cause downstream morbidity (heart failure, pulmonary overcirculation), that would be “ductal patency/left-to-right shunt causes symptoms,” not “DuctFlow causes the congenital heart disease.” Given typical diagnostic framing, DuctFlow is an observed effect of the underlying lesion.

5) **Most likely causal direction**
- **Disease → DuctFlow** is the more plausible and common relationship in pediatric CHD evaluation.
