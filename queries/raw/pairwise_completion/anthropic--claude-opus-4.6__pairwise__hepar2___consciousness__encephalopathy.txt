

## Step-by-step Reasoning

**Understanding the terms:**
- **Encephalopathy** is a broad term for brain disease, damage, or dysfunction (e.g., hepatic encephalopathy, metabolic encephalopathy, traumatic encephalopathy).
- **Consciousness** refers to a person's state of awareness and arousal.

**Evaluating Option A: Consciousness causes encephalopathy**
- Being conscious (or having a certain level of consciousness) does not lead to brain dysfunction. There is no established medical mechanism by which consciousness itself produces encephalopathy.

**Evaluating Option B: Encephalopathy causes consciousness (changes)**
- Encephalopathy is well known to **alter consciousness**. One of the hallmark clinical features of encephalopathy is a change in the level of consciousness — ranging from confusion and delirium to stupor and coma.
- In medical practice, altered consciousness is often a **consequence** of encephalopathy, not a cause.

**Conclusion:**
The causal direction flows from encephalopathy → (altered) consciousness. Brain dysfunction affects the state of consciousness, not the reverse.

<Answer>B</Answer>
