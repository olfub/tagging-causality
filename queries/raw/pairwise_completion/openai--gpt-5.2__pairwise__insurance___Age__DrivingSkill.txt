1. **Define the variables**
   - **Age**: a person’s chronological time since birth.
   - **DrivingSkill**: capability at driving (perception, reaction, hazard awareness, vehicle control), which can improve with practice and can decline with aging.

2. **Check physical/biological plausibility (can one variable change the other?)**
   - **Can DrivingSkill cause Age?** No. Improving or worsening driving skill cannot make someone older or younger.
   - **Can Age cause DrivingSkill?** Yes, plausibly and commonly:
     - **Younger → less experience** (on average), still developing judgment and risk perception.
     - **Middle ages → more experience** and often better hazard anticipation.
     - **Older ages → possible declines** in vision, reaction time, or cognition, which can reduce driving performance.

3. **Think in terms of interventions**
   - If we could “intervene” to change **Age** (hypothetically), we would expect **DrivingSkill** to change because experience and physiological factors change with age.
   - If we “intervene” to change **DrivingSkill** (training), **Age** stays the same.

4. **Common-cause vs direct cause**
   - While factors like **experience**, **training**, and **health** also influence DrivingSkill, **Age** is still a strong upstream driver through those pathways (and sometimes directly via aging effects).

Therefore, the more likely causal direction is **Age → DrivingSkill**.
