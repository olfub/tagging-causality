To determine which cause-and-effect relationship is more likely, let's analyze the biological and physiological mechanisms linking "fat" (both in terms of dietary fat and body fat/obesity) and "gallstones" (cholelithiasis).

**Step 1: Analyzing the link from "Fat" to "Gallstones" (Option A)**
*   **Obesity/Body Fat:** Being overweight or obese is a well-established major risk factor for developing gallstones. Excess body fat can increase the amount of cholesterol in the bile. When bile becomes supersaturated with cholesterol, it can crystallize and form stones. Additionally, obesity can decrease the motility of the gallbladder, preventing it from emptying completely, which also promotes stone formation.
*   **Dietary Fat:** While the relationship is complex, high intake of certain fats (specifically saturated fats and refined carbohydrates) contributes to obesity and high cholesterol levels, which in turn increases the risk of cholesterol gallstones. Conversely, rapid weight loss (often achieved by severely restricting fat) can also cause gallstones due to the liver secreting extra cholesterol into bile.
*   **Mechanism:** The causal pathway is clear: Excess adipose tissue $\rightarrow$ increased cholesterol secretion into bile $\rightarrow$ bile supersaturation $\rightarrow$ stone formation.

**Step 2: Analyzing the link from "Gallstones" to "Fat" (Option B)**
*   **Physiological impact of Gallstones:** Gallstones are hardened deposits of digestive fluid that can block the ducts of the gallbladder and liver. They cause symptoms like biliary colic (pain), cholecystitis (inflammation), and pancreatitis.
*   **Effect on Weight:** Having gallstones does not biologically cause the body to accumulate adipose tissue (fat). In fact, symptomatic gallstones often lead to *weight loss* or avoidance of fatty foods, because eating fat triggers the release of cholecystokinin (CCK), which causes the gallbladder to contract and induces pain if stones are present. Therefore, patients often adopt a low-fat diet to avoid pain, which does not cause fat accumulation.
*   **Mechanism:** There is no known physiological mechanism where the presence of a stone in the gallbladder signals the body to increase lipid storage or gain weight.

**Step 3: Comparing the Directionality**
*   **A causes B:** There is strong medical evidence and a clear biological mechanism showing that excess body fat (obesity) leads to the formation of gallstones.
*   **B causes A:** There is no mechanism by which stones cause the body to become fat.

**Conclusion**
The relationship is widely recognized in medical literature as obesity (excess fat) being a primary cause of gallstone formation.

<Answer>A</Answer>
