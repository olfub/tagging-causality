## Step 1: Understanding the Terms
- **Hepatitis**: Inflammation of the liver, typically caused by viral infection, alcohol, toxins, or autoimmune conditions
- **Phosphatase**: This refers to alkaline phosphatase (ALP), an enzyme found in liver tissue and other organs

## Step 2: Medical Pathophysiology
When hepatitis occurs:
1. Liver cells become inflamed and damaged
2. Damaged liver cells release their contents into the bloodstream
3. This includes enzymes like alkaline phosphatase
4. Blood tests show **elevated phosphatase levels** as a result

## Step 3: Determining Causal Direction
- **Hepatitis → Elevated Phosphatase**: This is the established medical relationship. Liver damage causes enzyme release.
- **Phosphatase → Hepatitis**: This is NOT correct. Enzymes are biomarkers/markers of disease, not causative agents. Elevated phosphatase doesn't cause liver inflammation.

## Step 4: Clinical Context
In medical practice:
- Doctors measure phosphatase levels to **detect** liver disease
- High phosphatase is a **consequence** of liver damage, not a cause
- Treating hepatitis may normalize phosphatase levels, not the other way around

## Conclusion
The causal relationship flows from the disease (hepatitis) to the biomarker (phosphatase elevation), not vice versa.
