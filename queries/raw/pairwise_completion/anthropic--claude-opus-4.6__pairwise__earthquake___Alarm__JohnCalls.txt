

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

This question relates to the classic **Bayesian network** known as the "Burglary-Alarm" network (from Judea Pearl's work), which models earthquake/burglary scenarios.

**Step 1: Understand the variables.**
- **Alarm**: A home alarm system that can be triggered by events like burglaries or earthquakes.
- **JohnCalls**: John calling to report that he heard the alarm.

**Step 2: Think about the real-world causal mechanism.**
- An alarm goes off due to some triggering event (earthquake, burglary, etc.).
- John *hears* or *notices* the alarm and then decides to call.
- John's call is a **response** to the alarm, not the other way around.

**Step 3: Determine the direction of causality.**
- The Alarm is the **cause** — it provides the stimulus.
- JohnCalls is the **effect** — it is the reaction/response to the alarm.
- John calling does not make the alarm go off; rather, the alarm going off prompts John to call.

**Conclusion:** The alarm activating is what causes John to call, making **A** the correct causal direction.
