To determine the cause-and-effect relationship between amylase and gallstones, let's break it down:

1. **Understanding Amylase and Gallstones**:
   - **Amylase** is an enzyme produced in the pancreas and salivary glands that helps break down carbohydrates into simple sugars. Elevated levels of amylase in the blood can indicate pancreatitis or other pancreatic issues.
   - **Gallstones** are small, hard deposits that form in the gallbladder, often composed of cholesterol or bilirubin. They can block the ducts that lead from the gallbladder or pancreas to the small intestine, potentially causing pain and other complications.

2. **Analyzing the Potential Relationship**:
   - **Option A (amylase causes gallstones)**: It's less likely that amylase directly causes gallstones. Amylase is involved in carbohydrate digestion, and its abnormal levels are more related to pancreatic conditions rather than directly influencing the formation of gallstones.
   - **Option B (gallstones cause amylase)**: This relationship is more plausible. If a gallstone blocks the bile or pancreatic ducts, it can lead to pancreatitis (inflammation of the pancreas), which in turn can cause elevated levels of amylase in the blood. Thus, gallstones can indirectly cause increased amylase levels through the mechanism of causing pancreatitis.

3. **Conclusion**:
   Based on the analysis, it's more reasonable to conclude that gallstones can lead to conditions that result in elevated amylase levels, rather than the other way around.
