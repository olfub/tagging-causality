

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship: Dewpoints vs. Scenario

## Step-by-step reasoning:

1. **What is "Scenario"?** In hail forecasting, the *Scenario* typically refers to the overarching meteorological or synoptic-scale weather regime (e.g., a warm advection pattern, a dryline setup, a frontal passage event, etc.).

2. **What are "Dewpoints"?** Dewpoints are a measure of atmospheric moisture at a given location, which is a more specific, localized atmospheric variable.

3. **Causal direction reasoning:**
   - The large-scale weather **Scenario** (synoptic pattern, air mass characteristics, moisture transport regimes) determines what dewpoint values are observed locally. For example, a Gulf moisture return scenario *causes* higher dewpoints, while a post-frontal continental scenario *causes* lower dewpoints.
   - Dewpoints alone do not *cause* the overall weather scenario — they are a **consequence** of the broader atmospheric setup.

4. **Hierarchy of causation in meteorology:** Large-scale features (scenarios/regimes) drive smaller-scale, more specific variables (like dewpoints), not the other way around.

## Conclusion:
The weather **Scenario** is the upstream causal driver that determines the observed **Dewpoints**, not vice versa.
