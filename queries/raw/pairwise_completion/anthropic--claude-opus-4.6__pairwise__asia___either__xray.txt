

# Causal Reasoning: Tuberculosis/Lung Cancer and X-ray

## Step-by-step Analysis

**Step 1: Identify what each variable represents.**
- **Tuberculosis and Lung Cancer** are diseases (conditions existing in the body).
- **X-ray** here refers to an **X-ray result/finding** (e.g., an abnormal chest X-ray).

**Step 2: Consider the direction of causation.**
- Diseases produce **observable signs** in the body (e.g., lesions, masses, infiltrates).
- When an X-ray is taken, these physical changes **caused by the disease** show up as abnormal findings.
- Therefore: **Disease → abnormal X-ray finding** (the disease causes what we see on the X-ray).

**Step 3: Evaluate Option B.**
- An X-ray is a diagnostic imaging tool. It **detects** disease; it does not **create** disease.
- Getting an X-ray does not give someone Tuberculosis or Lung Cancer.

**Step 4: Confirm with established models.**
- This matches the well-known **ASIA Bayesian network** in causal modeling, where "Either Tuberculosis or Lung Cancer" is an intermediate node that **causes** a positive X-ray result.

## Conclusion

Having either Tuberculosis or Lung Cancer causes an abnormal X-ray result — not the other way around.
