**Step 1: Define the variables.**
*   **Accident:** This variable represents the occurrence of a vehicle collision or incident. It is the physical event itself.
*   **ILiCost:** In insurance terminology, this typically stands for **"Insurance Liability Cost"** or simply the monetary cost associated with a liability claim (e.g., bodily injury or property damage caused to others).

**Step 2: Analyze Relationship A (Accident causes ILiCost).**
*   *Scenario:* A driver gets into a car accident. Because of this event, they are liable for damages, which results in a financial cost to the insurance company (the ILiCost).
*   *Logic:* This follows a chronological and physical logic. The event (accident) must happen first for the financial consequence (liability cost) to exist. If there is no accident, there is no liability claim to pay out. Therefore, the accident is the antecedent for the cost.

**Step 3: Analyze Relationship B (ILiCost causes Accident).**
*   *Scenario:* The existence of a liability cost causes a physical car accident to occur.
*   *Logic:* This violates temporal logic. A cost is a record or a payment resulting from an event. The cost cannot retroactively cause the physical event that generated the bill. While high *potential* costs might influence a driver's behavior (incentivizing them to be safer), the actual realized cost appears only after the event has transpired.

**Step 4: Conclusion from an Actuarial/Risk Modeling Perspective.**
In risk modeling (specifically Bayesian networks or Structural Equation Modeling for insurance), **Accident** is treated as the parent node. The occurrence of the accident determines whether a cost is generated and influences the magnitude of that cost.
