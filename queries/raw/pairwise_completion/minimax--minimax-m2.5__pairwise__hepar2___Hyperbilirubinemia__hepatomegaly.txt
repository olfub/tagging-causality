**Step‑by‑step reasoning**

1. **What is hyperbilirubinemia?**  
   - Elevated level of bilirubin in the blood.  
   - It results from increased production (e.g., hemolysis), impaired hepatic uptake/conjugation, or impaired bile excretion (cholestasis).  
   - Hyperbilirubinemia is a *laboratory finding* or *symptom*, not a disease that directly enlarges organs.

2. **What is hepatomegaly?**  
   - Enlargement of the liver, usually detected by physical exam or imaging.  
   - Common causes include fatty liver disease, hepatitis, cirrhosis, congestion (e.g., right‑heart failure), infiltrative disorders (tumors, amyloidosis), or biliary obstruction.  
   - Hepatomegaly reflects *structural change* in the liver and often implies compromised liver function.

3. **Possible causal directions**  

   - **A. Hyperbilirubinemia → hepatomegaly**  
     - Bilirubin itself does not stimulate liver cell proliferation or swelling.  
     - In isolated hyperbilirubinemia (e.g., hemolysis) the liver size is usually normal.  
     - Even when hyperbilirubinemia is due to liver disease, the enlargement is caused by the underlying disease (inflammation, fibrosis, congestion), not by the high bilirubin level.

   - **B. Hepatomegaly → hyperbilirubinemia**  
     - An enlarged liver often signifies hepatic pathology that impairs bilirubin metabolism/excretion.  
     - Conditions that cause hepatomegaly (hepatitis, cirrhosis, biliary obstruction, congestive hepatopathy) commonly produce elevated bilirubin.  
     - Thus, the presence of a larger liver can *lead to* reduced conjugating and excretory capacity, resulting in hyperbilirubinemia.

4. **Which direction is more plausible?**  
   - Hyperbilirubinemia is generally a *consequence* of hepatic dysfunction rather than a *cause* of liver enlargement.  
   - Hepatomegaly frequently accompanies disorders that also cause hyperbilirubinemia, making the pathway “hepatomegaly → hyperbilirubinemia” the more likely causal relationship.

**Conclusion:** The more plausible cause‑and‑effect relationship is that an enlarged liver (hepatomegaly) can lead to impaired bilirubin handling and thus to hyperbilirubinemia.

<Answer>B</Answer>
