**Step 1: Define the specific terms**
*   **Bilirubin:** A yellowish substance formed during the normal breakdown of red blood cells. It is processed by the liver and excreted in bile.
*   **Itching (Pruritus):** An uncomfortable sensation on the skin that provokes the desire to scratch.

*   When the liver cannot process bilirubin efficiently, or when bile flow is blocked (cholestasis), bilirubin accumulates in the blood and deposits in tissues (causing jaundice).
*   In cholestatic conditions, bile salts and other substances (possibly including direct bilirubin or associated byproducts like endogenous opioids or lysophosphatidic acid) accumulate.
*   While the exact mechanism is complex and debated (often attributed more to bile salts than bilirubin itself), high levels of conjugated bilirubin are a hallmark of conditions that cause severe pruritus (itching).
*   Therefore, the accumulation of bilirubin (and associated bile components) leads to the symptom of itching.

**Step 3: Analyze Relationship B (Itching $\rightarrow$ Bilirubin)**
*   Itching is a sensation and a neurological/dermatological response.
*   Scratching (the result of itching) can damage the skin, but it does not physiologically cause the liver to produce more bilirubin or block the bile ducts.
*   There is no known biological pathway where the sensation of itching causes the chemical compound bilirubin to be generated or accumulate in the body.

*   High levels of bilirubin (specifically in the context of liver excessive dysfunction or obstruction) are a known antecedent to pruritus.
*   Itching is a symptom, while high bilirubin is a biochemical state. The biochemical state causes the symptom, not vice versa.

**Conclusion:**
The accumulation of bilirubin (associated with cholestasis) leads to pruritus. Itching does not generate bilirubin.
