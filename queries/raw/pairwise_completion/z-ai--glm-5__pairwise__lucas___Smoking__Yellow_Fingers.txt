# Evaluating Cause-And-Effect Relationship Between Smoking and Yellow Fingers

I'll analyze both potential causal relationships between smoking and yellow fingers.

## Option A: Smoking causes Yellow_Fingers

Mechanism analysis:
- Cigarette smoke contains tar and nicotine
- These substances are known to stain surfaces they contact
- When holding cigarettes, fingers are in direct contact with smoke
- The staining compounds can penetrate and discolor skin
- This is a well-documented physical effect in medical literature
- Many smokers develop yellowish discoloration on their fingers (especially those used to hold cigarettes)

Temporal sequence:
- Yellow fingers develop after smoking initiation
- The discoloration increases with longer smoking history
- If a person stops smoking, the yellow discoloration may gradually fade

## Option B: Yellow_Fingers causes Smoking

Mechanism analysis:
- No plausible biological or social mechanism exists
- Yellow discoloration of fingers is a physical characteristic, not a behavioral trigger
- Having yellow fingers would not logically lead someone to begin smoking

Temporal sequence:
- Yellow fingers typically appear after someone has already started smoking
- There's no evidence of yellow fingers appearing before smoking initiation

## Conclusion
The evidence strongly supports a unidirectional causal relationship: smoking leads to yellow fingers through direct contact with staining compounds in tobacco smoke. No reasonable mechanism exists for the reverse relationship.

<Answer>A</Answer>
