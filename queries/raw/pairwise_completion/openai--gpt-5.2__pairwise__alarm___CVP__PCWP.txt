Let’s name what each pressure represents and then trace plausible causal pathways.

## 1) What are CVP and PCWP physiologically?
- **CVP (central venous pressure)** ≈ **right atrial pressure** (right-heart filling pressure / right-sided preload).
- **PCWP (pulmonary capillary wedge pressure)** ≈ **left atrial pressure** (left-heart filling pressure / left-sided preload), assuming no major pulmonary venous/mitral pathology that breaks the approximation.

They sit in **series** in the circulation:
Systemic veins → RA/RV (**CVP**) → pulmonary circulation → LA/LV (**PCWP**)

## 2) Can CVP plausibly cause changes in PCWP?
Yes, via the normal series-flow pathway:

- If **CVP rises** because of increased stressed venous volume (e.g., fluid loading) and the RV can respond,
- RV end-diastolic pressure/volume rises → RV stroke volume tends to rise (Frank–Starling),
- More blood traverses the pulmonary circulation,
- **Left atrial filling increases**, tending to raise **PCWP** (especially if LV compliance is limited).

So a **CVP → PCWP** influence is a common, straightforward mechanism, particularly during volume administration or changes in venous tone.

## 3) Can PCWP plausibly cause changes in CVP?
Sometimes, but it’s typically **more indirect**:

- If **PCWP rises** (e.g., LV failure), pulmonary venous pressure increases.
- This can increase pulmonary artery pressures (pulmonary hypertension), raising RV afterload.
- Over time, RV dysfunction/dilation can develop, which then elevates **CVP**.

That chain is real, but it generally requires an additional step (**pulmonary vascular response and/or RV failure**) and is often less immediate.

## 4) Which direction is *more likely* as a cause-and-effect relationship?
In many ICU monitoring situations (notably fluid/venous tone changes), it is more typical to see **right-sided filling pressure changes propagate forward** through the lungs to influence **left-sided filling** than for wedge pressure to directly drive CVP without intermediary pathology and time.
