To determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship, let's analyze the terms "Dewpoint" and "Scenario" within the context of meteorology and hail forecasting.

**Step 1: Define "Dewpoint".**
In meteorology, the dewpoint is a physical measurement of atmospheric moisture. It is the temperature to which air must be cooled to become saturated with water vapor. High dewpoints indicate high moisture content in the air, which is a critical ingredient for thunderstorm development and hail formation (instability).

**Step 2: Define "Scenario".**
In forecasting, a "scenario" refers to the broader synoptic or mesoscale setup. It is the overarching narrative or classification of the weather event being predicted. For example, a forecast expert might refer to a "Low CAPE / High Shear scenario" or a "Cold Core scenario." The scenario is the *result* or the *classification* of various atmospheric variables interacting.

**Step 3: Analyze the Relationship.**
*   **Does the Scenario cause the Dewpoint? (Option B)**: While the broader weather pattern (like a cold front moving in) influences what the dewpoints will be, the "Scenario" is essentially a label for the total sum of conditions. You wouldn't say the label "High Risk Scenario" physically creates the moisture in the air.
*   **Does the Dewpoint cause the Scenario? (Option A)**: The specific value of the dewpoint is a fundamental input that defines what kind of weather event is possible. For instance, if dewpoints remain in the 40s (Fahrenheit), a severe hail scenario is unlikely. If dewpoints rise into the 60s or 70s, it creates the conditions necessary for a "Severe Hail Scenario." Therefore, the specific atmospheric ingredients (like dewpoint) drive the resulting weather outcome (the scenario).

**Step 4: Conclusion.**
Forecasters build a scenario *based on* observed or predicted variables. The physical variable (Dewpoint) determines the behavior of the atmosphere, which leads to the resulting weather event (the Scenario). Without sufficient dewpoints (moisture), the scenario for hail cannot exist.
