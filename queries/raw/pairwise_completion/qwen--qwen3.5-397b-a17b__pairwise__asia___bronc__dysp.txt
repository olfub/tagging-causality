Here is the step-by-step reasoning to determine the correct causal relationship:

1.  **Define the Conditions:**
    *   **Bronchitis** is a pathological condition characterized by inflammation of the lining of the bronchial tubes. This inflammation often results in swelling, increased mucus production, and narrowing of the airways.
    *   **Dyspnoea** (or dyspnea) is a symptom described as the subjective sensation of difficult or uncomfortable breathing, often referred to as shortness of breath.

2.  **Analyze the Pathophysiology of Option A (Bronchitis causes Dyspnoea):**
    *   When bronchitis occurs, the inflammation and excess mucus obstruct the airways.
    *   This obstruction increases resistance to airflow, making it mechanically harder for the patient to ventilate their lungs.
    *   The body perceives this increased work of breathing and air hunger as dyspnoea.
    *   Therefore, the physical disease process (bronchitis) directly leads to the symptom (dyspnoea).

3.  **Analyze the Pathophysiology of Option B (Dyspnoea causes Bronchitis):**
    *   Dyspnoea is a symptom or sensation, not a pathogenic agent.
    *   Experiencing the sensation of shortness of breath does not trigger the inflammatory cascade, infection, or irritant exposure required to inflame the bronchial tubes.
    *   While anxiety related to dyspnoea can alter breathing patterns, it does not cause the tissue inflammation defined as bronchitis.

4.  **Conclusion:**
    *   In medical causality, pathological changes in lung tissue (like inflammation) cause functional symptoms (like breathlessness).
    *   Therefore, bronchitis is the cause, and dyspnoea is the effect.
