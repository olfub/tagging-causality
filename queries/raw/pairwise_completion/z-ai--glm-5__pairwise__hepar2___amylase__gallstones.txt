Here is the step-by-step reasoning to determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship:

**Step 1: Understand the biological role of amylase.**
Amylase is an enzyme produced primarily by the pancreas and the salivary glands. Its function is to help digest carbohydrates. In a clinical context, elevated levels of amylase in the blood are a key diagnostic marker, most commonly indicating injury or inflammation of the pancreas (pancreatitis).

**Step 2: Understand the formation of gallstones.**
Gallstones are solid particles that form from bile cholesterol and bilirubin in the gallbladder. The formation is typically related to the composition of the bile, the ability of the gallbladder to empty, or genetic factors. Amylase is an enzyme involved in carbohydrate digestion and does not play a role in the composition of bile or the formation of stones in the gallbladder. Therefore, it is biologically implausible that amylase causes gallstones.

**Step 3: Analyze the relationship between gallstones and amylase levels.**
While amylase does not cause gallstones, gallstones are a leading cause of elevated amylase levels. This occurs through a specific mechanism:
1.  Gallstones can migrate from the gallbladder and block the common bile duct or the pancreatic duct.
2.  This blockage prevents pancreatic enzymes from draining into the intestine.
3.  The backup of these enzymes causes inflammation of the pancreas, known as **gallstone pancreatitis**.
4.  This pancreatic injury causes amylase to leak into the bloodstream, resulting in high serum amylase levels.

**Step 4: Compare the likelihood.**
*   Option A (amylase causes gallstones): There is no known physiological mechanism for this.
*   Option B (gallstones causes amylase): This is a well-established medical pathway. Gallstones cause pancreatitis, which causes the release (and thus detection) of amylase. While the phrasing "gallstones cause amylase" is shorthand for "gallstones cause elevated amylase levels," the causal direction is medically accurate.

**Conclusion:**
Gallstones are a major cause of acute pancreatitis, which leads to elevated levels of amylase. Therefore, gallstones are the cause and the presence of amylase (in elevated levels) is the effect.
