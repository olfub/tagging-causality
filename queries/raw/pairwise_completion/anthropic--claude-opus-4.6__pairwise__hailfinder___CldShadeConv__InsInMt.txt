

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship

## Step-by-step reasoning:

**Understanding the variables:**
- **CldShadeConv** = Cloud Shade from Convection (the shading effect produced by convective clouds)
- **InsInMt** = Insolation in Mountains (the amount of solar radiation reaching mountain surfaces)

**Evaluating Option A: CldShadeConv → InsInMt**
- Convective cloud cover directly modulates how much solar radiation reaches the mountain surface.
- When convective clouds shade the terrain, insolation at the surface is reduced.
- Cloud shading acts as a physical filter/attenuator of incoming solar energy — this is a direct physical mechanism.

**Evaluating Option B: InsInMt → CldShadeConv**
- While surface heating from insolation can trigger convection (and eventually cloud formation), this involves a longer causal chain (heating → thermals → condensation → clouds → shading).
- Insolation contributes to convective initiation, but the *shading* specifically is a downstream property of existing clouds.

**Key distinction:** In the context of a hail forecasting network, CldShadeConv represents an atmospheric condition (presence of convective cloud shade) that directly determines the amount of solar radiation (InsInMt) reaching the mountains. The cloud cover is the *cause*, and the resulting surface insolation is the *effect*.
