Here is the step-by-step reasoning to determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship:

**1. Understand the biological functions:**
*   **The Liver:** The liver is the primary organ responsible for synthesizing (creating) proteins for the body.
*   **Albumin:** Albumin is a major protein produced by the liver. It helps maintain oncotic pressure (which keeps fluid inside blood vessels) and transports various substances in the blood.
*   **Cirrhosis:** Cirrhosis is a late stage of scarring (fibrosis) of the liver caused by many forms of liver diseases and conditions, such as hepatitis and chronic alcoholism. The scarring prevents the liver from functioning correctly.

**2. Analyze the mechanism:**
*   When a patient develops cirrhosis, the healthy liver tissue is replaced by scar tissue.
*   This damage significantly reduces the liver's ability to perform its normal functions, including protein synthesis.
*   Consequently, the damaged liver produces less albumin, leading to a condition called hypoalbuminemia (low albumin levels in the blood).

**3. Evaluate the causal direction:**
*   **Option A (Cirrhosis causes albumin changes):** This is medically accurate. The presence of cirrhosis leads to a decrease in the production of albumin. While the phrasing "causes albumin" is shorthand for "causes a decrease in albumin levels," the causal arrow points from the disease state to the biomarker level.
*   **Option B (Albumin causes Cirrhosis):** This is incorrect. Low albumin is a symptom or a biomarker of liver dysfunction, not the root cause of the scarring. Drinking alcohol or contracting hepatitis causes cirrhosis; having a specific level of albumin does not trigger the disease.

**4. Conclusion:**
Cirrhosis impairs the organ that makes albumin, resulting in lower levels of albumin. Therefore, Cirrhosis is the cause, and the change in albumin levels is the effect.
