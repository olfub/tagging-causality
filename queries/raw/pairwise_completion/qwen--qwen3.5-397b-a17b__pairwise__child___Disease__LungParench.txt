## Step-by-Step Reasoning

### Step 1: Understanding the Terms
- **Disease**: In this context, refers to pediatric congenital heart disease (CHD) - structural heart defects present from birth
- **LungParench**: Refers to lung parenchymal findings/abnormalities visible on imaging (chest X-ray, CT, etc.)

### Step 2: Pathophysiological Relationship
Congenital heart diseases commonly affect pulmonary circulation through several mechanisms:
- Left-to-right shunts increase pulmonary blood flow
- Heart failure causes pulmonary venous congestion
- Pulmonary hypertension develops from chronic pressure overload

These cardiac abnormalities lead to secondary changes in the lung parenchyma (edema, congestion, vascular prominence).

### Step 3: Temporal and Causal Direction
- **Congenital heart disease**: Present from birth (developmental/structural origin)
- **Lung parenchymal changes**: Develop over time as a consequence of altered hemodynamics

The heart defect exists first, and lung changes develop as a result of the cardiac pathology.

### Step 4: Medical Evidence
In pediatric cardiology:
- Lung findings on imaging are used as **indicators** of heart disease severity
- Pulmonary overcirculation patterns suggest significant shunting
- These are diagnostic clues, not primary causes

### Step 5: Ruling Out Reverse Causation
Lung parenchymal abnormalities do not cause congenital heart defects because:
- CHD arises during fetal cardiac development
- Lung parenchymal changes occur postnatally or develop secondary to cardiac function
- No known mechanism exists for lung tissue to create structural heart defects

### Conclusion
The causal relationship flows from the congenital heart disease to the lung parenchymal changes, not vice versa.
