1. **Interpret the variables**  
   - **MidLLapse** (mid-level lapse rate) is a *meteorological environment* feature: the temperature decrease with height in the mid-troposphere (often tied to EMLs, cold air aloft, etc.).  
   - **Scenario** sounds like an *outcome/category/state* used by the forecaster (e.g., a hail setup type, risk scenario, or forecast classification).

2. **Check physical direction of influence**  
   - Mid-level lapse rates strongly influence **instability (CAPE), updraft strength, and hail growth potential**, which in turn shape what “scenario” you end up in (e.g., elevated hail, discrete supercells with large hail, etc.).  
   - A “scenario” designation does **not** physically cause the atmosphere’s lapse rates to change; it’s descriptive of conditions, not a driver of them.

3. **Common-cause consideration**  
   - Both MidLLapse and Scenario can share upstream causes (e.g., an EML advecting over a moist boundary layer), but the most defensible causal arrow between the two is **environment → scenario**, not the reverse.
