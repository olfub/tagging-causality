

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship Between MeanRH and RHRatio

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

**Step 1: Define the variables**
- **MeanRH** = Mean Relative Humidity, representing the average relative humidity over an atmospheric layer. This is a fundamental atmospheric moisture measurement.
- **RHRatio** = A ratio derived from relative humidity values (e.g., the ratio of RH at one atmospheric level/layer to RH at another level/layer).

**Step 2: Determine which is more fundamental**
- MeanRH is a direct, primary measurement of the atmospheric moisture state.
- RHRatio is a *derived* quantity — it is typically computed from relative humidity values at different levels. It characterizes the vertical distribution or structure of humidity relative to some baseline.

**Step 3: Consider the physical/causal direction**
- The actual moisture content of the atmosphere (reflected in MeanRH) is a physical property that exists independently.
- RHRatio is calculated *from* humidity values. Changes in the overall moisture profile (MeanRH) will directly influence any ratio computed from those humidity values.
- It would not make physical sense for a derived ratio to *cause* the underlying mean humidity; rather, the underlying humidity field determines what the ratio will be.

**Step 4: Conclusion**
MeanRH is the more fundamental quantity, and RHRatio is derived from relative humidity measurements. Therefore, MeanRH causally influences RHRatio.
