1. **Clarify what each variable represents**
   - **Tuberculosis (TB)** and **Lung Cancer** are **diseases** (pathologic states in the lung).
   - An **X-ray** is a **diagnostic imaging test/result** (an observation/measurement).

2. **Check the plausible causal direction in medicine**
   - Diseases like **TB** or **lung cancer** can produce **abnormal findings on a chest X-ray** (e.g., infiltrates, cavitation, masses, nodules, lymphadenopathy, pleural effusion).
   - An **X-ray (the act of imaging)** does **not** biologically induce TB infection or cause lung cancer in the clinical causal sense here. (While radiation exposure can contribute to cancer risk at sufficiently high doses, standard diagnostic chest X-rays are low dose and would not plausibly be modeled as “causing lung cancer” in this kind of causal relation; TB certainly is not caused by X-ray.)

3. **Interpret the “Either TB or Lung Cancer” structure**
   - “Either TB or Lung Cancer causes X-ray” means the **presence of either disease** can lead to **X-ray evidence** (an abnormal image), which matches clinical reasoning: the X-ray is an effect/manifestation used for detection.

4. **Choose the more likely cause-and-effect relationship**
   - The medically coherent direction is **disease → imaging finding**, not **imaging → disease**.
