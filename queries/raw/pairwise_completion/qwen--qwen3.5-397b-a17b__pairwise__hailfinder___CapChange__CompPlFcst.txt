## Step-by-Step Analysis

### Step 1: Understanding the Variables

**CapChange**: This refers to changes in the atmospheric "cap" or capping inversion. A capping inversion is a layer of warm air aloft that suppresses convection. Changes in this cap are physical atmospheric processes that affect thunderstorm development potential.

**CompPlFcst**: This refers to Composite Parameter Forecast values. These are calculated meteorological indices that combine multiple atmospheric variables to help forecast severe weather (including hail).

### Step 2: Understanding the Causal Direction

In meteorological forecasting:
- **Physical atmospheric conditions** (like cap changes) occur naturally in the atmosphere
- **Forecast parameters and composite indices** are derived/calculated FROM these atmospheric conditions

### Step 3: Determining Cause and Effect

- Atmospheric conditions (CapChange) are the **input** - they represent actual physical changes in the atmosphere
- Forecast parameters (CompPlFcst) are the **output** - they are calculated based on the atmospheric conditions

The cap doesn't change because we calculate a forecast parameter. Rather, forecast parameters change because the atmospheric cap conditions change.

### Step 4: Conclusion

The logical causal flow is:
**Physical Atmospheric Changes → Forecast Parameter Calculations**

Therefore: **CapChange causes CompPlFcst**
