The liver is the primary organ responsible for synthesizing most of the proteins found in blood plasma, such as albumin and clotting factors.

Cirrhosis is a condition characterized by severe scarring of the liver and loss of liver function. Because the diseased liver loses its ability to function properly, it actually *decreases* the production of proteins (leading to conditions like hypoalbuminemia). Therefore, stating that Cirrhosis "causes proteins" is physiologically incorrect; it typically causes a deficiency of proteins.

*   **Viral Hepatitis:** The presence of viral proteins (from Hepatitis B or C) triggers chronic inflammation and immune responses in the liver, which is a leading cause of cirrhosis.

Option A describes a relationship that contradicts standard physiology (a failing liver does not create proteins; it stops creating them).
