1. **Define the variables**
   - **Hyperbilirubinemia**: elevated bilirubin in blood, typically from liver disease (e.g., hepatitis, cirrhosis), biliary obstruction (e.g., gallstones), or hemolysis, etc.
   - **Sex**: interpreted as sexual activity (or possibly biological sex). Either way, we can evaluate plausibility.

2. **Assess whether hyperbilirubinemia could plausibly cause sex (A)**
   - Hyperbilirubinemia generally causes symptoms like jaundice, pruritus, fatigue, and malaise.
   - There is no credible biological mechanism by which elevated bilirubin would *cause* someone to have sex (sexual behavior is driven by complex neuroendocrine, psychological, and social factors).
   - If anything, illness associated with hyperbilirubinemia would more commonly **reduce** libido/sexual activity rather than induce it.

3. **Assess whether sex could plausibly cause hyperbilirubinemia (B)**
   - Sexual activity can increase risk of acquiring **sexually transmissible infections** that can involve the liver, especially:
     - **Hepatitis B** (commonly sexually transmitted) → can cause hepatitis → **hyperbilirubinemia/jaundice**.
     - **Hepatitis C** (less efficiently sexually transmitted, but possible, especially in certain contexts) → can also cause hyperbilirubinemia during acute hepatitis.
   - Indirect causal chain is medically plausible: **sex → hepatitis infection → liver inflammation/cholestasis → hyperbilirubinemia**.

4. **Compare likelihood**
   - A is essentially biologically implausible.
   - B is plausible via recognized infectious disease pathways.

<Answer>B</Answer>
