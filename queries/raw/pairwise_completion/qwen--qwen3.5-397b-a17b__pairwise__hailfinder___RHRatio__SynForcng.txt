## Step-by-Step Analysis

### Step 1: Understanding the Variables

**RHRatio**: This likely represents a Relative Humidity Ratio - a thermodynamic property describing moisture content in the atmosphere, possibly between different atmospheric layers.

**SynForcng**: This represents Synoptic Forcing - large-scale atmospheric dynamics and forcing mechanisms (pressure systems, fronts, upper-level patterns) that drive weather systems.

### Step 2: Atmospheric Scale Hierarchy

In meteorology, there's a clear hierarchy of scales:
- **Synoptic scale** (1000s of km): Large-scale pressure systems, fronts, jet streams
- **Mesoscale** (10s-100s of km): Individual storm systems
- **Local scale**: Specific thermodynamic properties at a location

Larger scales **drive** smaller scales, not vice versa.

### Step 3: Physical Causal Mechanism

**Synoptic Forcing → Atmospheric Conditions**:
- Synoptic forcing determines air movement (rising, sinking, convergence, divergence)
- This air movement determines temperature and moisture profiles
- These profiles determine relative humidity distributions and ratios

**The reverse is not physically plausible**:
- Local humidity ratios don't create large-scale pressure systems or frontal boundaries
- Thermodynamic state variables don't drive dynamic forcing at the synoptic scale

### Step 4: Hail Forecasting Context

In severe weather forecasting:
1. Synoptic patterns set up the **environmental conditions**
2. These conditions determine moisture availability and stability
3. This creates potential for convection and hail

Synoptic forcing is a **prerequisite/driver**, while humidity ratios are **resulting conditions**.

### Step 5: Conclusion

The causal flow in atmospheric science goes from large-scale dynamics to local thermodynamic properties. Synoptic forcing creates the conditions that determine humidity ratios, not the other way around.
