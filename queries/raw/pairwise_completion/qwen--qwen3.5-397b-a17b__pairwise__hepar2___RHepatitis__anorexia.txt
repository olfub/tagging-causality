## Step 1: Understanding the Terms
- **Hepatitis**: Inflammation of the liver, commonly caused by viral infections (Hepatitis A, B, C, etc.), alcohol, toxins, or autoimmune conditions
- **Anorexia** (in medical context): Loss of appetite (not to be confused with anorexia nervosa, the eating disorder)

## Step 2: Clinical Presentation of Hepatitis
Hepatitis has well-documented symptoms including:
- Fatigue
- Nausea and vomiting
- **Loss of appetite (anorexia)**
- Abdominal pain
- Jaundice
- Dark urine

## Step 3: Analyzing the Causal Direction

**Option A (Hepatitis → Anorexia):**
- Liver inflammation affects metabolic processes
- Toxin buildup occurs due to impaired liver function
- This leads to nausea and loss of appetite
- This is a well-established clinical relationship

**Option B (Anorexia → Hepatitis):**
- Loss of appetite alone does not cause liver inflammation
- There is no known pathophysiological mechanism for this direction
- This is not supported by medical evidence

## Step 4: Medical Consensus
Medical literature consistently identifies anorexia (loss of appetite) as a **symptom** of hepatitis, not a cause. The liver dysfunction from hepatitis leads to systemic effects including appetite suppression.

## Conclusion
The causal relationship flows from hepatitis to anorexia, not the reverse.
