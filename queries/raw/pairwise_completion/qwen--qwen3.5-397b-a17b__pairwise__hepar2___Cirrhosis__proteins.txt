## Step-by-Step Analysis

### Step 1: Understanding Cirrhosis
Cirrhosis is a chronic liver disease where healthy liver tissue is replaced with scar tissue. It results from long-term liver damage caused by factors like:
- Alcohol abuse
- Viral hepatitis (B, C)
- Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease
- Autoimmune conditions

### Step 2: Understanding the Liver-Protein Relationship
The liver is responsible for producing many essential proteins, including:
- Albumin (maintains blood volume)
- Clotting factors (for blood coagulation)
- Transport proteins

### Step 3: Evaluating Option A (Cirrhosis → Proteins)
When cirrhosis develops:
- Liver function declines
- Protein synthesis is impaired
- Blood protein levels change (e.g., decreased albumin, altered clotting factors)

**This direction has medical validity** - cirrhosis affects protein production and levels.

### Step 4: Evaluating Option B (Proteins → Cirrhosis)
- Proteins themselves do not cause cirrhosis
- There is no known causal pathway where proteins lead to liver scarring
- Cirrhosis is caused by liver damage, not by proteins

**This direction lacks medical validity.**

### Step 5: Conclusion
While the wording "Cirrhosis causes proteins" is imprecise (it would be more accurate to say "Cirrhosis affects protein production/levels"), the causal direction in Option A is medically correct. Cirrhosis impacts protein metabolism, not the reverse.
