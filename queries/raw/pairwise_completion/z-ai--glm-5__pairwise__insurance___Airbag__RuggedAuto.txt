# Analyzing Cause-and-Effect Relationship Between Airbag and RuggedAuto

To determine which cause-and-effect relationship is more likely, I need to analyze what these terms likely represent in the context of car insurance risk modeling.

## Step 1: Understanding the terms
- **Airbag**: A safety device in vehicles designed to inflate rapidly during a collision to protect occupants from injury
- **RuggedAuto**: This likely refers to either:
  - A specific brand or model of vehicle designed for durability/off-road use
  - A category of vehicles built for rough conditions

## Step 2: Analyzing relationship A (Airbag causes RuggedAuto)
This would suggest that the presence of an airbag somehow causes a vehicle to become a rugged auto or influences its classification as such. This relationship seems illogical because:
- Airbags are safety features that can be installed in many vehicle types
- The presence of an airbag doesn't determine the ruggedness or classification of a vehicle
- Airbags are a component added to vehicles, not a defining characteristic that determines vehicle type

## Step 3: Analyzing relationship B (RuggedAuto causes Airbag)
This would suggest that being a RuggedAuto somehow causes the presence of an airbag. This relationship is more plausible because:
- Manufacturers might include airbags as standard safety features in rugged vehicles due to their intended rough use
- Rugged vehicles may require enhanced safety features due to their design purpose
- The classification or branding of a vehicle as "rugged" would precede decisions about which safety features to include

## Step 4: Conclusion
The second relationship is more plausible as vehicle classification/design would logically precede and influence decisions about safety features like airbags, rather than the reverse.

<Answer>B</Answer>
