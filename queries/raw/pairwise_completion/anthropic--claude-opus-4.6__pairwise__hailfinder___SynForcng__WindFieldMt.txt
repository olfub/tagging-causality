

# Analyzing the Cause-and-Effect Relationship

## Step-by-step reasoning:

1. **SynForcng (Synoptic Forcing)** refers to large-scale atmospheric dynamics — features like upper-level troughs, jet streams, frontal boundaries, and pressure gradients that organize weather at the synoptic scale.

2. **WindFieldMt (Wind Field at Mountain level)** refers to the local or mesoscale wind patterns observed at or near mountain environments.

3. **Causal direction in meteorology:** In atmospheric science, large-scale (synoptic) processes drive smaller-scale phenomena. Synoptic forcing establishes the broad pressure gradients and upper-level flow patterns, which in turn determine the wind fields experienced at mountain level. For example:
   - A deepening trough (synoptic forcing) increases pressure gradients → stronger winds at mountain level.
   - Frontal passages (synoptic forcing) alter wind direction and speed locally.

4. **The reverse (B)** — local mountain wind fields causing synoptic-scale forcing — is not physically consistent. While mountains can modify flow (e.g., lee waves, orographic effects), they don't *cause* the synoptic-scale forcing itself.

## Conclusion:
Synoptic forcing is the large-scale driver that shapes the wind field at mountain levels, not the other way around.
