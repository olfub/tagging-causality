To determine the most likely cause-and-effect relationship between **MeanRH** and **RHRatio** in the context of meteorology and hail forecasting, we need to understand the definitions of these variables and how they are typically derived.

**Step 1: Define the variables.**
*   **MeanRH:** This generally stands for "Mean Relative Humidity." It represents the average relative humidity across a specific layer of the atmosphere (e.g., the mixed layer or a specific pressure level) or over a specific time period. It is a fundamental measurement of moisture content relative to temperature.
*   **RHRatio:** This stands for "Relative Humidity Ratio." In severe weather and hail forecasting, this usually refers to the ratio of the relative humidity in a specific layer (often the Low-Level Relative Humidity) to the relative humidity in a different layer (often the Mid-Level Relative Humidity). Alternatively, it can refer to the ratio of the environmental RH to a critical threshold (though the layer-comparison definition is more common in indices).

**Step 2: Analyze the mathematical dependency.**
*   **How is MeanRH calculated?** It is calculated using raw observational data (temperature and dew point) from radiosondes or numerical weather prediction models. It exists independently as a fundamental state of the atmosphere.
*   **How is RHRatio calculated?** A ratio is, by definition, a derived quantity. To calculate an RHRatio, you must first have the RH values (or Mean RH values) for the specific layers you are comparing. You divide one humidity value by another.

**Step 3: Determine the direction of causality.**
*   Because RHRatio is a *function* of the underlying Relative Humidity values (MeanRH), the RHRatio cannot exist without the MeanRH values first existing.
*   Therefore, changes in the MeanRH (the input) will cause changes in the RHRatio (the output/derived metric).
*   Conversely, you cannot change the RHRatio without changing the underlying MeanRH components. The ratio is just a description of the relationship between the two humidity levels.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**
MeanRH is the independent variable (the fundamental measurement), and RHRatio is the dependent variable (the calculation derived from the measurement). Therefore, the state of the MeanRH dictates the resulting RHRatio.
