In meteorology, synoptic-scale forcing (SynForcng) refers to large-scale atmospheric processes, such as pressure gradients, frontal systems, and jet stream dynamics, which drive weather patterns. These processes establish the foundational conditions that influence smaller-scale phenomena. The wind field (WindFieldMt), which describes wind speed and direction distributions, is a direct result of these synoptic-scale forces. For example, pressure gradients from synoptic systems generate wind patterns, and terrain modifications (if "Mt" implies mountain effects) further adjust these winds but do not initiate the synoptic forcing itself. Causality in atmospheric dynamics typically flows from larger-scale drivers (synoptic) to smaller-scale responses (wind fields), not vice versa. Thus, SynForcng is the cause, and WindFieldMt is the effect.
