**Step‑by‑step reasoning**

1. **What is RHRatio?**  
   RHRatio (relative‑humidity ratio) measures how much moisture the air contains relative to the amount it could hold at that temperature and pressure. It is a *state variable* describing the moisture content of an air parcel.

2. **What is WindAloft?**  
   WindAloft (wind aloft) describes the horizontal movement of air at higher altitudes. It is a *motion variable* driven by pressure‑gradient forces, the Coriolis effect, and friction.

3. **Physical relationship**  
   - **Wind → Moisture:** Winds advect (transport) moist air from one region to another. When moist air is moved into an area, the local relative‑humidity ratio rises; when dry air is brought in, it falls. This is a direct, well‑observed mechanism (e.g., moisture plumes, low‑level jets).  
   - **Moisture → Wind:** Moisture can influence density and latent‑heat release, which may modify vertical stability and indirectly affect pressure gradients. However, the effect is indirect and generally weaker than the direct advection of moisture by wind.

4. **Causality likelihood**  
   The dominant and more immediate causal pathway in the atmosphere is that *wind aloft drives the transport of moisture, thereby changing the relative‑humidity ratio*. The reverse—RHRatio causing WindAloft—is possible only through secondary, less‑direct processes.

**Conclusion:** The more likely cause‑and‑effect relationship is that *WindAloft causes RHRatio*.
