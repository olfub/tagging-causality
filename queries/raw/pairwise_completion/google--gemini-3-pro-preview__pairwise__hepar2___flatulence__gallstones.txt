To determine the most likely cause-and-effect relationship, let's analyze the pathophysiology of both conditions.

**Step 1: Analyze Option A (Flatulence causes gallstones)**
*   **Mechanism:** Flatulence is the expulsion of gas from the digestive system through the rectum. It is primarily caused by swallowed air, the breakdown of undigested foods by bacteria in the large intestine, or lactose intolerance.
*   **Pathology of Gallstones:** Gallstones (cholelithiasis) form in the gallbladder when bile hardens into stone-like deposits. This is typically caused by an imbalance in the chemical makeup of bile (too much cholesterol, too much bilirubin, or not enough bile salts) or if the gallbladder doesn't empty correctly.
*   **Connection:** There is no known physiological mechanism by which the presence of gas in the intestines (flatulence) could cause bile to crystallize or the gallbladder to malfunction. Therefore, flatulence is not a cause of gallstones.

**Step 2: Analyze Option B (Gallstones causes flatulence)**
*   **Mechanism:** The gallbladder stores bile, which is released into the small intestine to help digest fats.
*   **Pathology:** When a person has gallstones, the stones can block the bile ducts or reduce the efficiency of the gallbladder. This leads to impaired digestion of fats.
*   **Connection:** When fats are not properly digested and absorbed in the small intestine, they travel to the large intestine. There, colonic bacteria ferment these undigested fats, producing excess gas as a byproduct. Additionally, gallstones often cause dyspepsia (indigestion), of which bloating and gas (flatulence) are common symptoms. In medical literature, "gaseousness" and flatulence are frequently cited as dyspeptic symptoms associated with symptomatic cholelithiasis.

**Conclusion:**
Flatulence does not create the conditions necessary for stone formation. Conversely, the digestive inefficiency caused by gallstones leads to fermentation in the gut, resulting in gas.
