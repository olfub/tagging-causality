Here is the step-by-step reasoning to determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship:

**Step 1: Define the terms.**
*   **Cancer (specifically Lung Cancer):** A disease characterized by the uncontrolled division of abnormal cells in the lungs. These cells can form tumors that interfere with the normal functioning of the lungs.
*   **Dyspnoea:** The medical term for shortness of breath or difficulty breathing. It is a symptom, not a distinct disease itself.

**Step 2: Analyze Option A (Cancer causes Dyspnoea).**
When a tumor grows in the lung, it can physically block airways, cause fluid to build up around the lungs (pleural effusion), or replace healthy lung tissue with non-functional tumor tissue. All of these physiological changes prevent the lungs from absorbing oxygen effectively. Therefore, the physical presence and progression of the cancer directly leads to the symptom of shortness of breath. This is a well-established clinical presentation in oncology.

**Step 3: Analyze Option B (Dyspnoea causes Cancer).**
Dyspnoea is a subjective sensation or symptom. While chronic inflammation or irritation (like from smoking) can contribute to cancer development, the act of having difficulty breathing itself does not cause cells to become cancerous. Dyspnoea is a result of a pathology, not the originator of malignant cellular mutations.

**Step 4: Compare the likelihood.**
Option A represents a standard disease-symptom relationship (Disease $\rightarrow$ Symptom). Option B suggests a symptom creates the disease, which contradicts medical pathophysiology. Therefore, it is far more likely that the cancer is the cause and the dyspnoea is the effect.

<Answer>A</Answer>
