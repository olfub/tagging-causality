This is a causal reasoning problem in the medical domain. Let's analyze the relationship between the two variables: "age" and "Hyperbilirubinemia."

**Step 1: Define the variables.**
*   **Age:** A biological variable representing the length of time a person has lived. It follows a chronological progression and is not generally reversible or causally influenced by disease states in a literal sense (though diseases can affect *aging* processes, they do not determine chronological age).
*   **Hyperbilirubinemia:** A medical condition characterized by a buildup of bilirubin in the blood, often causing jaundice.

**Step 2: Analyze Option A (Hyperbilirubinemia causes age).**
*   Can having high bilirubin levels literally make someone older in terms of chronological age? No. Time passes independently of pathology.
*   While severe illness might "age" a person's appearance or physiology, "age" as a demographic variable serves as a predictor or risk factor, not an outcome of a disease.

**Step 3: Analyze Option B (age causes Hyperbilirubinemia).**
*   Does age influence the likelihood or prevalence of Hyperbilirubinemia? Yes.
*   **Neonates (Newborns):** "Physiologic jaundice" and breast milk jaundice are extremely common in the first few days of life due to immature liver function. This is a very strong causal link where being a newborn (a specific age) is the primary risk factor for this specific type of hyperbilirubinemia.
*   **Older Adults:** While less direct than in neonates, age can be associated with conditions that cause hyperbilirubinemia, such as gallstones or malignancies (like pancreatic cancer), the incidence of which increases with age.
*   Therefore, age acts as a determinant or a cause for the varying risk levels of developing hyperbilirubinemia.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**
It is biologically impossible for a disease state (Hyperbilirubinemia) to cause chronological age. Conversely, specific ages (particularly the neonatal period) are major predisposing factors (causes) for the development of Hyperbilirubinemia.
