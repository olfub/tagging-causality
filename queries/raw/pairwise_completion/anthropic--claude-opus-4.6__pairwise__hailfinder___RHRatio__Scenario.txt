

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship: RHRatio vs. Scenario

## Step-by-step reasoning:

**Step 1: Define the variables in context**
- **Scenario** represents the overarching weather/atmospheric setup or storm environment classification (e.g., synoptic pattern, storm type category).
- **RHRatio** (Relative Humidity Ratio) is a specific, measurable atmospheric parameter describing the moisture profile (e.g., ratio of RH at different atmospheric levels).

**Step 2: Consider the physical causation**
- The large-scale atmospheric scenario (synoptic setup, airmass type, frontal position, etc.) drives the thermodynamic environment, which in turn determines the vertical moisture distribution and hence the RH ratio.
- The RH ratio is an *observable consequence* of the prevailing weather scenario, not the other way around.

**Step 3: Consider the modeling perspective**
- In causal/Bayesian network models for hail forecasting, **Scenario** typically acts as a latent or parent node representing the broader atmospheric state. Observable parameters like RHRatio are downstream (child) nodes that are generated or influenced by the scenario.
- The scenario dictates the environmental conditions, and RHRatio is one measurable manifestation of those conditions.

**Step 4: Conclusion**
The weather scenario is the broader causal driver that shapes the atmospheric moisture profile (RHRatio), not the reverse. While RHRatio might be used *diagnostically* to identify a scenario, the true causal direction flows from Scenario to RHRatio.
