Here is the step-by-step reasoning to determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship:

**1. Define the physiological role of Albumin:**
Albumin is a protein produced by the liver that circulates in the blood. Its primary physiological function is to maintain oncotic pressure (also known as colloid osmotic pressure). This pressure acts to hold water inside the blood vessels and pull fluid from the tissues back into the bloodstream.

**2. Define Edema:**
Edema is the medical term for swelling caused by excess fluid trapped in the body's tissues (the interstitial space).

**3. Analyze the mechanism linking the two (Starling forces):**
The balance of fluid between blood vessels and tissues is determined by the balance between hydrostatic pressure (pushing fluid out) and oncotic pressure (holding fluid in).
*   **Normal State:** Adequate levels of albumin create high oncotic pressure, keeping fluid inside the vessels.
*   **Pathological State (Hypoalbuminemia):** When albumin levels drop significantly (due to liver failure, nephrotic syndrome, or malnutrition), the oncotic pressure decreases. Consequently, fluid is no longer effectively held within the blood vessels. It leaks through the vessel walls into the surrounding tissues, resulting in edema.

**4. Evaluate Option A (Albumin causes edema):**
This relationship is medically accurate, specifically referring to the *lack* or low concentration of albumin (hypoalbuminemia). The change in the variable (albumin level) is the cause, and the physical manifestation (edema) is the effect.

**5. Evaluate Option B (Edema causes albumin):**
This relationship is not physiologically plausible. The accumulation of fluid in tissues (edema) does not induce the liver to produce albumin, nor does it create albumin. In fact, in conditions like nephrotic syndrome, the loss of albumin (cause) happens before the edema (effect). While severe edema can lead to the loss of proteins *from* the blood into the tissue (further lowering blood albumin), the initial causal arrow points from the albumin level to the fluid accumulation.

**Conclusion:**
A decrease in albumin levels disrupts the pressure balance required to keep fluid in the blood vessels, causing fluid to leak into tissues. Therefore, the status of albumin is the cause, and edema is the effect.
