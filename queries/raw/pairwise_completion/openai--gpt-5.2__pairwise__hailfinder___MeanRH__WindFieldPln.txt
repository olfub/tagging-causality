1. **Interpret the variables**
   - **MeanRH**: the average relative humidity in some layer/region/time window.
   - **WindFieldPln**: a representation/feature of the wind field (e.g., wind vectors/structure/planform in a layer).

2. **Physical causality in the atmosphere**
   - Wind fields arise primarily from **pressure-gradient forces** (set by temperature gradients, mass distribution) plus Coriolis/friction.
   - Relative humidity is a **thermodynamic ratio** (water vapor vs. saturation amount), strongly affected by **temperature** and **water vapor content**.

3. **How wind affects relative humidity**
   - Winds **advect moisture** (bring in drier or moister air masses).
   - Winds drive **mixing/entrainment** and can change the depth of the boundary layer, altering near-surface RH.
   - Convergence/divergence linked to wind patterns can induce **vertical motion**, changing temperature via adiabatic processes and thus changing RH.

4. **Does MeanRH cause the wind field?**
   - RH can correlate with wind regimes (both respond to the same synoptic setup), but RH itself is not typically a primary driver of the wind field. Any influence would be indirect via latent heating/cloud processes, and even then the more direct driver is **heating/temperature gradients**, not RH per se.
   - So “MeanRH → WindFieldPln” is generally less plausible as a direct causal direction.

5. **Most likely direction**
   - **WindFieldPln → MeanRH** is more consistent with atmospheric dynamics (transport/mixing/vertical motion affecting humidity).

<Answer>B</Answer>
