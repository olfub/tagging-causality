1. **Interpret the variables clinically**
   - **INTUBATION** is an intervention that places an endotracheal tube and typically initiates **invasive mechanical ventilation**.
   - **MINVOL** (minute volume / minute ventilation) in ICU monitoring is most commonly the **ventilator-measured exhaled minute ventilation** (tidal volume × respiratory rate), i.e., a value produced/recorded by the ventilator.

2. **Check what must come first for the measurement to exist**
   - Ventilator **minute volume** as a continuously charted bedside parameter generally requires the patient to be **connected to a ventilator circuit**, which in usual practice requires **intubation** (for invasive ventilation).
   - Without intubation (and thus without invasive ventilation), that specific monitored **MINVOL** often is not available in the same way (or is measured differently/less reliably).

3. **Assess the plausible causal direction**
   - **INTUBATION → MINVOL**: Once intubated, clinicians set respiratory rate and tidal volume (or pressure targets), which directly determines/changes the recorded **minute ventilation**. So the intervention has a direct mechanistic effect on MINVOL.
   - **MINVOL → INTUBATION**: While low or failing ventilation can contribute to a decision to intubate, the decision is rarely driven by “MINVOL” alone and depends on a broader clinical picture (work of breathing, gas exchange, mental status, airway protection, blood gases, etc.). Also, if MINVOL is ventilator-derived, it is not typically a pre-intubation driver.

4. **Most likely relationship in ICU monitoring data**
   - Given MINVOL is typically a ventilator-derived monitored variable, **intubation is the more plausible upstream cause** of the recorded MINVOL.
