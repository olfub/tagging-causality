

# Causal Reasoning: LVH vs. LVHreport

## Step-by-step Analysis:

1. **Define the variables:**
   - **LVH** = Left Ventricular Hypertrophy — the actual *physical condition* where the left ventricle wall is abnormally thickened.
   - **LVHreport** = The *clinical report/observation* of LVH, typically generated from ECG findings, echocardiography, or other diagnostic tools.

2. **Determine the causal direction:**
   - In medical causal models (e.g., Bayesian networks for diagnosis), the **true underlying condition** is the cause, and the **observed/reported finding** is the effect.
   - The physical condition of left ventricular hypertrophy exists in the patient *first*, and this condition then *produces* detectable signs (e.g., voltage criteria on ECG, wall thickness on echo) that lead to a report.

3. **Check the reverse:**
   - Could writing a report *cause* the heart to physically hypertrophy? No — the documentation of a finding does not create the pathology itself. The report is merely an observation of reality.

4. **Conclusion:**
   - The true disease state (LVH) causally influences whether it gets reported (LVHreport), not the other way around. This follows the standard **disease → observation** causal framework used in medical diagnostic models.
