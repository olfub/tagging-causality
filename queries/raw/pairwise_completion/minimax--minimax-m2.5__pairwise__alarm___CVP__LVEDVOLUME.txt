**Step‑by‑step reasoning**

1. **Define the variables**  
   - **CVP** (central venous pressure) ≈ right‑atrial pressure. It reflects the pressure in the right atrium and the “back‑pressure” for systemic venous return.  
   - **LVEDVOLUME** (left‑ventricular end‑diastolic volume) is the amount of blood in the left ventricle at the end of diastole – i.e., left‑ventricular preload.

2. **Place them in the circulatory loop**  
   Blood flows: systemic veins → right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary circulation → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → systemic arteries → back to veins.  
   Consequently, the **left‑ventricular output** (determined largely by LVEDVOLUME via the Frank‑Starling mechanism) determines how much blood returns to the right heart.

3. **How a change in LVEDVOLUME influences CVP**  
   - An increase in LVEDVOLUME → larger stroke volume → greater aortic flow → increased venous return to the right atrium.  
   - The higher venous return raises right‑atrial pressure, so **CVP rises**.  
   - Conversely, a fall in LVEDVOLUME reduces stroke volume, diminishing venous return and lowering CVP.

4. **How a change in CVP influences LVEDVOLUME**  
   - A rise in CVP (higher right‑atrial pressure) reduces the pressure gradient for venous return (mean systemic pressure – CVP). This decreases venous return, which can lower right‑ventricular output and thus left‑ventricular filling, tending to **decrease LVEDVOLUME**.  
   - The effect is indirect and opposite in sign; CVP is not a primary driver of left‑sided preload.

5. **Which direction is the more direct, physiologically plausible causality?**  
   - Changes in left‑ventricular preload (LVEDVOLUME) directly affect cardiac output and consequently the venous return that determines CVP.  
   - CVP mainly reflects right‑sided hemodynamics and influences LVEDVOLUME only indirectly (e.g., via inter‑ventricular interdependence or by limiting venous return).

**Conclusion:** The more likely causal direction is that **LVEDVOLUME drives CVP** (i.e., LVEDVOLUME → CVP).
