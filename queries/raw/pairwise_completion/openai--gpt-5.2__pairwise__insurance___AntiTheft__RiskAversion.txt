1. **Clarify the variables**
   - **RiskAversion**: a relatively stable individual trait/preferences (how much a person dislikes risk).
   - **AntiTheft**: a behavioral choice or outcome (installing/using anti-theft devices).

2. **Consider plausible causal mechanisms**
   - If **RiskAversion → AntiTheft**: Risk-averse drivers are more likely to take precautionary actions (install alarms, immobilizers, tracking) to reduce theft risk and potential loss. This is a straightforward behavioral pathway.
   - If **AntiTheft → RiskAversion**: Installing an anti-theft device would need to *change* someone’s underlying risk preferences. That’s less plausible—anti-theft is typically a consequence of preferences, not a driver of them.

3. **Check for common confounding (why they might be correlated)**
   - Factors like income, neighborhood theft rates, prior theft experience, vehicle value, or insurer requirements could influence **AntiTheft** and be associated with measured risk-related behaviors. But even with confounding, the more natural direction remains that **risk aversion drives adoption**.

4. **Typical interpretation in insurance risk modeling**
   - Anti-theft adoption is commonly treated as a **protective action** selected by certain types of policyholders (often more cautious/risk-averse), which supports **RiskAversion → AntiTheft**.

<Answer>B</Answer>
