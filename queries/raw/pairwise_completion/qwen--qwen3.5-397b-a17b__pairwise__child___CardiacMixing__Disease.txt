## Step 1: Understanding the Terms

**Cardiac Mixing**: In pediatric cardiology, this refers to the abnormal mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood within the heart chambers. This occurs when there are structural defects that allow blood to flow between chambers that should be separated.

**Disease**: In this context, this refers to Congenital Heart Disease (CHD) - structural abnormalities of the heart present at birth.

## Step 2: Understanding the Pathophysiology

In congenital heart disease:
- The primary problem is a **structural defect** (e.g., septal defects, transposition of great arteries, truncus arteriosus)
- These structural abnormalities are present from fetal development
- The structural defects **allow** abnormal blood flow patterns to occur
- Cardiac mixing is one of the **hemodynamic consequences** of these structural defects

## Step 3: Determining Causal Direction

- The congenital heart disease (structural abnormality) exists first
- This structural abnormality **creates the conditions** for blood to mix abnormally
- Cardiac mixing is therefore a **result/manifestation** of the underlying disease process
- Cardiac mixing does not create the structural defect; rather, the structural defect enables the mixing

## Step 4: Clinical Reasoning

From a diagnostic perspective:
- Physicians identify the underlying structural disease
- Cardiac mixing is observed as a **consequence** of that disease
- Treatment targets the underlying structural defect, which then resolves the mixing problem

## Conclusion

The disease (congenital heart defect) is the cause, and cardiac mixing is the effect.
