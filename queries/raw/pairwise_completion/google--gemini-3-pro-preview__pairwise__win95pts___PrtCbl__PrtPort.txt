To determine the most likely cause-and-effect relationship between "PrtCbl" (Printer Cable) and "PrtPort" (Printer Port) in the context of Windows 95 printer troubleshooting, let's analyze the technical dependencies.

**Step 1: Define the Variables**
*   **PrtCbl (Printer Cable):** This refers to the physical status or functionality of the cable connecting the printer to the computer (usually a parallel DB-25 cable in the Windows 95 era). If this variable is "true" or "working," the cable is transmitting data correctly. If "false," the cable is disconnected or broken.
*   **PrtPort (Printer Port):** This refers to the status of the computer's interface (LPT1) receiving data from the printer subsystem. Specifically, it often refers to whether the OS detects the port is active and connected to a device.

**Step 2: Analyze the Direction of Dependency**
*   **Does the Port determine the status of the Cable? (B: PrtPort causes PrtCbl)**
    *   No. The physical integrity of the cable exists independently of the computer's port setting. You can have a perfectly configured port on the motherboard, but if the cable is cut in half or missing, the cable is still "bad." The port settings do not create the physical cable.

*   **Does the Cable determine the status of the Port? (A: PrtCbl causes PrtPort)**
    *   Yes, in a diagnostic context. For the Printer Port (LPT1) to successfully register a connection or send data to the printer, the medium of transmission (the Cable) must be working.
    *   If the Cable is disconnected or broken (Cause), the signal cannot reach the Port, leading the diagnostic system or the user to perceive that the Port is functionally down or unable to communicate (Effect).
    *   In Bayesian networks often used for troubleshooting, the functionality of the "Port" node is conditional upon the "Cable" node being functional. If the cable fails, the port cannot function for the purpose of printing.

**Step 3: Conclusion**
In the causal chain of sending data from the PC to the printer:
1.  The Computer attempts to send data.
2.  The data travels through the virtual **Port** settings.
3.  The data travels through the physical **Cable**.
4.  The data reaches the Printer.

However, in *troubleshooting* logic (specifically referring to the Microsoft Windows 95 Printer Troubleshooter belief network):
We ask, "Why is the Port not working?" One of the reasons (causes) is that the Cable is bad. Therefore, the status of the Cable influences (causes) the resulting functional status of the Port. Just as "Battery causes Lights" in a car (a bad battery causes lights to fail), a bad Printer Cable causes the Printer Port connection to fail.
