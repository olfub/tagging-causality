

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship between HREKG and HRSAT

## Understanding the Variables

- **HREKG**: Heart rate as measured by an **electrocardiogram (EKG)**, which detects the **electrical activity** of the heart directly.
- **HRSAT**: Heart rate as measured by a **pulse oximeter (SpO2 sensor)**, which detects **pulsatile blood flow** in peripheral tissue.

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

1. **Physiological sequence**: The heart's electrical activity (detected by EKG) triggers mechanical contraction → blood is pumped → pulsatile flow reaches peripheral tissue → pulse oximeter detects the pulse wave.

2. **Directness of measurement**: HREKG is a **more proximal/direct** measurement of the heart's activity (electrical signals at the source), while HRSAT is a **more distal/downstream** measurement (peripheral blood flow as a consequence of heart contraction).

3. **Causal chain**: Electrical activity → Mechanical contraction → Blood flow → Peripheral pulse detection. This means the phenomenon measured by EKG **precedes and causes** the phenomenon measured by the pulse oximeter.

4. **In Bayesian network models** (e.g., the ALARM network for ICU monitoring), both are typically driven by the true underlying heart rate, but HREKG is closer to the causal origin, making HREKG → HRSAT the more plausible directional relationship.

## Conclusion

The EKG-based heart rate reflects the upstream electrical event that ultimately produces the peripheral pulse detected by the oximeter.
