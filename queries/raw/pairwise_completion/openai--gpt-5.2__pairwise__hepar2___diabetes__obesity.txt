1) **Clarify what “diabetes” and “obesity” mean clinically**
- Most causal discussions in epidemiology refer to **type 2 diabetes (T2D)**, because it is strongly linked to adiposity and metabolic syndrome.
- **Type 1 diabetes** is autoimmune and not caused by obesity (though weight can change after treatment).
- “Obesity” is excess adiposity, not just weight; adipose tissue is metabolically active.

2) **Assess biological plausibility**
- **Obesity → diabetes (especially T2D)** has a well-established mechanism:
  - Increased visceral and ectopic fat (liver, muscle, pancreas) promotes **insulin resistance** via lipotoxicity, inflammatory cytokines (e.g., TNF-α, IL-6), altered adipokines (↓adiponectin), and increased free fatty acids.
  - Over time, pancreatic **β-cell stress and failure** leads to hyperglycemia and overt T2D.
- **Diabetes → obesity** is less generally causal as a primary pathway:
  - Untreated diabetes can cause **weight loss** (glycosuria, catabolism).
  - Some diabetes treatments (insulin, sulfonylureas, TZDs) can cause **weight gain**, but that’s a *treatment-mediated* effect and not the dominant population-level causal direction.

3) **Check epidemiologic evidence and interventions**
- Higher BMI strongly predicts incident T2D in longitudinal cohorts (dose-response).
- **Weight loss** (lifestyle, pharmacotherapy, bariatric surgery) substantially reduces progression from prediabetes to diabetes and can induce T2D remission—supporting obesity as a causal antecedent.
- While diabetes management can influence weight, removing hyperglycemia alone does not typically “cause” obesity in the way adiposity drives T2D risk.

4) **Consider exceptions/nuance**
- There is bidirectionality in specific contexts (e.g., insulin therapy–associated weight gain), but the **more likely primary causal relationship** in general is **obesity causing (or strongly contributing to) type 2 diabetes**.
