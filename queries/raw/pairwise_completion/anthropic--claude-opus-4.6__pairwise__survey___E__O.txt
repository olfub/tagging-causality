

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

**Step 1: Temporal Order**
Education typically precedes occupation in a person's life trajectory. People usually complete their education (or a substantial portion) *before* entering a full-time occupation.

**Step 2: Causal Mechanism**
Education directly determines the range of occupations available to a person:
- A medical degree is required to become a physician.
- A law degree is required to practice law.
- Advanced technical education opens doors to engineering, research, etc.
- Lower levels of education tend to limit access to certain professional occupations.

**Step 3: Consider the Reverse**
Could occupation cause education? In some cases (e.g., returning to school mid-career), occupation might influence *further* education. However, this is a secondary and less common pathway compared to the dominant direction.

**Step 4: Demographic Research Consensus**
In sociology and demography, education is consistently modeled as a **predictor** of occupational attainment, forming part of the classic status attainment model (Blau & Duncan, 1967). Education is an upstream determinant of occupation.

## Conclusion
The dominant and more likely causal direction is that **Education causes Occupation**.
