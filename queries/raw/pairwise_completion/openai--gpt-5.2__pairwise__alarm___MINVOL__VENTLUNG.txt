MINVOL typically refers to **minute volume/minute ventilation** (total liters of gas moved per minute). It’s an *output/measurement* that results from a combination of:

- Ventilator-delivered settings (e.g., set tidal volume and respiratory rate in controlled modes), and/or  
- Patient effort interacting with ventilator support (in assisted/spontaneous modes), and  
- Lung mechanics (compliance/resistance) affecting what volume is actually delivered and measured.

VENTLUNG most naturally denotes something about **being on mechanical ventilation / ventilator-lung support** (a ventilator-related state or intervention). In ICU causal terms:

1. **Initiating or changing ventilator-lung support (VENTLUNG)** directly changes delivered ventilation (rate/volume/pressure support).
2. Those changes in delivered ventilation then change the measured **minute volume (MINVOL)**.

The reverse direction (MINVOL → VENTLUNG) is less direct: clinicians may *respond* to a low/high minute volume by adjusting ventilator support, but that’s a downstream decision process rather than the physiologic/mechanical causal generation of the measurement.
