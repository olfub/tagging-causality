

# Analyzing the Cause-and-Effect Relationship

## Step 1: Define the terms in medical context
- **Edema**: Swelling caused by excess fluid trapped in body tissues
- **Spiders**: In medicine, this could refer to **spider bites** (from arachnids) or **spider veins/angiomas**

## Step 2: Evaluate Option A (Edema → Spiders)
- Edema does not cause actual spiders to appear
- Edema and spider veins can co-occur in venous insufficiency, but edema doesn't directly *cause* spider veins — they are both consequences of the same underlying condition

## Step 3: Evaluate Option B (Spiders → Edema)
- **Spider bites** trigger an inflammatory response, leading to localized **edema** (swelling) at and around the bite site
- This is a well-established, direct cause-and-effect mechanism: venom/injury → inflammatory mediators → increased vascular permeability → fluid leakage → edema

## Step 4: Compare
Option B presents a clear, direct causal mechanism (spider bite → inflammatory response → edema), while Option A lacks a plausible direct causal pathway.

<Answer>B</Answer>
