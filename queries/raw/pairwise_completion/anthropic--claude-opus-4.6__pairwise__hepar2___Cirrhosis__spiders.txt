

# Evaluating the Cause-and-Effect Relationship

## Step-by-step reasoning:

1. **Understanding "spiders" in medical context**: "Spiders" refers to **spider angiomas** (also called spider nevi) — small, dilated blood vessels visible near the skin surface with a central arteriole and radiating capillaries resembling a spider.

2. **Understanding cirrhosis**: Cirrhosis is chronic liver scarring/damage that impairs liver function, including its ability to metabolize hormones like **estrogen**.

3. **The mechanism**: In cirrhosis, the damaged liver cannot adequately metabolize estrogen → **hyperestrogenemia** → arteriolar vasodilation → formation of spider angiomas on the skin (especially in the distribution of the superior vena cava).

4. **Direction of causality**: Spider angiomas are a well-known **clinical sign/consequence** of cirrhosis. They do not cause liver damage themselves — they are a downstream manifestation of the hormonal imbalance caused by liver dysfunction.

## Conclusion:
Cirrhosis leads to impaired estrogen metabolism, which causes the development of spider angiomas — not the reverse.
