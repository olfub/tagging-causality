## Step-by-Step Analysis

### Step 1: Understanding the Variables

**Hyperbilirubinemia:** A medical condition characterized by elevated levels of bilirubin in the blood. It's commonly associated with liver dysfunction, hemolysis, or immature liver function (especially in newborns).

**Age:** A temporal measurement representing the time elapsed since birth. It's a demographic variable that cannot be changed by medical conditions.

### Step 2: Evaluating Option A (Hyperbilirubinemia causes age)

- **Logical problem:** Age is determined solely by time since birth
- **Temporal impossibility:** A medical condition cannot alter how long someone has been alive
- **Medical evidence:** There is no mechanism by which elevated bilirubin levels could cause aging or change a person's age
- **Conclusion:** This causal direction is not scientifically plausible

### Step 3: Evaluating Option B (Age causes Hyperbilirubinemia)

- **Logical consistency:** Age influences physiological processes that affect bilirubin metabolism
- **Medical evidence:**
  - **Newborns:** Physiological jaundice is very common due to immature liver enzyme systems
  - **Older adults:** Age-related liver disease, gallstones, and malignancies increase hyperbilirubinemia risk
  - **Liver function:** Changes with age, affecting bilirubin processing
- **Risk factor relationship:** Age is consistently used as a risk stratification factor for hyperbilirubinemia in medical literature
- **Conclusion:** This causal direction is medically and scientifically supported

### Step 4: Final Determination

Age is a predisposing factor that can influence the development of hyperbilirubinemia through various physiological mechanisms. The reverse relationship (hyperbilirubinemia causing age) is logically impossible.

<Answer>B</Answer>
