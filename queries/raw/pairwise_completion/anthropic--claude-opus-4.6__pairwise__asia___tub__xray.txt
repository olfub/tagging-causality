

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

**Understanding the entities:**
- **Tuberculosis** is an infectious lung disease caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*.
- **X-ray** is a diagnostic test/imaging result used to detect lung abnormalities.

**Evaluating Option A: Tuberculosis causes X-ray**
- In medical causal reasoning (e.g., Bayesian networks like the well-known ASIA model), diseases are modeled as **causes** of observable findings. Tuberculosis produces lung abnormalities (e.g., cavities, infiltrates) that **cause** an abnormal X-ray result.
- The presence of disease leads to (causes) the diagnostic observation.

**Evaluating Option B: X-ray causes Tuberculosis**
- An X-ray is a diagnostic tool — it *detects* disease but does not *create* it. Radiation from an X-ray does not cause tuberculosis (which is caused by a bacterial infection).
- This reverses the logical direction of causation.

**Conclusion:**
In the standard causal framework for medical diagnosis, the disease (Tuberculosis) is the **cause**, and the diagnostic finding (X-ray result) is the **effect**.
