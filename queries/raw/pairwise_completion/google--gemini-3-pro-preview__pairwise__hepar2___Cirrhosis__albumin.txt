Here is a step-by-step analysis to determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship between Cirrhosis and albumin:

1.  **Define the terms:**
    *   **Cirrhosis:** A late-stage liver disease characterized by the replacement of healthy liver tissue with scar tissue, leading to a progressive loss of liver function.
    *   **Albumin:** The most abundant protein in human blood plasma, which is exclusively synthesized by the liver. It helps maintain oncotic pressure (keeping fluid in the bloodstream so it doesn't leak into tissues) and transports various substances.

2.  **Analyze the physiological relationship:**
    *   The liver is the "factory" responsible for producing albumin.
    *   When the liver is healthy, it produces sufficient amounts of albumin.

3.  **Analyze the pathological relationship (Disease state):**
    *   In the case of **Cirrhosis**, the liver's functional capacity is severely compromised due to scarring and hepatocyte (liver cell) death.
    *   As liver function declines, its ability to synthesize proteins, including albumin, decreases.
    *   Therefore, patients with cirrhosis typically present with *hypoalbuminemia* (low levels of albumin in the blood).

4.  **Evaluate Option B (albumin causes Cirrhosis):**
    *   An excess or deficiency of albumin itself does not cause liver scarring or cirrhosis. While albumin infusions are used to treat complications of cirrhosis, the level of albumin is a *marker* of liver function, not the *source* of the liver damage.

5.  **Evaluate Option A (Cirrhosis causes albumin [levels to change]):**
    *   Strictly speaking, cirrhosis causes a change in albumin *levels* (specifically, it causes low albumin). In medical causality questions, "Disease X causes Biomarker Y" is often shorthand for "Disease X causes abnormal levels of Biomarker Y."
    *   Because the liver makes albumin, damage to the liver (Cirrhosis) directly results in the altered production of albumin.

6.  **Conclusion:**
    The relationship is that the disease state (Cirrhosis) impairs the organ responsible for creating the protein, thereby influencing the presence and levels of that protein.
