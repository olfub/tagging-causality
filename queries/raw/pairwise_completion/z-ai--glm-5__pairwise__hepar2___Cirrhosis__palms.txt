# Evaluating Causal Relationship Between Cirrhosis and Palms

I need to determine which causal relationship is more medically plausible.

## Step 1: Understanding the terms
- **Cirrhosis**: A chronic liver disease characterized by fibrosis and impaired liver function
- **Palms**: In this medical context, likely referring to "palmar erythema" or "liver palms" - a reddening of the palms

## Step 2: Analyzing the causal direction
### Option A: Cirrhosis causes palms (palmar erythema)
- This is a well-documented medical phenomenon
- Cirrhosis leads to hormonal imbalances (particularly elevated estrogen)
- The liver normally metabolizes hormones, but cirrhosis impairs this function
- Elevated estrogen causes vasodilation in the palms
- This results in the characteristic reddening known as "liver palms"
- This is considered a clinical sign of advanced liver disease

### Option B: Palms causes Cirrhosis
- No established medical mechanism exists for this causal direction
- Palmar erythema is a symptom, not a disease process
- There is no evidence that red palms can cause liver damage or fibrosis
- Palmar erythema can occur in conditions other than liver disease (pregnancy, certain medications)

## Step 3: Conclusion
The medical evidence strongly supports cirrhosis as the cause of palmar erythema, not the reverse.

<Answer>A</Answer>
