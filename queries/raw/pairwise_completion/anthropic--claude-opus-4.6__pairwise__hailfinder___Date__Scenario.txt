

# Analyzing the Cause-and-Effect Relationship

## Step-by-Step Reasoning:

**Step 1: Define the variables in context.**
- **Date**: The calendar date (time of year/season).
- **Scenario**: The weather/hail forecast scenario (e.g., atmospheric setup, storm type, severity).

**Step 2: Consider direction A (Date → Scenario).**
The date determines seasonal and climatological factors such as:
- Solar angle and surface heating
- Jet stream position and strength
- Moisture availability
- Atmospheric instability profiles
- Frequency of frontal systems

These collectively shape which weather **scenarios** are physically possible or likely. For instance, large-hail scenarios are far more common in spring/early summer due to favorable thermodynamic and dynamic environments tied to the time of year.

**Step 3: Consider direction B (Scenario → Date).**
A weather scenario cannot cause a particular date. The calendar date is an exogenous variable — it is determined by time itself and is not influenced by any meteorological process.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**
The date (time of year) constrains and influences which hail-producing scenarios can occur, not the other way around. This is a clear one-way causal relationship.
