**Step‑by‑step reasoning**

1. **What is flatulence?**  
   - Flatulence is the passage of excess gas from the digestive tract (mainly the colon) through the rectum.  
   - It results from bacterial fermentation of undigested carbohydrates, swallowing air, or malabsorption.  
   - The gas is located in the lumen of the large intestine and is not related to the gallbladder or bile.

2. **What are gallstones?**  
   - Gallstones are hardened deposits in the gallbladder, most commonly composed of cholesterol or bilirubin.  
   - They form when there is an imbalance in the composition of bile (e.g., excess cholesterol, insufficient bile acids) or when gallbladder emptying is sluggish (stasis).

3. **Can flatulence cause gallstones?**  
   - There is no known physiological mechanism by which intestinal gas could alter the composition of bile or affect gallbladder motility.  
   - Epidemiologic studies do not show that people with higher flatulence have a higher incidence of gallstones.  
   - Therefore, a causal link from flatulence → gallstones is implausible.

4. **Can gallstones cause flatulence?**  
   - Gallstones can obstruct bile flow, leading to impaired fat digestion and dyspeptic symptoms such as bloating, abdominal discomfort, and increased gas production.  
   - Clinical experience and some studies indicate that patients with gallstone disease often report flatulence and other gas‑related complaints.  
   - While the relationship is not strictly “cause‑and‑effect” in every case, it is biologically plausible and supported by observation.

5. **Conclusion**  
   - The more plausible direction of the relationship is that gallstones can contribute to (or be associated with) flatulence, not the reverse.  

Thus, the cause‑and‑effect relationship that is more likely is **gallstones cause flatulence**.
