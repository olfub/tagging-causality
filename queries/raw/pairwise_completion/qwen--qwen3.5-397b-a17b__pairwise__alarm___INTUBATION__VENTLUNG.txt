To determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship, we must analyze the clinical workflow and temporal sequence of these procedures in an Intensive Care Unit (ICU) setting.

1.  **Define the Terms**:
    *   **INTUBATION**: This is the medical procedure of inserting a tube (endotracheal tube) into a patient's trachea to secure an airway.
    *   **VENTLUNG**: This refers to mechanical ventilation, where a machine breathes for the patient or assists their breathing.

2.  **Analyze the Clinical Sequence**:
    *   Invasive mechanical ventilation requires a secure connection between the ventilator machine and the patient's lungs.
    *   This connection is established through the endotracheal tube placed during **intubation**.
    *   Therefore, the procedure of intubation must physically occur *before* invasive mechanical ventilation can begin.
    *   Clinicians perform intubation *in order to* facilitate mechanical ventilation.

3.  **Evaluate Causality**:
    *   **A. INTUBATION causes VENTLUNG**: This is logically sound. The placement of the tube (cause) enables the delivery of mechanical breaths (effect). Without the tube, the specific mode of invasive ventilation cannot happen.
    *   **B. VENTLUNG causes INTUBATION**: This is logically incorrect. Ventilation does not cause the insertion of the tube. Rather, the need for ventilation leads to the decision to intubate, but the physical act of ventilation relies on the intubation having already taken place.

4.  **Conclusion**: Since intubation is the prerequisite procedure that enables mechanical ventilation to occur, the causal direction flows from Intubation to VENTLUNG.
