

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship Between Amylase and Gallstones

## Step-by-step reasoning:

**Option A: Amylase causes gallstones**
- Amylase is a digestive enzyme (produced by the pancreas and salivary glands) that breaks down starches.
- Gallstones form due to factors like excess cholesterol, bilirubin imbalance, or gallbladder motility issues.
- There is no well-established mechanism by which amylase would lead to gallstone formation.

**Option B: Gallstones causes amylase (elevation)**
- Gallstones can migrate from the gallbladder into the common bile duct.
- Since the common bile duct and pancreatic duct share a common opening at the **ampulla of Vater**, a gallstone can obstruct pancreatic drainage.
- This obstruction can trigger **gallstone pancreatitis**, a well-known clinical condition.
- Pancreatitis leads to the release of pancreatic enzymes, including **elevated serum amylase** — a hallmark diagnostic finding.
- This is a classic and well-established pathophysiological mechanism taught in medicine.

## Conclusion:
Gallstones can directly cause elevated amylase levels through gallstone pancreatitis, making option B the far more plausible causal direction.

<Answer>B</Answer>
