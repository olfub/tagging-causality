**Step 1: Define the Variables**
*   **LVEDVOLUME (Left Ventricular End-Diastolic Volume):** This is the volume of blood in the left ventricle at the end of filling (diastole). It represents the "preload," or the stretch of the heart muscle prior to contraction.
*   **PCWP (Pulmonary Capillary Wedge Pressure):** This is a pressure measurement obtained by inflating a balloon on a Swan-Ganz catheter. It acts as a surrogate for Left Atrial Pressure, which in turn approximates Left Ventricular End-Diastolic Pressure (LVEDP).

**Step 2: Analyze the Physiological Mechanism**
The relationship between volume and pressure inside a compliant container (like the left ventricle) is governed by the concept of **compliance**.
*   **Compliance = Volume / Pressure.**
*   Physiologically, the ventricle fills with blood (Volume). As the volume of blood increases inside the chamber, it exerts force against the ventricular walls, which creates pressure.
*   Therefore, the presence of the volume is the physical driver of the pressure.

**Step 3: Establish Causality**
*   **Option A (LVEDVOLUME causes PCWP):** This aligns with the laws of physics and physiology. An increase in the volume of blood in the left ventricle leads to an increase in the pressure within that chamber. Since PCWP is a measurement that reflects the pressure in the left ventricle (LVEDP), changes in the volume directly cause changes in the pressure reading.
*   **Option B (PCWP causes LVEDVOLUME):** This is physiologically incorrect in the primary sense. While a high pressure in the atrium (upstream) helps drive filling, PCWP specifically represents the pressure *resulting* from the volume status. Pressure does not "create" volume in the ventricle; rather, the volume creates the pressure. Clinically, we use PCWP to *estimate* LVEDVOLUME, but the causal direction flows from the physical volume stretching the chamber to the resulting pressure measurement.

**Conclusion**
The left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVEDV) is the primary determinant of the pressure inside the ventricle. The pressure (approximated by PCWP) is the dependent variable resulting from the volume (the independent variable).
