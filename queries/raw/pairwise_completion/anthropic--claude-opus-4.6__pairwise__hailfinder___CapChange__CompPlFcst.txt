

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship: CapChange vs. CompPlFcst

## Step-by-step reasoning:

1. **Understanding the variables:**
   - **CapChange** refers to the change in the convective cap (CIN/Convective Inhibition) — this is a physical atmospheric parameter describing how the "lid" suppressing thunderstorm development is evolving.
   - **CompPlFcst** refers to a composite or complete placement forecast — this is an integrated forecast product that synthesizes various atmospheric inputs.

2. **Causal direction logic:**
   - Physical atmospheric conditions (like changes in the cap) are **observed/measured inputs** that drive forecast outputs.
   - A forecast is **derived from** atmospheric parameters, not the other way around — a forecast cannot physically alter the convective cap.

3. **Meteorological reasoning:**
   - A weakening cap (CapChange trending negative) increases the likelihood of convective initiation, which directly influences forecasts of severe weather placement and intensity.
   - The composite forecast is **downstream** of atmospheric observations and parameters like CapChange.

4. **General principle:** In causal networks for weather forecasting, physical atmospheric variables are parent nodes (causes), and forecast products are child nodes (effects).

## Conclusion:
CapChange is an atmospheric condition that serves as a causal input to the composite forecast, not the reverse.
