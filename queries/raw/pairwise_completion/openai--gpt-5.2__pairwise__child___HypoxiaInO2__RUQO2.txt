1. **Interpret the variables clinically**
   - **HypoxiaInO2** represents a *physiologic state*: inadequate oxygenation (low arterial oxygen content/saturation) due to a cardiopulmonary/congenital heart mechanism (e.g., right-to-left shunt, low pulmonary blood flow, V/Q mismatch).
   - **RUQO2** (in congenital heart disease contexts often meaning a *right upper extremity/preductal O2 saturation reading*) is a *measurement/observation* of oxygenation at a specific site.

2. **Ask: what generates what?**
   - The patient’s **true oxygenation status (hypoxia)** determines what you will **measure** in arterial oxygen saturation at any site (including right upper extremity/preductal).
   - A *low RUQO2 value* does not itself create hypoxia; it is typically an *indicator* reflecting the underlying hypoxic physiology.

3. **Causal direction in congenital heart disease**
   - In CHD (e.g., ductal-dependent lesions, transposition physiology, pulmonary outflow obstruction), the *underlying mixing/shunting/flow problem* causes **systemic hypoxemia**, which then yields a **low preductal (RUQ) saturation**.
   - The saturation number is downstream of physiology, not upstream.

4. **Conclusion**
   - It is more plausible that **HypoxiaInO2 → RUQO2 (the measured saturation reflects the hypoxia)**, rather than RUQO2 causing hypoxia.
