   - **Grunting**: the infant is actually grunting (a physiologic sign, often reflecting respiratory distress or increased work of breathing).
   - **GruntingReport**: a clinician/caregiver documents or reports that the infant is grunting (an observation/record of the sign).

2. **Direction of information flow**
   - In clinical settings, the **physical sign happens first**, then someone **notices and reports** it.
   - A report/documentation does not typically make the infant start grunting; it’s downstream of the physiologic event.

3. **Causal plausibility**
   - **Grunting → GruntingReport** is a straightforward measurement/observation relationship.
   - **GruntingReport → Grunting** would require the act of reporting to cause the physiologic sign, which is not medically plausible (except in very artificial scenarios like experimental prompting, which is not the clinical context here).

4. **Conclusion**
   - The more likely cause-and-effect relationship is **Grunting causes GruntingReport**.
