

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

**Option A: Cancer causes Smoker**
- This would mean that developing cancer leads a person to start smoking.
- There is no known biological or behavioral mechanism by which having cancer would cause someone to take up smoking.
- This direction is not supported by medical evidence.

**Option B: Smoker causes Cancer**
- Tobacco smoke contains over 70 known carcinogens (e.g., benzene, formaldehyde, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons).
- These chemicals damage DNA in lung cells, leading to mutations that can result in uncontrolled cell growth (cancer).
- Decades of epidemiological research (starting with landmark studies in the 1950s) consistently show that smoking dramatically increases the risk of lung cancer.
- There is a well-established **dose-response relationship**: more smoking → higher cancer risk.
- The temporal order is clear: smoking behavior precedes the development of cancer, often by years or decades.

## Conclusion

The causal pathway is well established: smoking introduces carcinogens into the body, which damage cellular DNA, ultimately leading to cancer.

<Answer>B</Answer>
