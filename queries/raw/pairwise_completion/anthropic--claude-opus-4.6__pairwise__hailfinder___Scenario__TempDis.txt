

# Causal Reasoning: Scenario vs. TempDis

## Step-by-step Analysis

**Step 1: Define the variables in context**
- **Scenario**: In hail forecasting, this typically refers to the overarching weather scenario or synoptic regime (e.g., the large-scale atmospheric setup, storm type, or weather pattern).
- **TempDis**: This likely refers to the temperature distribution (e.g., vertical temperature profile, lapse rates, or temperature anomalies in the atmosphere).

**Step 2: Consider the causal direction**
- The large-scale weather **Scenario** (synoptic pattern, frontal systems, upper-level troughs, etc.) sets up the atmospheric environment, which in turn **determines** local and regional temperature distributions.
- Temperature distributions are an *observable consequence* of the broader atmospheric scenario — not the other way around.

**Step 3: Check for reverse causality**
- While temperature distributions are important diagnostic and prognostic variables, they are *manifestations* of the governing weather scenario, not the fundamental driver of it. The scenario is the higher-level causal structure from which temperature profiles emerge.

**Step 4: Conclusion**
The weather scenario is the parent cause that shapes temperature distributions, making **Scenario → TempDis** the more natural and physically consistent causal direction.
