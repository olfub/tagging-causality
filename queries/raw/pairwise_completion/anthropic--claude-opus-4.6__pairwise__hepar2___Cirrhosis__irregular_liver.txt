

## Step-by-step Reasoning

1. **Understanding Cirrhosis**: Cirrhosis is a chronic liver disease characterized by progressive fibrosis (scarring) and the formation of regenerative nodules in the liver. It results from sustained liver damage (e.g., from alcohol, hepatitis, etc.).

2. **Understanding Irregular Liver**: An irregular liver refers to the physical finding of a nodular, bumpy, or irregular liver surface/margins, typically detected on imaging or physical examination.

3. **Pathophysiology**: In cirrhosis, ongoing liver injury leads to:
   - Destruction of normal hepatic architecture
   - Replacement with fibrous scar tissue
   - Formation of regenerative nodules
   - These structural changes make the liver surface **irregular and nodular**

4. **Causal direction**: The disease process (cirrhosis) is what **produces** the irregular liver morphology. An irregular liver is a **consequence/sign** of cirrhosis, not a precursor to it.

5. **Reverse direction check**: An irregular liver shape does not independently cause cirrhosis. Rather, it is a downstream manifestation of the cirrhotic process.

## Conclusion
Cirrhosis (the pathological process of fibrosis and nodule formation) causes the liver to become irregular. The irregular liver is a physical sign/effect of the underlying disease.
