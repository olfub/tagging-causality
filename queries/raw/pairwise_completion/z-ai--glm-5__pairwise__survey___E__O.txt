# Analyzing the Causal Relationship Between Education and Occupation

## Step 1: Consider Temporal Sequence
In most people's lives, formal education precedes occupation. The typical life course follows a pattern of completing education first, then entering the workforce in a specific occupation.

## Step 2: Examine Mechanisms
- **Education → Occupation**: Education provides knowledge, skills, credentials, and qualifications that directly qualify individuals for specific occupations. Many jobs explicitly require certain educational backgrounds.
- **Occupation → Education**: While one's occupation might influence continuing education decisions, this is typically supplementary to the initial education that qualified the person for that occupation.

## Step 3: Evaluate Empirical Evidence
Labor market research consistently demonstrates that:
- Educational qualifications serve as gatekeepers for many occupations
- People often select educational paths based on occupational aspirations
- Strong correlations exist between education levels and occupational categories

## Step 4: Consider Counterfactuals
If a person had no education or a different type of education, their occupational options would likely be substantially different. This supports education as the causal factor.

## Step 5: Address Alternative Explanations
While some exceptions exist (e.g., employer-sponsored education), these represent secondary effects rather than the primary causal direction.
