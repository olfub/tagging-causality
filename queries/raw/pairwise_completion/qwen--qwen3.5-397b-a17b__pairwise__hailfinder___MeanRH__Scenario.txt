## Step-by-Step Analysis

### Step 1: Understanding the Variables
- **MeanRH**: Mean Relative Humidity - a meteorological measurement of atmospheric moisture content
- **Scenario**: In hail forecasting, this refers to the forecast outcome or classification (e.g., hail event vs. no-hail event)

### Step 2: Physical Causality in Weather Systems
In meteorology, atmospheric conditions are the **drivers** of weather outcomes:
- Relative humidity affects cloud formation, storm development, and precipitation processes
- Higher humidity can contribute to more intense convective storms that may produce hail
- These are physical atmospheric properties that exist independently of forecasts

### Step 3: Temporal Relationship
- **MeanRH** is measured/observed **before or during** storm development
- **Scenario** (hail outcome) is determined **after** atmospheric conditions are established
- Cause must precede effect in time

### Step 4: Direction of Influence
- Atmospheric conditions (humidity, temperature, wind shear, etc.) → Determine weather outcomes
- A forecast scenario cannot physically change the actual atmospheric humidity
- The scenario is a **result** of the atmospheric state, not a cause of it

### Step 5: Conclusion
In hail forecasting, MeanRH is a **predictor variable** that influences the forecast scenario. The causal flow goes from atmospheric measurements to forecast outcomes, not vice versa.
