1. **Interpret the variables**  
   - **Hospital**: being admitted to or receiving care in a hospital setting.  
   - **Transfusion**: receiving blood products (RBCs, platelets, plasma), which is typically a medically supervised procedure.

2. **Typical temporal/clinical ordering**  
   - In most real-world cases, a patient is **in the hospital (or hospital-based care setting)** because of bleeding, anemia, surgery, trauma, etc., and then clinicians decide to **administer a transfusion** based on labs and clinical status.  
   - Transfusions are most commonly delivered **in hospitals** (or in closely related facilities like hospital outpatient infusion units).

3. **Mechanism plausibility**  
   - **Hospital → Transfusion** has a clear mechanism: evaluation and management in hospital leads to interventions, including transfusion when indicated.  
   - **Transfusion → Hospital** is less direct: while transfusion reactions or complications can prompt hospitalization, those are comparatively rarer and also many transfusions already occur in the hospital, so transfusion is often not the initiating cause of “hospital.”

4. **Confounding/common causes**  
   - Conditions like hemorrhage, surgery, trauma, malignancy, or severe anemia can cause **both** hospitalization and transfusion. Even with that, the *care pathway* still usually runs through hospital care enabling/initiating transfusion.

**Conclusion:** It is more likely that being in the hospital (hospital care/admission) leads to receiving a transfusion, rather than transfusion causing hospitalization.
