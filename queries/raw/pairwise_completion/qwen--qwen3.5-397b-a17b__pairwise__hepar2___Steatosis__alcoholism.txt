Here is the step-by-step reasoning to determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship:

1.  **Define the Terms:**
    *   **Steatosis:** Also known as fatty liver, this is a pathological condition characterized by the accumulation of fat (triglycerides) within liver cells (hepatocytes).
    *   **Alcoholism:** Also referred to as Alcohol Use Disorder (AUD), this is a chronic condition characterized by the inability to control or stop alcohol use despite adverse social, occupational, or health consequences.

2.  **Analyze the Pathophysiology of Alcoholism leading to Steatosis:**
    *   When alcohol (ethanol) is consumed, it is primarily metabolized in the liver.
    *   Chronic excessive alcohol consumption alters the liver's metabolic processes. It increases the production of fatty acids, decreases the breakdown of fatty acids, and impairs the export of lipids from the liver.
    *   This metabolic shift directly results in the accumulation of fat within liver cells.
    *   Consequently, alcoholic steatosis is the earliest and most common stage of Alcoholic Liver Disease (ALD).

3.  **Analyze the Pathophysiology of Steatosis leading to Alcoholism:**
    *   Steatosis is a physical condition of the liver. While advanced liver disease can affect brain function (e.g., hepatic encephalopathy), simple steatosis is often asymptomatic.
    *   There is no known biological or psychological mechanism by which the accumulation of fat in the liver induces the behavioral cravings, dependence, or compulsive drinking patterns associated with alcoholism.
    *   Many individuals develop steatosis due to non-alcoholic causes (such as obesity, diabetes, or metabolic syndrome) without ever developing an addiction to alcohol.

4.  **Evaluate Medical Consensus:**
    *   Medical literature extensively documents that chronic heavy drinking is a direct cause of hepatic steatosis.
    *   There is no evidence supporting the reverse hypothesis that having a fatty liver causes a person to become an alcoholic.

5.  **Conclusion:**
    *   The causal direction is clearly from the behavior/substance use (alcoholism) to the physiological outcome (steatosis).

<Answer>B</Answer>
