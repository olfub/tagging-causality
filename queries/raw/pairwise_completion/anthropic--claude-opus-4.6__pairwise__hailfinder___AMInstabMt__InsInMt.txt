

# Analyzing the Cause-and-Effect Relationship

## Step 1: Interpret the Variables
- **AMInstabMt**: This refers to **morning (AM) instability over mountains** — an early atmospheric measurement of instability in mountainous terrain.
- **InsInMt**: This refers to **instability in the mountains** — a measure of the actual or resulting instability present in mountainous areas.

## Step 2: Consider Temporal and Physical Reasoning
- Morning atmospheric conditions (temperature profiles, moisture, lapse rates) measured over mountains serve as **precursors** that set up the atmospheric environment.
- The instability that develops in the mountains later is a **consequence** of those earlier (morning) conditions.
- Temporally, AM (morning) measurements come first and influence later instability development.

## Step 3: Determine Causal Direction
- Morning instability (AMInstabMt) is an **upstream/antecedent** condition.
- Instability in the mountains (InsInMt) is a **downstream/resultant** condition that develops partly because of those morning instability characteristics.
- Therefore, **AMInstabMt causes InsInMt** is the physically and temporally consistent direction.
