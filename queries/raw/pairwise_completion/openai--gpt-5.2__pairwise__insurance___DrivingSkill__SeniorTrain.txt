1. **Interpret the variables**
   - **SeniorTrain**: participation in a senior driver training course/program.
   - **DrivingSkill**: underlying driving competence (hazard perception, reaction, rule compliance, etc.).

2. **Check temporal/structural plausibility**
   - Training is an *intervention* that occurs at a specific time and can change behavior/knowledge afterward.
   - “DrivingSkill causes SeniorTrain” would mean skill level is a primary driver of enrolling in training.

3. **Assess the more direct causal mechanism**
   - It’s straightforward that **training can improve skill** via instruction, refreshed rules, practice, and awareness of age-related limitations.
   - That is a classic **treatment → outcome** relationship.

4. **Consider selection effects (reverse direction)**
   - It’s also plausible that drivers with **lower perceived skill** (or concerns after an incident) are more likely to seek training.
   - However, that’s typically modeled as **confounding/selection into treatment** (e.g., risk perception, prior crash, physician/family advice), not as the primary causal meaning of the variables.

5. **Most likely causal direction for risk modeling**
   - For a cause-and-effect relationship between these two, the more policy/actionable and mechanistically supported link is:
     - **SeniorTrain → DrivingSkill** (training affects skill).
   - Any tendency for low skill to increase training enrollment is better treated as a separate driver of both (or as selection) rather than the main causal arrow.
