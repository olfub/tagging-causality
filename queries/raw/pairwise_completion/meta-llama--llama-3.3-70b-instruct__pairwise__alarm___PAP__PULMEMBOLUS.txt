1. **Understanding PAP and PULMEMBOLUS**:
   - **PAP (Pulmonary Artery Pressure)**: This refers to the blood pressure in the pulmonary artery, which carries deoxygenated blood from the heart to the lungs. Elevated PAP can indicate various conditions, including pulmonary hypertension, which can be due to several causes including chronic diseases, heart disease, or blockages in the lungs' blood vessels.
   - **PULMEMBOLUS (Pulmonary Embolus)**: This is a blockage of an artery in the lungs. The most common cause of pulmonary embolism is a blood clot that travels to the lungs from another part of the body, often the legs. This condition can significantly increase the resistance against which the right ventricle must pump blood, potentially increasing pulmonary artery pressure.

2. **Analyzing the Relationship**:
   - **Option A: PAP causes PULMEMBOLUS**: This implies that high pulmonary artery pressure leads to the formation or occurrence of a pulmonary embolus. While high PAP can indicate and result from various pulmonary and cardiac issues, it is not a direct cause of a pulmonary embolus. Pulmonary embolism typically results from a blood clot or other material (like fat or air) that travels to the lungs and blocks an artery, rather than being a direct consequence of elevated PAP.
   - **Option B: PULMEMBOLUS causes PAP**: This option suggests that a pulmonary embolus leads to increased pulmonary artery pressure. This relationship is more direct and physiologically plausible. A pulmonary embolus can block or restrict blood flow through the pulmonary arteries, increasing the resistance against which the right ventricle of the heart must pump blood. This increased resistance can lead to elevated pulmonary artery pressure (PAP) as the heart works harder to overcome the obstruction.

3. **Conclusion**:
   Given the physiological and pathological mechanisms, the most likely cause-and-effect relationship is that a pulmonary embolus (blockage of an artery in the lungs) can cause an increase in pulmonary artery pressure due to the increased resistance to blood flow in the lungs.
