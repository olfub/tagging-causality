1. **Define the entities**
   - **ChHepatitis (chronic hepatitis)**: persistent hepatic inflammation, commonly due to viral infection (HBV/HCV), autoimmune disease, alcohol, NAFLD/NASH, etc.
   - **Fibrosis (liver fibrosis)**: accumulation of extracellular matrix/scar tissue in the liver, reflecting wound-healing responses to ongoing injury/inflammation.

2. **Mechanistic directionality**
   - Chronic hepatitis involves **ongoing hepatocyte injury and inflammation**.
   - In response, **hepatic stellate cells activate** (via cytokines like TGF-β, PDGF), producing collagen and other matrix components → **fibrosis**.
   - This is the classic progression: **chronic inflammation → fibrosis → cirrhosis**.

3. **Clinical/epidemiologic pattern**
   - In chronic viral hepatitis (especially HCV, HBV) and autoimmune hepatitis, the **severity and duration of hepatitis** correlate with **fibrosis stage** over time.
   - Treatments that reduce hepatitis activity (e.g., viral suppression/eradication) often **stabilize or regress fibrosis**, consistent with hepatitis being upstream.

4. **Check the reverse claim**
   - Fibrosis is largely a **structural outcome** of chronic injury; it does not typically *initiate* chronic hepatitis.
   - While advanced fibrosis/cirrhosis can worsen liver function and portal pressures, it is not a primary cause of chronic inflammatory hepatitis.
