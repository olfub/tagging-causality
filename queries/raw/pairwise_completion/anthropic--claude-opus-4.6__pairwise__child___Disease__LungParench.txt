

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship Between Disease and LungParench

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

**Step 1: Understanding the variables**
- **Disease** refers to the underlying congenital heart disease (e.g., VSD, Tetralogy of Fallot, TGA, etc.)
- **LungParench** refers to the lung parenchymal findings (e.g., normal, congested/plethoric, oligemic)

**Step 2: Medical reasoning**
- Congenital heart diseases directly affect pulmonary hemodynamics:
  - **Left-to-right shunts** (e.g., VSD, ASD) → increased pulmonary blood flow → **congested/plethoric** lung parenchyma
  - **Right-to-left shunts** (e.g., Tetralogy of Fallot) → decreased pulmonary blood flow → **oligemic** lung fields
  - **Normal heart** → **normal** lung parenchyma

**Step 3: Directionality check**
- The underlying cardiac pathology is a **structural/congenital** condition that exists from birth and drives downstream physiological changes, including how the lungs appear.
- Lung parenchymal findings are an **observable consequence** (effect) of the disease, not the origin of it.
- Lung parenchymal appearance does not cause the congenital heart defect — it is a manifestation of it.

**Step 4: Consistency with established models**
- This aligns with the well-known **CHILD Bayesian network** for congenital heart disease diagnosis, where **Disease → LungParench** is the established causal direction.

## Conclusion
The underlying congenital heart disease determines the lung parenchymal appearance, not the reverse.
