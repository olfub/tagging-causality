1. **Interpret the variables clinically:**  
   - **Cirrhosis** is end-stage chronic liver disease with fibrosis and loss of functional hepatocyte mass.  
   - **“Proteins”** in a medical context usually refers to **serum proteins** (especially **albumin** and clotting factors), i.e., proteins the liver synthesizes.

2. **Check biological plausibility (mechanism):**  
   - The liver is the primary site of synthesis for **albumin** and many other plasma proteins.  
   - **Cirrhosis impairs hepatic synthetic function**, typically causing **decreased** albumin and other proteins (and altered protein patterns).  
   - So cirrhosis clearly **drives changes in protein levels** (even if the phrasing “causes proteins” is imprecise, the direction “cirrhosis → protein abnormalities” is medically standard).

3. **Evaluate the reverse direction:**  
   - **Proteins causing cirrhosis** is not a typical causal pathway. High-protein diet does not generally cause cirrhosis; common causes are alcohol, viral hepatitis, NASH, autoimmune/cholestatic diseases, etc.  
   - While certain metabolic disorders involve protein-related phenomena, “proteins” broadly is not a recognized primary cause of cirrhosis.

4. **Choose the more likely causal direction:**  
   - **Cirrhosis → (changes in) proteins** is far more likely than **proteins → cirrhosis**.
