**Step‑by‑step reasoning**

1. **What is bilirubin?**  
   - Bilirubin is a yellow pigment produced when red blood cells are broken down. The liver normally processes bilirubin and excretes it in bile.

2. **What does bilirubin do to the skin?**  
   - When bilirubin builds up in the blood (e.g., due to liver disease, hemolysis, or biliary obstruction), it deposits in the skin and mucous membranes, giving them a yellow‑tinged appearance—this is known as jaundice.

3. **Can the skin produce bilirubin?**  
   - The skin does not synthesize bilirubin. It is a byproduct of heme catabolism, primarily generated in the spleen and liver.

4. **Which direction of causality is medically plausible?**  
   - Elevated bilirubin **causes** a visible change (yellowing) in the skin. The reverse (skin causing bilirubin production) is not supported by physiology.

Therefore, the more likely cause‑and‑effect relationship is **bilirubin → skin**.
