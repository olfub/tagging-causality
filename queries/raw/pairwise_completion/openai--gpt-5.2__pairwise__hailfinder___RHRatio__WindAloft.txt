1. **Interpret the variables**
   - **WindAloft**: wind speed/direction in the mid/upper levels (e.g., 850–500 mb), set primarily by pressure-gradient forces and the large-scale flow (jet stream, troughs/ridges).
   - **RHRatio**: a moisture metric related to relative humidity (or a ratio derived from RH at one or more levels), controlled by temperature, moisture advection, vertical motion, and mixing.

2. **Check physical directionality**
   - Winds aloft can **transport moisture** (advection) and influence **mixing and subsidence/ascent**, all of which change temperature/dewpoint profiles and thus **relative humidity**.
   - Relative humidity (or a derived RH ratio) generally does **not** create the large-scale pressure-gradient structure needed to *cause* winds aloft. Moisture can affect buoyancy/latent heating in storms, but that’s a more indirect, conditional pathway and not the primary driver of the ambient wind field.

3. **In forecasting terms**
   - In hail environments, wind profiles (shear/flow aloft) often act as an **upstream control** on moisture transport and storm organization, while RH metrics respond to that transport and the associated vertical motions.
