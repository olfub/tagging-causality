**Step 1: Define the terms.**
*   **Cirrhosis:** This is a late stage of scarring (fibrosis) of the liver caused by many forms of liver diseases and conditions, such as hepatitis and chronic alcoholism. It represents a specific pathological process where healthy liver tissue is replaced by scar tissue and regenerative nodules.
*   **Irregular liver (Irregular liver margin/surface):** This describes the morphological appearance of the liver, typically observed via physical palpation or medical imaging (like ultrasound, CT, or MRI). A healthy liver is smooth. An "irregular" liver refers to a nodular, bumpy, or uneven surface texture.

**Step 2: Analyze the physiological progression.**
*   When the liver is damaged (e.g., by alcohol or viruses), it attempts to repair itself.
*   In the process of cirrhosis, this repair leads to the formation of scar tissue (fibrosis).
*   As the scarring progresses, the liver architecture becomes distorted. The liver tissue creates regenerative nodules—clumps of cells attempting to function amidst the scar tissue.
*   These nodules push against the liver capsule (the outer lining), transforming the normally smooth surface into a bumpy, nodular, or "irregular" surface.

**Step 3: Evaluate the direction of causality.**
*   **Does an irregular liver cause cirrhosis?** No. "Irregular liver" is a description of a physical state or appearance. Having a bumpy liver doesn't *cause* the disease process; rather, the bumpiness is the *result* of the structural changes occurring within the tissue.
*   **Does cirrhosis cause an irregular liver?** Yes. The pathological process of cirrhosis (fibrosis + regenerating nodules) structurally alters the organ, resulting in the clinical sign of an irregular liver surface.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**
Cirrhosis is the underlying disease process. An irregular liver surface is a morphological sign or observation resulting from that disease process. Therefore, the disease (Cirrhosis) causes the physical manifestation (irregular_liver).
