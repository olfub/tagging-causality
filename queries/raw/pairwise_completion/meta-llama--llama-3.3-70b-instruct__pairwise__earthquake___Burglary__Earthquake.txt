To determine the more likely cause-and-effect relationship between "Burglary" and "Earthquake," let's break down the possibilities step by step.

1. **Understanding Earthquakes**: Earthquakes are natural disasters caused by the movement of tectonic plates in the Earth's crust. These movements can lead to the release of significant amounts of energy, resulting in tremors that can cause damage to structures on the Earth's surface.

2. **Understanding Burglary**: Burglary refers to the act of breaking and entering into a structure with the intent to commit a crime, typically theft. It is a deliberate action carried out by individuals.

3. **Analyzing A. Burglary causes Earthquake**: For burglary to cause an earthquake, the act of breaking into a building or any human action related to burglary would need to have the capability to move tectonic plates or disrupt the Earth's crust to the extent of triggering an earthquake. Given the scale and forces involved in both phenomena, it is highly improbable that a burglary could generate the immense energy required to cause an earthquake.

4. **Analyzing B. Earthquake causes Burglary**: While earthquakes are not direct causes of burglaries in the sense that the seismic activity itself prompts individuals to commit crimes, the aftermath of an earthquake can create conditions that might encourage looting or burglary. For example, the destruction caused by an earthquake can leave buildings unsecured and valuables exposed, potentially enticing opportunistic theft. Additionally, the disruption of normal social and law enforcement structures can provide an environment where criminal activities, including burglary, might increase.

5. **Conclusion**: Based on the analysis, the relationship where an earthquake might indirectly influence the occurrence of burglaries due to the conditions it creates (e.g., destruction, chaos, and increased vulnerability) is more plausible than the idea that a burglary could cause an earthquake.

<Answer>B</Answer>
