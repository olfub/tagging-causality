1. **Define the candidate causal directions**
   - **A:** Cirrhosis → alcohol use  
   - **B:** Alcohol use → cirrhosis

2. **Check biological plausibility / mechanism**
   - Chronic heavy **alcohol consumption** can directly injure hepatocytes, promote inflammation, and lead to fibrosis and ultimately **cirrhosis** (well-established pathophysiology of alcohol-associated liver disease).
   - **Cirrhosis** itself does not biologically cause alcohol to exist or be consumed; while illness can influence behavior (e.g., stress-related drinking), that is not a direct, general causal relationship and certainly not the primary/typical direction.

3. **Check clinical/epidemiologic evidence**
   - Alcohol is a major, common etiologic factor for cirrhosis across populations.
   - The reverse (cirrhosis causing alcohol use) is not supported as a standard causal claim and would at most be a confounded/secondary behavioral association in some individuals, not the dominant causal pathway.

4. **Conclusion**
   - The more likely causal direction is **alcohol → cirrhosis**.

<Answer>B</Answer>
