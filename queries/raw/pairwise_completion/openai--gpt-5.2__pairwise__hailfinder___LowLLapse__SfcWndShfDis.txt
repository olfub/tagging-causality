1. **Interpret the variables**
   - **LowLLapse**: low-level lapse rate (how quickly temperature decreases with height in the lowest part of the atmosphere). This is a **thermodynamic stability** measure, strongly influenced by surface heating/cooling, low-level advection, and boundary-layer mixing.
   - **SfcWndShfDis**: surface wind shift/discontinuity (a **kinematic boundary** such as a фронт, dryline, outflow boundary, or other convergence line where wind direction/speed changes abruptly).

2. **Ask which one is more “upstream” physically**
   - A **surface wind shift/discontinuity** typically reflects a boundary between different air masses or the presence of an outflow/front. That boundary often brings **temperature and moisture advection** and/or **cold pool air**, which directly alters the **near-surface temperature profile** and thus the **low-level lapse rate** (often making it more stable behind the boundary, sometimes steeper ahead of it depending on heating/mixing).
   - By contrast, a **low-level lapse rate** on its own does not usually *create* a sharp surface wind shift/discontinuity. Stability can influence boundary-layer mixing and the expression of winds at the surface, but it’s not the primary generator of a discrete wind-shift boundary; those are driven by dynamics/advection/outflows.

3. **Causal direction most consistent with meteorology**
   - Boundaries (wind shifts/discontinuities) → change low-level thermal structure/stability → change low-level lapse rate.
   - The reverse is possible in indirect/feedback ways, but is generally less direct and less likely as the primary causal arrow.
