1. **Physiology of albumin:** Albumin is the main contributor to **plasma oncotic (colloid osmotic) pressure**, which helps keep fluid inside the intravascular space.

2. **Mechanism leading to edema:** When **albumin is low (hypoalbuminemia)**—as in nephrotic syndrome (urinary protein loss), cirrhosis (reduced synthesis), or severe malnutrition—**oncotic pressure falls**, fluid shifts out of capillaries into interstitial tissue, and **edema develops**.

3. **Reverse direction check:** **Edema itself does not generate albumin.** While severe fluid overload/edema can be associated with **hemodilution** (lower measured concentrations), edema is generally a **consequence** of processes that also affect albumin (loss, decreased production), not a cause producing albumin.

4. **Conclusion:** The clinically typical and mechanistically supported direction is **(low) albumin → edema**, not edema → albumin.
