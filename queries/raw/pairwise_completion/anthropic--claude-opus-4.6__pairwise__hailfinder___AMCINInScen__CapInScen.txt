

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship Between AMCINInScen and CapInScen

## Definitions
- **AMCINInScen**: Morning (AM) Convective Inhibition (CIN) in the scenario — a thermodynamic measure from morning soundings reflecting the negative buoyancy energy parcels must overcome.
- **CapInScen**: Cap (capping inversion) strength in the scenario — the degree to which a warm layer aloft suppresses convective development during the forecast period.

## Step-by-step reasoning

1. **Temporal ordering**: Morning CIN is measured/assessed earlier in the day (from AM soundings), while the cap strength in the scenario refers to conditions during the convective forecast period (typically afternoon). The earlier state naturally influences the later state.

2. **Physical mechanism**: The morning CIN reflects the initial atmospheric stability profile. As daytime heating progresses, this morning CIN determines how much the cap will erode. A high morning CIN means the cap is more likely to remain strong; low morning CIN suggests the cap may break more easily.

3. **Forecasting logic**: In hail forecasting Bayesian networks, morning CIN is treated as an upstream (causal/input) variable that determines whether the cap will weaken enough for convective initiation — making it a cause of the cap strength in the scenario.

4. **Directionality**: The cap strength in the scenario is a *consequence* of the initial CIN conditions measured in the morning, not the other way around.

## Conclusion
Morning CIN (AMCINInScen) serves as a precursor that causally influences the cap strength (CapInScen) in the forecast scenario.
