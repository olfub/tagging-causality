## Step-by-Step Analysis

### 1. Understanding the Conditions

**Hyperbilirubinemia**: Elevated bilirubin levels in the blood, indicating impaired bilirubin metabolism or excretion.

**Hepatomegaly**: Enlargement of the liver, typically due to inflammation, congestion, infiltration, or other pathological processes.

### 2. Pathophysiological Relationship

- **Bilirubin metabolism**: Bilirubin is processed by healthy liver cells (hepatocytes), conjugated, and excreted in bile
- **Liver pathology**: When the liver is diseased or damaged, it often becomes enlarged (hepatomegaly) AND loses its ability to properly process bilirubin

### 3. Causal Direction Analysis

**Option A (Hyperbilirubinemia → Hepatomegaly)**:
- Elevated bilirubin itself does not cause liver enlargement
- Hyperbilirubinemia is a biochemical marker, not a structural cause
- This direction is not supported by pathophysiology

**Option B (Hepatomegaly → Hyperbilirubinemia)**:
- Liver enlargement reflects underlying liver pathology (inflammation, congestion, infiltration)
- These same pathological changes impair hepatocyte function
- Impaired liver function leads to reduced bilirubin processing → hyperbilirubinemia
- This follows the clinical disease progression

### 4. Clinical Evidence

In practice:
- Liver diseases (hepatitis, cirrhosis, congestive hepatopathy) cause hepatomegaly first
- As liver dysfunction progresses, bilirubin metabolism becomes impaired
- Hyperbilirubinemia is considered a **consequence** of liver dysfunction, not a cause

### 5. Important Caveat

Both conditions are typically caused by an underlying liver pathology. However, in the causal chain, the structural/functional liver changes (manifesting as hepatomegaly) precede the metabolic consequence (hyperbilirubinemia).

<Answer>B</Answer>
