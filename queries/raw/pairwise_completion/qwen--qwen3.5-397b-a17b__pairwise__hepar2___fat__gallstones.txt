Here is the step-by-step analysis to determine the more likely cause-and-effect relationship:

**Step 1: Define the variables in a medical context.**
*   **"Fat":** In medical epidemiology regarding gallstones, this typically refers to obesity (excess body adiposity) or, secondarily, a high dietary fat intake.
*   **"Gallstones":** This refers to cholelithiasis, the formation of hardened deposits (calculi) in the gallbladder, most commonly composed of cholesterol.

**Step 2: Evaluate the relationship "Fat causes gallstones" (Option A).**
*   **Epidemiological Evidence:** Obesity is one of the classic risk factors for gallstone disease, historically summarized by the "4 Fs" (Female, Fat, Forty, Fertile).
*   **Physiological Mechanism:** Excess body fat increases the secretion of cholesterol into the bile. When bile becomes supersaturated with cholesterol, it can crystallize and form stones. Additionally, rapid mobilization of fat (during rapid weight loss) can also increase biliary cholesterol secretion, further supporting the link between adipose tissue metabolism and stone formation.
*   **Conclusion for A:** There is a strong, well-documented causal link where excess fat (obesity) contributes to the development of gallstones.

**Step 3: Evaluate the relationship "Gallstones causes fat" (Option B).**
*   **Physiological Mechanism:** There is no known biological mechanism by which the presence of calculi in the gallbladder regulates metabolism to induce adipogenesis (the creation of fat cells) or weight gain.
*   **Clinical Observation:** Patients with symptomatic gallstones often experience biliary colic (pain) after eating, particularly fatty foods. This frequently leads to food avoidance, nausea, and unintended weight loss rather than weight gain.
*   **Conclusion for B:** There is no evidence to support the claim that gallstones cause the accumulation of body fat.

**Step 4: Compare the likelihood.**
*   Option A is supported by extensive clinical data and pathophysiological understanding.
*   Option B is physiologically implausible and contradicts typical clinical presentations.

**Conclusion:**
The medical consensus confirms that obesity (fat) is a risk factor for the formation of gallstones, whereas gallstones do not cause obesity.
