To determine the most likely cause-and-effect relationship between Steatosis and AST (Aspartate Aminotransferase), let's break down the definitions and pathophysiological mechanisms of these terms in a medical context.

**Step 1: Define the terms**
*   **Steatosis:** This refers to the abnormal retention of fat (lipids) within a cell or organ. It is most commonly associated with the liver (hepatic steatosis or fatty liver disease).
*   **AST (Aspartate Aminotransferase):** This is an enzyme found in high concentrations in the liver, heart, and muscles. When liver cells are damaged or injured, they release AST into the bloodstream. Therefore, elevated AST levels in the blood are a *marker* or *symptom* of liver injury.

**Step 2: Analyze the mechanism for Option A (Steatosis causes AST elevation)**
*   In conditions like Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD) or Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease, the accumulation of fat (steatosis) within the liver cells (hepatocytes) can induce cellular stress.
*   This fat accumulation can lead to lipotoxicity, oxidative stress, and inflammation (a progression from simple steatosis to steatohepatitis).
*   This cellular stress and damage cause the hepatocytes to retain membrane integrity poorly or die (apoptosis/necrosis), causing them to leak intracellular enzymes, including AST, into the blood.
*   Therefore, the physical presence of steatosis and the subsequent cellular damage directly lead to increased levels of AST.

**Step 3: Analyze the mechanism for Option B (AST causes Steatosis)**
*   AST is an enzyme involved in amino acid metabolism.
*   Its presence in the blood is a result of leakage from damaged cells.
*   There is no physiological mechanism by which the enzyme AST itself creates fat deposits in the liver. AST is a byproduct or sign of the damage, not the generator of the fat accumulation.

**Step 4: Compare the causality**
*   Steatosis is the underlying pathological condition (the disease state).
*   Elevated AST is a clinical manifestation or biomarker resulting from that condition (the effect).
*   Therefore, the accumulation of fat (Steatosis) leads to liver cell injury, which causes the release of AST.

**Conclusion**
The presence of hepatic steatosis leads to hepatocellular injury, which results in the release of enzymes like AST into the bloodstream. AST does not cause the fat to accumulate; rather, it is a signal that the fat (and associated inflammation) is damaging the liver.
