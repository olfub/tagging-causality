When ionizing radiation passes through living tissue, it has enough energy to remove electrons from atoms, which can damage DNA within cells. If this DNA damage is not repaired correctly, it can lead to mutations that result in uncontrolled cell growth, known as cancer. Medical science establishes that significant exposure to ionizing radiation is a known risk factor for developing cancer.

4.  **Compare the likelihood:** Option A describes a biological impossibility (tissue emitting X-rays). Option B describes a well-documented physiological mechanism (radiation damaging DNA leading to carcinogenesis). While the risk from a single diagnostic X-ray is low, the causal direction is scientifically valid, whereas the reverse is not.
