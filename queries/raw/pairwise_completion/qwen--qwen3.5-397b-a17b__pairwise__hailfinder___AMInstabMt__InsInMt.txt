# Step-by-Step Causal Reasoning

## Step 1: Understand the Variables

In hail forecasting contexts:
- **AMInstabMt** = Morning/AM Atmospheric Instability in Mountains (a physical atmospheric condition)
- **InsInMt** = Instability Index in Mountains (a calculated/derived measurement)

## Step 2: Consider the Temporal Order

- Atmospheric instability conditions (AMInstabMt) exist as physical phenomena in the atmosphere
- Instability indices (InsInMt) are calculated metrics derived from observed atmospheric conditions
- Physical conditions must exist before they can be measured and converted into indices

## Step 3: Consider the Logical Flow in Meteorology

In weather forecasting:
1. Raw atmospheric observations are collected first (temperature, humidity, pressure profiles)
2. These observations determine the actual instability state (AMInstabMt)
3. Instability indices (InsInMt) are then computed from these observations
4. Indices are used to predict severe weather outcomes (like hail)

## Step 4: Evaluate Causal Direction

- **Option A**: Physical instability conditions → Instability Index (makes logical sense)
- **Option B**: Instability Index → Physical instability conditions (doesn't make sense - indices don't create physical conditions)

## Step 5: Conclusion

The morning atmospheric instability (AMInstabMt) is the underlying physical condition that determines what the instability index (InsInMt) will be. The index is a measurement/derivation of the actual condition, not a cause of it.
