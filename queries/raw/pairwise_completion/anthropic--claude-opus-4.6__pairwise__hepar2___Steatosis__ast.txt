

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship Between Steatosis and AST

## Step-by-step Reasoning:

**Step 1: Define the terms**
- **Steatosis** (hepatic steatosis) = fatty liver disease, the accumulation of fat within liver cells (hepatocytes).
- **AST** (Aspartate Aminotransferase) = a liver enzyme measured in blood; elevated levels indicate liver cell damage.

**Step 2: Consider Direction A (Steatosis → AST)**
- Steatosis causes inflammation and damage to hepatocytes (liver cells).
- When hepatocytes are damaged, intracellular enzymes like AST leak into the bloodstream.
- This is a well-established pathophysiological mechanism: fatty infiltration → hepatocyte injury → enzyme release → elevated AST.

**Step 3: Consider Direction B (AST → Steatosis)**
- AST is merely an enzyme released as a **biomarker** of liver damage.
- Elevated AST does not cause fat to accumulate in liver cells.
- AST is a *consequence*, not a *cause*, of hepatic pathology.

**Step 4: Conclusion**
The causal pathway is clear: steatosis (fatty liver) leads to hepatocellular injury, which results in elevated AST levels. AST is a downstream indicator, not an upstream cause.
