1. **What each variable represents**
   - **LVEDVOLUME (LVEDV)**: the amount of blood in the left ventricle at end-diastole (a *volume/preload* state).
   - **PCWP**: a *pressure* measured by wedging a pulmonary artery catheter; it estimates **left atrial pressure**, which (when there’s no significant mitral valve disease/obstruction) approximates **LV end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP)**.

2. **Physiologic linkage**
   - For the left ventricle, **end-diastolic pressure is largely a function of end-diastolic volume and ventricular compliance**:
     - Conceptually: **LVEDP = f(LVEDV, compliance)**.
   - PCWP tracks **LA pressure → LV filling pressure (LVEDP)**, so it tracks that pressure consequence.

3. **Direction of causality**
   - If **LVEDV increases** (with compliance held constant), the ventricle is more filled/stretched at end-diastole, which **raises LVEDP**, which **raises LA pressure**, which **raises PCWP**.
   - The reverse (“PCWP causes LVEDV”) is less direct: PCWP is mainly a *measurement/surrogate of downstream filling pressure*, not an upstream driver of how much blood returns to and fills the LV. Pressure can influence filling dynamics, but **the primary causal chain is volume/compliance → pressure**, not pressure → volume.

4. **ICU caveat (doesn’t change the main direction)**
   - PCWP can be misleading when compliance changes (e.g., stiff LV): you can have **high PCWP with relatively low LVEDV**. That still reflects that **pressure depends on both volume and compliance**, not that pressure is the primary cause of volume.
