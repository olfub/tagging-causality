

# Analyzing the Causal Relationship Between HBsAg and Anti-HBc

## Step-by-step reasoning:

**Step 1: Identify the markers**
- **HBsAg** (Hepatitis B surface antigen): A protein on the surface of the Hepatitis B virus. Its presence indicates active HBV infection (acute or chronic).
- **Anti-HBc** (Hepatitis B core antibody): An antibody produced by the immune system in response to the hepatitis B core antigen during infection.

**Step 2: Understand the temporal sequence of HBV infection**
1. HBV enters the body and begins replicating.
2. **HBsAg appears first** in the blood — it is the earliest detectable serological marker of infection.
3. The immune system then mounts a response, producing **anti-HBc antibodies** shortly after.

**Step 3: Determine causal direction**
- HBsAg reflects the presence of the virus (cause/exposure).
- Anti-HBc is the immune system's *response* to the infection (effect/consequence).
- The viral infection (marked by HBsAg) **triggers** the immune response that produces anti-HBc — not the other way around.

**Step 4: Rule out reverse causation**
- Anti-HBc is an antibody; it does not cause the virus to produce HBsAg. It would make no biological sense for an immune response to cause the viral antigen.

## Conclusion
The presence of the virus (indicated by HBsAg) causes the immune system to produce anti-HBc, not the reverse.
