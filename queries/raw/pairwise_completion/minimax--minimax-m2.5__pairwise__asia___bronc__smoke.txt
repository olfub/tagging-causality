**Step‑by‑step reasoning**

1. **What is bronchitis?**  
   Bronchitis is inflammation of the bronchial tubes (the airways that carry air to the lungs). It can be acute (often from infections) or chronic (long‑standing irritation).

2. **What does smoking introduce?**  
   Cigarette smoke contains many irritants (tar, nicotine, carbon monoxide, free radicals, etc.). When inhaled, these substances directly irritate and damage the lining of the bronchial tubes.

3. **Evidence from epidemiology and clinical studies**  
   - Large cohort studies consistently show that smokers have a markedly higher prevalence of chronic bronchitis than non‑smokers.  
   - The risk of developing bronchitis rises with the amount and duration of smoking.  
   - Smoking cessation leads to a reduction in bronchitis symptoms and a slower decline in lung function.

4. **Plausibility of reverse causation**  
   - Having bronchitis does not logically lead a person to start smoking. In fact, individuals diagnosed with respiratory disease are often advised to quit smoking, and many do so.  
   - There is no known biological mechanism by which bronchial inflammation would cause someone to begin using tobacco.

5. **Conclusion**  
   The causal direction supported by biological plausibility, observational data, and clinical guidelines is that smoking is a cause, and bronchitis is an effect.

**Therefore, the more likely cause‑and‑effect relationship is:**  
Smoking → Bronchitis.
