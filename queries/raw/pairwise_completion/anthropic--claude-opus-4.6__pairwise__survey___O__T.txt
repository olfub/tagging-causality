

## Step-by-Step Analysis

**Option A: Occupation causes Travel**
- A person's occupation directly determines work-related travel needs (e.g., consultants, pilots, sales representatives travel frequently).
- Occupation determines income level, which in turn affects one's ability and frequency of leisure travel.
- Certain professions (e.g., diplomats, journalists, flight crew) inherently require extensive travel.
- Work schedules dictated by occupation influence when and how often someone can travel.

**Option B: Travel causes Occupation**
- While travel experiences *could* inspire someone to pursue a travel-related career (e.g., travel blogger, tour guide), this is a relatively rare and indirect pathway.
- Travel alone does not broadly determine what occupation someone holds.

## Conclusion

In demographic modeling, occupation is typically treated as a more fundamental, upstream variable that shapes downstream behaviors like travel patterns. The causal influence from occupation to travel is both more direct and more universal than the reverse.
