To determine which cause-and-effect relationship is more likely between Age and Education, let's analyze the direction of causality and the temporal constraints.

**Step 1: Analyze Relationship B (Education causes Age)**
*   **Definition:** Age is the length of time a person has lived. Education is the process of facilitating learning or the acquisition of knowledge, skills, values, and beliefs.
*   **Mechanism Check:** Does acquiring more education make a person physically older or younger in a chronological sense?
*   **Result:** No. Time passes regardless of whether one is in school or not. While education can affect *life expectancy* (how *old* someone eventually gets before dying), it does not cause the passage of time itself. A degree does not grant someone extra years of age immediately upon receipt. Chronological age is purely a function of time elapsed since birth.

**Step 2: Analyze Relationship A (Age causes Education)**
*   **Definition:** As previously defined, Age is time lived; Education is acquired knowledge/schooling.
*   **Mechanism Check:** Does getting older allow or cause someone to acquire more education?
*   **Temporal Constraint:** Education takes time. One cannot complete high school, college, or graduate school without the passage of time (aging). Therefore, increased age is a necessary condition for achieving higher levels of education.
*   **Social/Biological Constraint:** Human development dictates that certain cognitive abilities required for education develop as we age. Furthermore, societal structures (primary school, secondary school) are organized by age cohorts. One generally accumulates more years of education *as* they age.
*   **Result:** Yes. While age doesn't force everyone to get educated (one can be old and uneducated), age is the prerequisite vehicle that allows the accumulation of education. Younger people are physically incapable of having achieved the same duration of education as older adults simply because they haven't lived long enough to attend the classes.

**Step 3: Compare Probabilities**
*   Relationship B reverses the flow of time (implying an action changes how much time has passed since birth). This is physically impossible.
*   Relationship A aligns with the flow of time (accumulation of years allows for the accumulation of schooling).

**Conclusion**
Education accumulation is a function of time; therefore, age is a causal factor in the potential amount of education one possesses.
