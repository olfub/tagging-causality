1. **Define the terms in this clinical context**
   - **Disease**: a congenital heart defect (e.g., transposition of the great arteries, truncus arteriosus, TAPVR, single-ventricle physiology) or other pathology.
   - **CardiacMixing**: a **physiologic state** where oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mix within the heart or great vessels.

2. **Typical causal direction in congenital heart disease**
   - Structural cardiac abnormalities (the **disease**) change normal anatomy and blood flow pathways.
   - Those altered pathways **produce** mixing (or prevent separation), leading to desaturation/cyanosis.

3. **Check plausibility of the reverse direction**
   - “Cardiac mixing” by itself is not usually an initiating pathology; it’s a **consequence/feature** arising from underlying anatomic or functional defects.
   - Mixing can worsen symptoms and downstream complications, but it generally does not **cause** the congenital defect/disease entity.

4. **Conclusion**
   - The most likely relationship is: **Disease → CardiacMixing**.
