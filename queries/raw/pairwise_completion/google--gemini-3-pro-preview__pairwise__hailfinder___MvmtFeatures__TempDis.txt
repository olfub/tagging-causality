**Step 1: Define the variables in a meteorological context.**
*   **TempDis (Temperature Distribution):** This refers to the spatial and vertical profile of temperature within and around a storm environment. In hail forecasting, it includes critical factors like the freezing level, cloud top temperatures, and lapse rates (how fast air cools with height).
*   **MvmtFeatures (Movement Features):** This refers to how a storm or convective cell moves. It includes speed, direction, and changes in vector, often influenced by environmental wind shear and the storm's internal dynamics (like supercell propagation).

**Step 2: Analyze Relationship A (MvmtFeatures causes TempDis).**
*   *Does the movement of a storm cause the temperature distribution of the atmosphere?* Generally, no. A storm moves through an existing environment. While a large storm system can modify its local environment slightly (e.g., through rain-cooled outflow), the fundamental temperature structure of the atmosphere (lapse rates, freezing levels) is a broad environmental condition determined by larger synoptic scale weather patterns, not merely by the specific movement vector of a single storm cell.

**Step 3: Analyze Relationship B (TempDis causes MvmtFeatures).**
*   *Does the temperature distribution influence the movement features of a storm?* Yes, indirectly but significantly.
*   Temperature distribution drives pressure gradients and instability (buoyancy).
*   Differences in temperature (thermal gradients) create wind. The vertical wind profile (shear) largely dictates how a storm moves and evolves.
*   Furthermore, storm propagation (deviant motion) is often driven by where the updraft can access the most unstable air. The instability is fully dependent on the vertical temperature distribution (lapse rates). Therefore, the thermodynamic environment (TempDis) dictates the growth and sustenance of the storm, which in turn influences its propagation path (MvmtFeatures).

**Step 4: Evaluate the hierarchy of causes.**
*   In meteorology, the thermodynamic state of the atmosphere (Temperature/Moisture Distribution) is a primary driver. It creates the potential energy for storms.
*   The resulting dynamics (Movement Features) are a response to these environmental conditions. A storm moves *because* the atmospheric setup (winds driven by temperature/pressure diffs) pushes it or because it propagates toward warmer/more unstable air.

**Conclusion:**
It is scientifically more accurate to say that the temperature distribution (which drives instability and wind patterns) influences the movement features of a storm, rather than the storm's movement dictating the atmosphere's temperature profile.
