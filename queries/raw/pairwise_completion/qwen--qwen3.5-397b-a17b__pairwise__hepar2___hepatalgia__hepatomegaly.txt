## Step-by-Step Analysis

### Step 1: Define the Terms

**Hepatalgia**: Pain in the liver (hepat- = liver, -algia = pain)

**Hepatomegaly**: Enlargement of the liver (hepat- = liver, -megaly = enlargement)

### Step 2: Analyze the Causal Direction

**Option A: Hepatalgia → Hepatomegaly**
- This suggests liver pain causes liver enlargement
- Pain is typically a **symptom**, not a cause of structural changes
- Pain sensations themselves do not cause organs to physically enlarge
- This direction is medically implausible

**Option B: Hepatomegaly → Hepatalgia**
- This suggests liver enlargement causes liver pain
- The liver is covered by **Glisson's capsule**, which contains pain-sensitive nerve fibers
- When the liver enlarges, it stretches this capsule
- Capsular stretching activates nociceptors (pain receptors)
- This results in pain perception (hepatalgia)
- This is a **well-established medical mechanism**

### Step 3: Clinical Evidence

In medical practice:
- Conditions causing hepatomegaly (hepatitis, fatty liver, congestion, tumors, etc.) often present with liver pain
- The pain is a **consequence** of the enlargement, not the cause
- Pain does not independently cause organ enlargement

### Step 4: Conclusion

The physiologically and clinically accurate relationship is that liver enlargement (hepatomegaly) causes liver pain (hepatalgia) through capsular stretching, not vice versa.

<Answer>B</Answer>
