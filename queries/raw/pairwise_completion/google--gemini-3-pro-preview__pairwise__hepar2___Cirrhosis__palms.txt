To determine the correct cause-and-effect relationship, let's analyze the medical concepts involved.

**Step 1: Define the terms.**
*   **Cirrhosis:** This is a late stage of scarring (fibrosis) of the liver caused by many forms of liver diseases and conditions, such as hepatitis and chronic alcoholism.
*   **"Palms":** In a medical context, this usually refers to a specific physical sign known as "palmar erythema." Palmar erythema is a condition where the palms of the hands become reddish.

**Step 2: Analyze Relationship A (Cirrhosis causes "palms").**
*   In patients with cirrhosis, the liver's ability to metabolize hormones is compromised.
*   Specifically, the liver struggles to break down estrogen. This leads to increased levels of circulating estrogen (hyperestrogenemia).
*   High estrogen levels affect the vascular system, causing dilation of surface capillaries.
*   This dilation manifests visibly as reddening of the palms (palmar erythema).
*   Therefore, the disease process of cirrhosis leads to changes in the appearance of the palms.

**Step 3: Analyze Relationship B ("palms" causes Cirrhosis).**
*   The palms are an anatomical part of the human body (the hands).
*   Having palms (or having red palms) does not biologically trigger fibrosis or scarring of the liver.
*   While red palms are a *sign* or *symptom* associated with cirrhosis, they are not the mechanism that creates the liver disease.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**
Reviewing the causality, cirrhosis involves physiological changes (hormonal imbalance) that result in a specific physical manifestation on the palms. The existence or condition of the palms does not cause the liver disease.

Therefore, the relationship is that the disease (Cirrhosis) causes the symptom (changes in the palms/palmar erythema).
