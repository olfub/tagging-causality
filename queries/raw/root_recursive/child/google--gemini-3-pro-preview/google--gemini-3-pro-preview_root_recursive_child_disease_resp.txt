To determine which variables are caused by `disease` (infant methemoglobinemia), let's analyze the pathophysiology of the condition and its direct clinical effects based on general medical causal reasoning:

1.  **Nature of the Disease:** Methemoglobinemia is a condition where the hemoglobin in the blood is oxidized (Fe3+ instead of Fe2+), causing it to lose its ability to bind oxygen effectively. Although the oxygen uptake in the lungs might be normal, the tissues do not receive enough oxygen.

2.  **Effect on Oxygen Saturation (Upper and Lower Body):** Because the hemoglobin itself is defective throughout the bloodstream, the measurement of oxygen saturation will be low everywhere. It is not dependent on ductal flow or cardiac mixing in the way a structural heart defect is. Therefore, it causes low oxygen levels in both the upper body (`ruqo2` - right upper quadrant/arm) and the lower body (`lowerbodyo2`).

3.  **Effect on Cyanosis/Hypoxia:** The defining characteristic is cyanosis (blue skin) or hypoxia that does not improve significantly with supplemental oxygen because the problem is the blood's carrying capacity, not the lungs' ability to acquire oxygen. This leads to `hypoxiaino2` (hypoxia when breathing oxygen).

4.  **Effect on Clinical Signs (illness):** The presence of this disease makes the infant clinically `sick`.

5.  **Effect on Gas Distribution:** Since the blood circulating everywhere is affected, the low oxygen levels are spread throughout the body, leading to `hypdistrib` (low oxygen areas equally distributed around the body).

6.  **Exclusions:**
    *   It does not typically cause cardiac structural changes like Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (`lvh`) directly in an acute presentation.
    *   It does not cause pulmonary parenchyma issues (`lungparench`) or specific X-ray findings (`chestxray` / `xrayreport`) like congestion, as it's a blood issue, not a lung tissue issue.
    *   It does not cause `birthasphyxia` or change the `age`.
    *   It usually doesn't cause `co2` retention (ventilation is usually normal).

Based on this step-by-step analysis of the specific pathology of infant methemoglobinemia provided in the context of these variables:

The disease directly lowers measured oxygen levels everywhere (`ruqo2`, `lowerbodyo2`) leading to an equal distribution of hypoxia (`hypdistrib`), causes hypoxia that withstands oxygen therapy (`hypoxiaino2`), and defines the state of the infant as `sick`.
