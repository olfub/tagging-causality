To determine the variables that are unaffected by any other variables (root nodes), we need to analyze the logical flow of a printing process and identifying the fundamental inputs or states that initiate causal chains. In a troubleshooting context, these are usually the initial hardware states, configurations, user actions, or available resources that exist independently of the process outcome.

1.  **Hardware States:** The physical state of the printer components are fundamental conditions.
    *   `<prton>` (Printer On): Whether the printer is turned on is a primary state. No other variable in this list causes the printer to be on; it's a user action/switch state.
    *   `<prtpaper>` (Printer Paper): The existence of paper in the tray is an initial condition.
    *   `<tnrspply>` (Toner Supply): The level of toner is an initial physical resource.
    *   `<prtmem>` (Printer Memory): The amount of installed memory is a physical specification.
    *   `<prtcbl>` (Printer Cable): The integrity of the cable itself is a physical pre-condition.
    *   `<prtport>` (Printer Port): The functionality of the hardware port (LPT1, etc.) is a hardware root cause.

2.  **Configuration and Setup:** Settings and software installation states are often root causes for failures.

    *   Error states like `<problem1>` or `<grbldotpt>` are the final effects (leaf nodes), not roots.
