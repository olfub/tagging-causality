

# Identifying Variables Caused by Sex

Looking at this medical/liver disease network, I need to determine which variables would be **directly caused by** the biological sex of a patient.

## Step-by-step reasoning:

1. **Sex as a root node**: Sex (along with age) is given as an exogenous variable not affected by anything else, so it can only be a parent/cause of other variables.

2. **Medical knowledge about sex-dependent conditions**:

   - **Alcoholism (`alcoholism`)**: Males have significantly higher rates of alcoholism. Sex is a well-established direct risk factor.
   
   - **PBC (`pbc`)**: Primary Biliary Cholangitis is overwhelmingly a female disease (~90% of cases are women). Sex is a direct causal factor.
   
   - **Gallstones (`gallstones`)**: Females are significantly more likely to develop gallstones due to estrogen's effect on bile cholesterol. Sex directly influences this.

3. **Other variables considered but excluded**: Variables like cirrhosis, carcinoma, obesity, etc., while they may show sex-based differences epidemiologically, are more naturally modeled as being caused by intermediate disease processes (e.g., cirrhosis is caused by fibrosis/alcoholism/hepatitis) rather than directly by sex in this causal graph structure.

## Final Answer

<Answer>alcoholism, pbc, gallstones</Answer>
