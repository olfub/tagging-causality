# Identifying Exogenous Variables (Root Causes)

To construct a causal graph, I first need to identify variables that are **exogenous** - meaning they have no incoming causal arrows because they are not caused by any other variables in the system.

## Analysis by Category

### Demographic Variables
- **`<age>`**: A person's age is a fundamental demographic factor. No medical condition or other variable in this list can cause someone to be older or younger. Age is clearly **exogenous**.
- **`<sex>`**: Biological sex is determined at conception/birth. No medical condition in this dataset causes someone's biological sex to change. Sex is clearly **exogenous**.

### Lifestyle/Behavioral Factors
- **`<alcohol>`**: This represents alcohol consumption behavior/exposure. While alcohol consumption can lead to alcoholism (`<alcoholism>`) and liver disease, the consumption itself is a behavioral choice/exposure that is not caused by other medical conditions in this list. This is **exogenous**.

### Medical Interventions/Events
Let me consider whether medical interventions are exogenous:
- **`<hospital>`**: Hospitalization is typically *caused by* medical conditions requiring care - not exogenous
- **`<surgery>`**: Surgery is performed *because of* underlying conditions - not exogenous
- **`<choledocholithotomy>`**: This surgical procedure is performed *because of* gallstones - not exogenous
- **`<injections>`**, **`<transfusion>`**: These are interventions performed for medical reasons - not exogenous

### Diseases, Conditions, and Symptoms
All other variables in the list are either:
- Medical conditions caused by risk factors
- Symptoms caused by underlying diseases
- Laboratory values reflecting disease states
- Treatments prompted by medical necessity

None of these would be exogenous.

## Conclusion

The variables that have no causal parents (are unaffected by any other variables in this system) are:
