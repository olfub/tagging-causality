# Step-by-Step Analysis of Causal Relationships

Let me analyze each variable to determine which ones are root causes (unaffected by other variables in this system):

## 1. Pollution
- **Nature**: Environmental factor
- **Causal role**: Can contribute to lung cancer risk
- **Affected by other variables?**: No - pollution levels are not caused by smoking behavior, cancer status, x-rays, or dyspnoea in this system
- **Conclusion**: Root variable (exogenous)

## 2. Smoker
- **Nature**: Behavioral/lifestyle choice
- **Causal role**: Major risk factor for lung cancer
- **Affected by other variables?**: No - smoking status is not caused by pollution, cancer, x-rays, or dyspnoea in this model
- **Conclusion**: Root variable (exogenous)

## 3. Cancer
- **Nature**: Disease outcome
- **Causal role**: Caused by risk factors, leads to symptoms and tests
- **Affected by other variables?**: Yes - caused by pollution and smoking
- **Conclusion**: Not a root variable

## 4. Xray
- **Nature**: Diagnostic test
- **Causal role**: Used to detect lung problems
- **Affected by other variables?**: Yes - typically ordered when cancer is suspected
- **Conclusion**: Not a root variable

## 5. Dyspnoea
- **Nature**: Symptom (shortness of breath)
- **Causal role**: Result of lung conditions
- **Affected by other variables?**: Yes - caused by cancer and other lung conditions
- **Conclusion**: Not a root variable

## Summary
The variables that are **unaffected by any other variables** in this causal system are the exogenous/root variables: **pollution** and **smoker**. These are the starting points of the causal chain.
