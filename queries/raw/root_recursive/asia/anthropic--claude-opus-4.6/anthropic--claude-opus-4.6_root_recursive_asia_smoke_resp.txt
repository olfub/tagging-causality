

# Identifying Variables Caused by Smoke

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

Looking at the classic "Asia" (chest clinic) Bayesian network structure:

1. **`asia`** and **`smoke`** are root nodes (not affected by any other variable) — confirmed by the problem statement.

2. **`asia → tub`** is explicitly given.

3. Now, for **`smoke`**: In this well-known causal model, smoking is a direct cause of:
   - **`lung`** (lung cancer) — smoking causes lung cancer.
   - **`bronc`** (bronchitis) — smoking causes bronchitis.

4. **`either`** is caused by `tub` and `lung` (not directly by `smoke`).

5. **`xray`** is caused by `either`.

6. **`dysp`** is caused by `either` and `bronc`.

While `smoke` has downstream effects on `either`, `xray`, and `dysp` through intermediate variables, the variables **directly caused** by `smoke` are `lung` and `bronc`.

<Answer>lung, bronc</Answer>
